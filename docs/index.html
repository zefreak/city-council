Vancouver Thu 15 Oct · 12:30pm · written comment closed 5pm Wed 14 Oct

City Center Redevelopment Authority — fees framed as a risk, boots for parking debt, discounted public land

7
Watch — briefing; Council acts on traffic impact fees in Q1 2027

Development fees: the City's own numbers say they are low

Combined impact fees and SDCs on a 50-unit project come to $10,963 per unit, the lowest of seven jurisdictions ECONorthwest compared (Portland $18,776, Camas $26,833). All fees come to 2.9–3.7% of development cost. The deck still leads with “hinder new development” and lists “pause new increases” as an option. The commercial park impact fee in the plan is genuinely good: it moves part of the parks cost off housing. Ask for it to come paired with affordable-housing exemptions, not traded for a pause.

6
Watch — to Council December 2026, or 9 November on consent; the dates conflict

Parking: fines up, and booting and impound after four unpaid tickets

Fines rise for the first time since 2017, for example overtime from $30 to $40 and prohibited parking from $40 to $65. A scofflaw list at 4+ citations unpaid for 45 days leads to a boot, then impound, with at least 77 days of notices and payment plans. The deck never mentions people living in vehicles. For them an impound takes away their shelter. Ask for a hardship waiver, and a referral to outreach before any boot on a vehicle in use as a home, before the code is drafted.

4
Watch — “adoption” of a consultant study; no staff report

Ground-floor study: discounted public land, and a good idea about public ground floors

BAE recommends offering City land “at discounted rates” through cheap ground leases or sales, and drawing on County and Vancouver Housing Authority surplus sites, for “target industry” development. Ask that any discount carry community-benefit terms, and that VHA land stay first in line for housing. The better idea is in the same list: the City buying the ground floors of new buildings and leasing them to local businesses, and subsidising downtown childcare.

The Waterfront Gateway update with Lincoln Property has no attachment (flagged on title alone). Written comment and pre-registration (callie.taylor@cityofvancouver.us, 360-487-7846) closed at 5pm Wednesday 14 October.

Clark County Thu 15 Oct · 6:30pm · Planning Commission public hearing

Planning Commission — the road programme's first public hearing, and a good disaster-rebuild code

2
Act — testify in person or on WebEx, or email jeffrey.delapena@clark.wa.gov until the hearing ends

Transportation Improvement Program, $260.8M: development scored over transit and walking

This meeting is not on the Council listing, so it was found by hand. In the project scoring, multimodal access is worth 6 points and economic development 20, and concurrency points go to “moratorium areas”, so road capacity is scored to unlock development. Only $36.7M of $74.9M in grants is secured. The TIP went to the developers' Development and Engineering Advisory Board on 3 September, before any public hearing.

The strongest testimony on file (Heidi Pozzo, doc 238636) uses the County's own decks. In July the six-year programme was shown balanced at $246.8M. On 7 October Public Works said it would draw down reserves “even with a 1% levy increase”. The 20-year gap is about $500M. Growth at the edges of the urban growth areas has not been paid for, and RCW 36.70A.070(6)(a)(iv)(C) requires the plan to say how it will be. Ask for the project scores to be published: the priority list is an image, and no project's score is shown.

How to testify: include your name, address, city, zip code and phone number, or you are not a party of record. Anything for the record must be handed in or attached. A link does not count. Parks CIP ($89.8M; Parks is “purposefully slowing new park development” because it cannot staff what it has) and stormwater ($23.5M, routine) are heard tonight too.

Good — Council work session 21 Oct, hearing 3 Nov

Columbia Gorge: rebuild after a fire without starting over

After the 2025 Rowena and Burdoin fires, owners whose homes are destroyed get expedited review to rebuild on the same footprint, ten years to start instead of two plus two, and the right to live in an RV on their own land meanwhile. Cultural-resource review still applies near known sites. About 7,800 acres of the county lie in the Scenic Area.

City Council — the MFTE hearing with nothing fixed, a homelessness report with no shelter opening date

04
Act — public hearing and final vote; email or register online by noon, in person by 6:30pm

MFTE: same text as first reading, and the same three errors

Diffed line by line against the 12 October first reading: no changes. So:

1. The HB 1494 protection for tenants whose income rises sits in a recital, not in VMC 3.22. The recital also misstates it: the statute says 150% of the income limit, about 120% of AMI for an 80% unit, not “150% of AMI”. 2. The family-sized option is based on “10% of the restricted units”, about 1% of the building. The statute's “10 percent of the units” most naturally reads as all units (a reading, not settled). 3. The recitals still give the first reading and hearing as 5 and 12 October.

Credit where due: new target areas need an anti-displacement finding, and the 20-year station-area option asks more of developers than the 12-year option beside it. These are fixes to ask for, not grounds to oppose. Comment: council@cityofvancouver.us, or register under “Community Testimony” or on (360) 487-8600. The API's “no comment” flags are wrong.

Ahead Oct 2026 – 2031

Dated, published, and worth putting in a diary now

MPD
Watch — a permanent countywide parks tax is being lined up

The Greater Clark Parks District is being prepared for expansion across the county

From the packet for Vancouver's Parks & Recreation Advisory Commission retreat on 23 September. Its slide reads “Focus is on expansion of existing MPD. Public opinion survey after election with results in early '27”. The May 2026 regional task team report behind it ends with “Possible ballot measure in Fall 2027.” At about 13¢ per $1,000 countywide it would raise ~$15M a year.

The need is real, and the numbers are the county's own. County Parks has 0.69 staff per 10,000 residents against a national median of 3.6 for its size. A third of daily maintenance tasks go undone. Regional parks have run on reserves for seven years, and the report warns of closures. Vancouver's parks department has lost 37.5% of its staff since 2007 while the city grew by 47,000.

But look at which vehicle was picked. The report compared two options. A six-year levy needs a 60% vote and comes back to voters for renewal. A Metropolitan Park District needs a simple majority and is permanent, and its rate is set each year by the governing body — for the existing district, the County Council. The report counts the levy's renewal campaign as a cost. That renewal vote is also the only moment voters get to judge how the money was spent. It is a property tax, and renters pay it through rent.

What to ask for before the petition drive: an independent board, a published rate ceiling, and revenue-sharing terms with the cities written down first. Hold them to the “accountability benchmarking” the report itself lists. Vancouver's parks plan update will also add park impact fees on non-residential development. That is a good idea: part of the cost of growth moves off new housing.
