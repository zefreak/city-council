MFTE: same text as first reading, and the same three errors

Diffed line by line against the 12 October first reading: no changes. So:

1. The HB 1494 protection for tenants whose income rises sits in a recital, not in VMC 3.22. The recital also misstates it: the statute says 150% of the income limit, about 120% of AMI for an 80% unit, not “150% of AMI”. 2. The recitals still give the first reading and hearing as 5 and 12 October.

Credit where due: new target areas need an anti-displacement finding, and the 20-year station-area option asks more of developers than the 12-year option beside it. These are fixes to ask for, not grounds to oppose. Comment: council@cityofvancouver.us, or register under “Community Testimony” or on (360) 487-8600. The API's “no comment” flags are wrong.

North County transfer station, Phase II: $1,606,526.85, and the cut goes to willing sellers

Parametrix will narrow four sites to two, then write a full EIS for both. The EIS is $1.0M and outreach $212K, paid from the regional solid waste fund, which is garbage ratepayers' money. The scope says the choice of two will follow “the level of interest in selling of the property owners”. In August the County's own deck asked the Council to “conduct a public hearing on the siting”. PH
Watch — two public hearings; testimony taken

Solid waste code and a new drinking water code: mostly sound, with two questions

Solid waste repeals the 2017 $1.43-per-ton temporary tip fee. Ask whether it comes off customers' bills, and what now pays for illegal-dumping enforcement. That rests on a Public Works / Public Health agreement still being drafted. Drinking water (CCC 24.22) is the County's first local code for wells and the 900+ small Group B systems serving ~80,000 people. It is sound public health practice. The cost comes later: it lets the Board of Health set fees and require annual operating permits, which rural mobile home parks would pay. The draft still carries a template placeholder and skips from Section 2 to Section 4.

Aviation Advisory Committee: the revised charter fixes two of the worst three edits

III
Act — email meredith.fox@cityofvancouver.us by 5pm Wed; or pre-register on 360-487-8619

VMC 10.05.040, 14 October draft: better, but the airport manager still runs the committee

Diffed against the adopted code. Fixed: the airport manager's veto over the committee's own items is gone. The National Park Service clause is back. Grant review returns, as “discussion” rather than review before application. Still there: the manager sets every agenda; commercial-lease oversight is narrowed to fees and rates; and the “best interests of the airport” clause is added. New: meetings “every other month”, which the manager may cancel as long as two in a row are not. That allows as few as three meetings a year, fewer than today's guaranteed four (derived from the text).

The committee voted on 9 September to see the revision before it goes to Council. This is that review. The master plan deck is image-only and was not read.

MPD
Watch — a permanent countywide parks tax is being lined up

The Greater Clark Parks District is being prepared for expansion across the county

From the packet for Vancouver's Parks & Recreation Advisory Commission retreat on 23 September. Its slide reads “Focus is on expansion of existing MPD. Public opinion survey after election with results in early '27”. The May 2026 regional task team report behind it ends with “Possible ballot measure in Fall 2027.” At about 13¢ per $1,000 countywide it would raise ~$15M a year.

The need is real, and the numbers are the county's own. County Parks has 0.69 staff per 10,000 residents against a national median of 3.6 for its size. A third of daily maintenance tasks go undone. Regional parks have run on reserves for seven years, and the report warns of closures. Vancouver's parks department has lost 37.5% of its staff since 2007 while the city grew by 47,000.

But look at which vehicle was picked. The report compared two options. A six-year levy needs a 60% vote and comes back to voters for renewal. A Metropolitan Park District needs a simple majority and is permanent, and its rate is set each year by the governing body — for the existing district, the County Council. The report counts the levy's renewal campaign as a cost. That renewal vote is also the only moment voters get to judge how the money was spent. It is a property tax, and renters pay it through rent.

What to ask for before the petition drive: an independent board, a published rate ceiling, and revenue-sharing terms with the cities written down first. Hold them to the “accountability benchmarking” the report itself lists. Vancouver's parks plan update will also add park impact fees on non-residential development. That is a good idea: part of the cost of growth moves off new housing.
