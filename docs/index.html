Act — public hearing

Rules of Procedure — ordinance rewriting the public comment rules

The Council is amending Exhibit “A” to CCC 2.09.040 — its own rules on how meetings run and what the public may say. Read from the draft ordinance already cached in this project (markup dated 27 July 2026, unadopted: ordinance number blank, adoption date blank):

The decorum clause (§VII.L.vi) is being rewritten. The struck-out old text barred remarks “defamatory to County staff, uncivil, rude, vulgar, profane.” The replacement bars statements concerning private activities, lifestyles or beliefs of others; topics unrelated to county business; and “professional duties and performance of County employees or employees of its elected officials.” That last category is the ordinary substance of public comment at a county council — it is how a permit backlog, a jail medical contract or a named official's conduct gets raised in public at all.

Point of order widens. New text: “Any Councilor may ask for a point of order,” after which the Chair or Vice Chair may tell a speaker to stop. Today that starts with the Chair alone.

Two changes that are good, and worth saying so. Open public comment moves earlier in the Tuesday order — to right after Amendments to the Agenda, instead of after the hearings and bid awards, so you no longer sit through three hearings to use your three minutes. And a new subsection provides that “members of the public may provide public testimony on removed agenda items during Public Comment,” closing the trick where pulling an item from Council Time makes it un-commentable under the agenda-items-only rule. Additions to Council Time must also now be “time sensitive in nature.”

Firm and not firm. That the cached draft is unadopted and contains these changes: firm. That the ordinance heard on 1 September is this draft: not verified — the Exhibit “A” attached to Tuesday's agenda could not be fetched, and the draft is five weeks old. The underline / strikethrough reading is inferred from PDF text-layer artefacts, not from seeing the marked-up page; whether the “professional duties and performance” clause is new or carried over is not settled. Read the posted ordinance first. It wins.

9
Act — public hearing

Title 20 update: affordability requirements for HB 1491 compliance

The most consequential housing item on any agenda this cycle. Vancouver is deciding how to comply with HB 1491's affordability mandate near transit, and staff have already argued on the record that the law is "partially funded inclusionary zoning." This hearing is where the size of the carve-out gets set.

HB 1491 (2025) requires 50-year affordability within a quarter-mile of Bus Rapid Transit stations — 10% of units at 60% AMI, or 20% at 80% AMI, or 10% at 80% AMI where a tenth are two-bed or larger. Cities must offer offsets: a 20-year MFTE exemption, a 50% impact fee reduction, minimum FAR, density bonuses, no parking minimums.

Vancouver has 51 stations across two existing Vine BRT routes — one of few jurisdictions outside Puget Sound with high-capacity transit, so a law written for Sound Transit light rail lands unusually hard here.

From the 10 August council deck: staff argue 50 years of required affordability against at most 20 years of offset, that the other incentives are already available citywide and so offset nothing at the margin, and that the result "decreases overall production and directs development away from transit." Their proposed approach is exclusions — downtown and other minimum-FAR areas, plus roughly 25% of parcels near stations.

Worth engaging rather than dismissing: the production argument is about volume, while HB 1491 targets tenure and depth. Trading one for the other is a choice, not an arithmetic result.

Clark County Wed 26 Aug

5.2
Follow-up

Mental Health Sales Tax advisory board — resolution and bylaws

The board shapes the County Manager’s recommended budget for the 0.1% treatment sales tax the county has levied since 2007 under RCW 82.14.460. As attached, the bylaws set eight voting seats — three of them courts, the rest county directors and two councilors, and none for anyone who uses the services the tax funds, or for peers, providers or the public. Chair and vice-chair are both councilors (Article V), so the board cannot elect its own leadership, and it meets at least annually (Article IV.1).

The live part is who gets appointed and when the board first meets — neither was settled on 26 August. Clark County’s Tuesday meetings take three minutes of comment on any county business, which is the routine opening to raise membership without waiting for it to appear on an agenda.

Check before quoting. The resolution attached to the agenda was the original staff report dated 27 February 2018 (Resolution 2018-02-18), not a 2026 document. What the 2026 item changed could not be determined from the attachments. The membership above is the bylaws as attached.

Work session — no public comment

North County Recycling and Transfer Station siting study, Phases I & II

A waste facility is being sited in north county, and it is being shaped in the one format that takes no public comment at all (Rules §VIII). Minutes are an audio recording only. By the time this becomes a votable item the candidate sites will be chosen.

The county needs the facility — the alternative to a north-county transfer station is longer hauls, higher tipping costs and more illegal dumping. The fight is never whether, it is where, and siting studies reliably land facilities next to the households least able to object. The screening criteria, their weighting and the surviving candidate sites are all in the two decks (Parametrix and county) that could not be opened. Those are the three things to check. The routes in are the audio recording, the decks, and three minutes at a Tuesday meeting.
