Vancouver Mon 12 Oct · 4:00pm workshops · 6:30pm meeting

City Council — HB 1491's only hearing, an MFTE rewrite on consent, and an arts-grant conflict rule

04
Act — public hearing and final vote; register by noon online, 6:30pm in person

HB 1491: the ordinance is looser than the law it implements

On BRT station-area parcels, new housing must keep one of three affordability options for 50 years. The third option is 10% of units at 80% AMI, provided there are family-sized units. The definition of family-sized varies by document:

DocumentFamily-sized condition
Statute, 3SHB 1491 §3(7)(a)(ii)10% of the units have more than two bedrooms
Title 20 ordinance (this item)10% of the units have two or more bedrooms
MFTE ordinance (consent item 1)10% of the restricted units have more than two bedrooms

Ask for “more than two bedrooms” before final passage. Counting two-bedrooms as family housing makes the cheapest compliance route easier than state law allows. The MFTE version gets the bedrooms right but shrinks the base to about 1% of a building. The two Vancouver ordinances cannot both be right.

Still unfixed from 5 October: the recital dates the hearing “October 12, 2025”, the RCW citation is blank, and there is no Section 3 or severability clause. The attachment is byte-for-byte the same file. And the main ask stands: adopt the impacted/exempt parcel map as an exhibit, so staff cannot change which parcels are exempt without a vote.

Staff's case deserves an answer: they call HB 1491 partially funded inclusionary zoning that will push building away from transit. That is an argument for better incentives in Olympia, not for a code looser than the statute. Written comment to council@cityofvancouver.us by noon Monday; three minutes. The API's “no signup” flags are wrong again.

01
Consent — first reading; hearing Mon 19 Oct

MFTE: a 20-year station-area exemption, approved by staff, and a tenant protection left out of the code

This adds the state-mandated 20-year exemption in BRT station areas. It requires a 50-year covenant and a mandatory 50% cut in transportation impact fees, and it is approved administratively, “without public hearing”. HB 1494 clean-up adds an anti-displacement finding for any new target area, which is genuinely good.

The gap: the staff report says an MFTE tenant whose income rises can stay eligible up to 150% (p. 51), and the recitals list it. The amended code never says it. The statute probably protects them anyway (HB 1494 §2(9)). But staff working from VMC 3.22 will not see it, and the statute measures 150% of the income limit, not of AMI. For an 80% AMI unit that is about 120% AMI. Ask for it to be written in. The recital also gets its own reading dates wrong (it says 5 and 12 October).

No dollar estimate is given for the 20 years of forgone tax or the impact-fee cut. Staff expect “few developments, if any” to use the option. It asks more of a developer, over a longer period, than the 12-year option beside it.

02
Good — consent, first reading; hearing Mon 19 Oct

Inspire Vancouver: no arts-tax grants to commissioners' own organisations

Three serving Culture, Arts & Heritage commissioners applied for year-one grants. The amendment bars any grant where a serving commissioner benefits as a board member, employee or paid contractor, directly or through a fiscal sponsor. It is the right rule. Two gaps are worth a line in writing: it does not cover the eight-member Grant Review Committee that does the scoring, and a commissioner can resign and keep the application in the pool, with no cooling-off period.

WS
Watch — workshops; comment via Community Communication

Climate-smart homes: push for renters, with tenant protections

Staff ask whether the City should inform, navigate or fund home retrofits, and for whom. The City's rehab programme serves ~10 households a year with an 80–100 waitlist and waits of up to two years. Small rentals have “few, if any, requirements”. Back funding for 1–4 unit rentals, with rent and tenancy conditions attached, as the background report's own recommendation 4 says. Otherwise a retrofit becomes a reason to raise the rent.

The biennial budget workshop has no attachment (flagged on title alone). “City Council Vacancies” under the City Manager's report has no attachment and is unexplained; the masthead shows a full council. Watch for an appointment window. Vouchers: $8.12M, including $118,515 to Do Good Multnomah with no memo.

Camas was the biggest of the five, and it was stopped. That is a real win. It is also a partial one. Ridgefield was already 1,379 units over its allocation (Exhibit 4, Table 3), and both of its expansions passed. Commerce said the existing boundaries are “sufficient”, and the County told Commerce that this kind of de-designation “historically has not been found to be defensible”.

Why write now, if the votes are made. Only “a person who has participated orally or in writing … regarding the matter” may petition the Growth Management Hearings Board (RCW 36.70A.280(2)(b)). The petition is due within 60 days of the County's notice of adoption (.290(2)), and the expansions take effect 60 days after adoption (ordinance §V.1.c). If you or your organisation have not commented on the farmland question before, a short written comment by noon on Tuesday that names items 7–9 keeps that door open. That is a reading of the statute, not legal advice.

Submit at clark.wa.gov/councilors/public-comment, or speak at the start of the 1pm meeting. Keep a copy. The staff report's action box is ticked “Consent”, though the agenda lists Separate Business. Watch the agenda-amendment moment. The reason for the rush: the Hearings Board's compliance deadline is 29 October.

HC
Opening — the Council ordered a Housing Commission

A Housing Commission, a Mineral Resources Commission, and a farmland tax break

The ordinance records that the Council directed the County to “form a Housing Commission, form a Mineral Resources Commission, and commence work on a Public Benefit Rating System”. The plan describes the Housing Commission only as “residents and representatives with diverse housing experiences and other stakeholders” (policy 2.5.4). Its make-up is not set, so the ask starts now: named seats for tenants, manufactured-home residents and people with lived experience of homelessness, written into the charter. A PBRS cuts the property tax on enrolled open land and shifts it to everyone else, renters included. It may be worth it for farmland, but argue it as a tax shift.

code
Watch — the housing code adopted with the plan

Mobile home park protection with the escape hatch intact; shelter siting rules

  • Manufactured Home Park overlay (CCC 40.250.120). UGAs only; parks, transitional housing and PSH only. A rezone is allowed on any one of three grounds: no “reasonable use”; park use “not economically viable due to … surrounding development”; or replacement housing at ≤60% AMI with a right of return. The third is good. The first two make it unnecessary. There is still no relocation help, notice period or resident right to buy.
  • Shelters and supportive housing (CCC 40.260.076). Indoor shelters are permitted, but “temporary structures are prohibited”. Whether that bars pallet-shelter villages is unclear, and it is the question to ask. Operators must notify everyone within 500 feet and hold more than one community meeting. Near a school or another shelter, the County may deny after 180 days of negotiation, except for youth, family and DV shelters.
  • Good, as printed: no density cap on affordable housing, transitional housing or PSH in mixed-use zones; 100%-affordable projects are exempt from ground-floor commercial; co-living is legal (state law). Whether the first two are new cannot be told from the flattened redline.
TDR
Watch — adopted; the work is the 2027 roundtable

Councilor Little's TDR policy is in the plan, minus its example price

Policy 1.1.24 commits to a countywide transfer-of-development-rights programme “starting in 2027”, with the County as bank and fee-in-lieu payments allowed as a “mitigation tool”. The draft's “e.g. $25,000 per TDR credit” is gone. There is still no price floor, no acre-for-acre ratio and no no-net-loss goal. Those are the asks when the roundtable convenes.

The arguments made before the hearing still stand, with the County's own figures: countywide capacity is 10,498 over the 20-year allocation, about 64% of it in jurisdictions asking for no expansion. The plan's 0–80% AMI “capacity” is multifamily zoning with no rent attached. And the Vancouver UGA lost about 24,500 units of modelled capacity, most of it by changing assumptions rather than code. Full detail is in the 1 and 5 October briefs in the archive.

Carried unchanged from 6 October. A five-year compact (to 31 January 2031) moves the recording surcharge for households at or below 50% AMI away from “outdated capital and bond-related allocations” towards shelter operations, transitional and permanent housing. Clark County Wed 14 Oct · 9:00am · work sessions, no comment

Three capital plans: roads scored for development, and new parks deliberately slowed

WS
Watch — Planning Commission 15 Oct; Council hearing Tue 3 Nov

Transportation Improvement Program, $260.8M; Parks CIP, $89.8M

  • Roads. 43% of the six-year TIP goes to urban road projects, 18% to sidewalks and ADA. In the ranking formula, multimodal access is worth 6 points and economic development 20. Concurrency points go to moratorium areas, so road capacity is scored to unlock development. Contest the weights on 3 November.
  • Parks. The draft CIP says the County is “purposefully slowing new park development” because operations cannot absorb it, despite level-of-service shortfalls in every category. That is the case being built for a 2027 regional levy vote (see the parks district item below).
  • Park fees for 2027: processing $25 → $50, early opening $20 → $75 an hour, and vendors at events pay 10% of gross. That falls hardest on small community and cultural festivals; Vancouver charges vendors nothing.

Stormwater, now attached: 32 projects, $23.5M over 2027–32, for existing development only. It is funded from the Clean Water Fund, REET 2 and grants, and is adopted by resolution with no hearing. Routine and good. The free parking days calendar and the TIP project schedule are images and could not be read.

Vancouver Thu 15 Oct · 12:30pm · comment by 5pm Wed 14 Oct

City Center Redevelopment Authority — fees framed as a risk, boots for parking debt, discounted public land

7
Watch — briefing; Council acts on traffic impact fees in Q1 2027

Development fees: the City's own numbers say they are low

Combined impact fees and SDCs on a 50-unit project come to $10,963 per unit, the lowest of seven jurisdictions ECONorthwest compared (Portland $18,776, Camas $26,833). All fees come to 2.9–3.7% of development cost. The deck still leads with “hinder new development” and lists “pause new increases” as an option. The commercial park impact fee in the plan is genuinely good: it moves part of the parks cost off housing. Ask for it to come paired with affordable-housing exemptions, not traded for a pause.

6
Watch — to Council December 2026, or 9 November on consent; the dates conflict

Parking: fines up, and booting and impound after four unpaid tickets

Fines rise for the first time since 2017, for example overtime from $30 to $40 and prohibited parking from $40 to $65. A scofflaw list at 4+ citations unpaid for 45 days leads to a boot, then impound, with at least 77 days of notices and payment plans. The deck never mentions people living in vehicles. For them an impound takes away their shelter. Ask for a hardship waiver, and a referral to outreach before any boot on a vehicle in use as a home, before the code is drafted.

4
Watch — “adoption” of a consultant study; no staff report

Ground-floor study: discounted public land, and a good idea about public ground floors

BAE recommends offering City land “at discounted rates” through cheap ground leases or sales, and drawing on County and Vancouver Housing Authority surplus sites, for “target industry” development. Ask that any discount carry community-benefit terms, and that VHA land stay first in line for housing. The better idea is in the same list: the City buying the ground floors of new buildings and leasing them to local businesses, and subsidising downtown childcare.

Ahead Oct 2026 – Fall 2027

Dated, published, and worth putting in a diary now

MPD
Watch — a permanent countywide parks tax is being lined up

The Greater Clark Parks District is being prepared for expansion across the county

From the packet for Vancouver's Parks & Recreation Advisory Commission retreat on 23 September. Its slide reads “Focus is on expansion of existing MPD. Public opinion survey after election with results in early '27”. The May 2026 regional task team report behind it ends with “Possible ballot measure in Fall 2027.” At about 13¢ per $1,000 countywide it would raise ~$15M a year.

The need is real, and the numbers are the county's own. County Parks has 0.69 staff per 10,000 residents against a national median of 3.6 for its size. A third of daily maintenance tasks go undone. Regional parks have run on reserves for seven years, and the report warns of closures. Vancouver's parks department has lost 37.5% of its staff since 2007 while the city grew by 47,000.

But look at which vehicle was picked. The report compared two options. A six-year levy needs a 60% vote and comes back to voters for renewal. A Metropolitan Park District needs a simple majority and is permanent, and its rate is set each year by the governing body — for the existing district, the County Council. The report counts the levy's renewal campaign as a cost. That renewal vote is also the only moment voters get to judge how the money was spent. It is a property tax, and renters pay it through rent.

What to ask for before the petition drive: an independent board, a published rate ceiling, and revenue-sharing terms with the cities written down first. Hold them to the “accountability benchmarking” the report itself lists. Vancouver's parks plan update will also add park impact fees on non-residential development. That is a good idea: part of the cost of growth moves off new housing.
