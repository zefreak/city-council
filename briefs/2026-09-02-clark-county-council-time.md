## Watch

### The comp plan hearing dates were set in an added agenda item, and are not published anywhere

From the 26 August 2026 Council Time minutes (`ct-minutes-8-26-26.txt`), approved as item 4.1
today: County Manager Kathleen Otto asked to **add** "Comp Plan Public Hearing Dates" to the agenda
as item 4.3 at the start of the meeting. Council supported adding it, and then:

> "Council supported starting the Public Hearing on **Thursday, October 1st at 4pm**, continuing to
> **Friday, October 2nd at 10am**, with a continuation to **Monday, October 5th at 10am** if
> necessary."

The county's published notice (news release, 5 August) still says the Council hearing is **6
October at 10am** with adoption **13 October at 1pm**, and the Planning Commission decision table
updated 27 August — the day *after* this decision — still heads its column "Council Decision
10/13/2026." The 20-year comprehensive plan for Clark County will be heard on dates that were
agreed by consensus at an informal Wednesday meeting, added to that meeting's agenda on the day,
and recorded only in minutes. **Anyone working from the county website will have the wrong date.**

The three slots are a Thursday afternoon, a Friday morning and a Monday morning — no evening
session. See `briefs/2026-09-03-clark-county-planning-commission.md` for the hearing itself.

### Parks and Nature 2027–2032 Capital Improvement Plan — work session requested for 14 October

Item 7.1, a nine-step planner signed by David Stipe, Parks Planning & Development Manager, on
25 August (`pw-parks-cip-work-session-request.txt`). Requested date **14 October 2026**, 30 minutes,
staff David Stipe, Ross Hoover, Jonathan Beckhart.

Work sessions take **no public comment**. This one prepares "a hearing to consider a resolution to
adopt the CIP and amend the Parks, Recreation, and Open Space Plan, and the Natural Areas
Acquisition Plan" — i.e. the shape is set before anything is votable. The form states the direction
of travel plainly: the plan "continues a more proactive approach to major maintenance within the
park system along with continuing to **shift from new park development to developing new facilities
in existing parks**." That is a real policy choice — it favours maintaining what exists over
extending parks into underserved areas — and it is declared under "Impacts," with "**No identified
policy impacts**" written under Policy Implications. Those two statements do not sit together.

The form says the annual CIP process "has included a public comment period, presentations to the
Parks Advisory Board (PAB), and the County Planning Commission." **When that comment period opens
was not stated and is not on any calendar I found.** That is the thing to ask Parks and Nature for
by name, ahead of 14 October.

## Money and land

**Letter of Concurrence — NW 31st Ave safety improvements (item 5.1).** Clark County concurs in the
Cowlitz Indian Tribe's application for roughly **$750,000 in WSDOT Highway Safety Improvement
Program funding**, for about 0.30 miles of NW 31st Ave spanning tribal and unincorporated county
right-of-way. Scope is FHWA-approved countermeasures: shoulder widening, rumble strips,
high-visibility thermoplastic, reflective raised pavement markers, signage, illumination and
pavement repair. WSDOT's award is contingent on county concurrence, and the county's concurrence is
conditioned on an interlocal agreement being executed before any work in county right-of-way.
Signed by County Engineer Jeremy Provenzola.

**This is a good item and worth saying so.** It is $750,000 of state safety money the county does
not have to match, spent on physical countermeasures rather than enforcement, on a road that spans
a jurisdictional seam where nothing usually happens. The tribe did the work of applying. The only
thing to keep an eye on is the interlocal agreement, which is not attached and which is where any
county cost would appear.

Nothing else on the agenda moves money. Policy Updates: none.
