**The empty fee schedules.** In all four Safe Stay agreements the document ends: `EXHIBIT "B":` /
`Fee Schedule` / `EXHIBIT "C": City Property`. There is nothing between the heading and the next
exhibit. Attachment B to the Bridge Shelter amendment is the same — "Attachment B / Fee Schedule",
page 15 of 16, blank. §3 of each agreement says *"The City will pay Contractor pursuant to Exhibit
'B'"*, so the payment mechanism is a document the public packet does not contain. This is the
specific failure `BRIEF-PROMPT.md` §3c is about: the dollar ceiling is disclosed and the *rate* is
not, so cost per bed, per site, per year cannot be computed from the record. **Request the executed
Exhibit B for all five.**

**Two terms in the Safe Stay agreements worth knowing.** §3 allows an annual compensation adjustment
indexed to the **Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue CPI**, at the City's sole discretion — so the ceiling is a
ceiling on the base, not on the eventual spend. §4 permits renewal for five further years to a
ten-year total. The City supplies sites, utilities, garbage, portable restrooms, handwashing
stations and fencing; the operator supplies staffing and services. Exhibit C lists what the City
owns on site: tents, sheds, **security cameras**, spare pallet supplies, kitchen equipment.

**One drafting error to note before quoting the record.** C-102350 is titled "Safe Stay 1 Service
Provider" on its own front page — the same label as C-102349 — although the agenda item is the
415 West community. The contractor is spelled "OutsdiersInn.Org" in both. Neither is substantive;
both make the executed record harder to search.

**The Bridge Shelter scope of work has an unresolved editorial comment in it.** §1.2 lists required
staff training, and item (j) reads, verbatim: *"Should we also include 'other trainings coordinated
by City'? (Since we pay CRMH to provide trainings for our whole system)"*. That is a question from
one drafter to another, left in the scope of work of a $3.7M contract that went to Council on
consent. Harmless in itself; a sign the document was not given a final read.

**Two Bridge Shelter provisions that do matter:**

- **§3.2 — Clark County residents.** *"The City and County have agreed that the City will make
  available space to participants living unsheltered in Clark County for the first four years of this
  SOW. Contractor may not exclude participants based on their Clark County living status during this
  period."* Four years, then the commitment lapses. A City-owned, City-funded 120-bed shelter that
  serves the county for four years and may then close its doors to people who live outside the city
  limits. Nothing in the document says what happens in year five.
- **§3.1 — eligibility.** The contractor may not write criteria that "automatically disqualif[y] a
  participant for substance use, criminal history, system involvement, or high service needs" unless
  required by law or necessary to mitigate imminent safety risk. **This is a genuinely good clause**
  and it is the one to hold the City to: it is the difference between a low-barrier shelter and a
  shelter that screens out the people who need it. §2.3 likewise prohibits bed set-asides or referral
  exclusivity without City approval — so jail re-entry, hospitals and HART cannot quietly reserve
  capacity.
- **§4.5 — involuntary exits.** The contractor may remove someone immediately where behaviour "poses
  a threat to others' safety **as determined by Contractor**", reporting to the City within one
  business day. There is no grievance route named in the amendment for the person exited, and no
  requirement that the City review.
- **Severe weather is excluded** from the $3,588,391.51 and will come back as a further amendment.
  So the winter cost of this shelter is not yet in any number the Council has approved. §5.2.1 lets
  the operator activate expanded overflow capacity on Clark County Severe Weather Taskforce criteria.

**The E-Verify clause.** Every one of these contracts — both public works bids and all four Safe Stay
agreements — requires the contractor to "register and enter into a Memorandum of Understanding with
the E-Verify Program", ensure all its employees and subcontractors "are eligible to work in the
United States", and provide verification on City request. **Failure is a material breach.** It is a
standing City contract term rather than anything new on this agenda, and it is not flagged in any
staff report. A city that funds low-barrier shelter for people regardless of criminal history is
requiring its shelter operators to run federal work-authorisation checks on their own staff. Worth
knowing it is there; worth asking when the Council adopted it and whether it is required by any
funder.

**The Army Corps lease rate.** $12,000/yr for 15,930 sq ft is **$0.75 per square foot per year** on
land at SE Columbia Way. It is a succeeding lease (it replaces DACW57-5-21-0028) for a federal
government use, one-year term renewable annually to 30 September 2031, terminable by the tenant on
30 days' notice. No appraisal or rent-comparison document is in the packet. Not obviously wrong for
a federal agreement; not obviously right either, and nothing in the record supports the number.

**Covington House is a good item.** The 1846 building — the oldest extant house in Washington, on
the National Register since 1972 — moves from the dissolving Covington House Heritage Society
(formerly the Vancouver Women's Club, licensee since 1927) to the Clark County Historical Society for
$1/year, with the City continuing to maintain the building and grounds and the Society expressly
required to make it available for public use "including for low-income persons". The land underneath
belongs to Vancouver School District; the City's own right to occupy runs only to **30 November
2027**. That is the date to watch.

## Public hearings

### 1. Camping banned within 1,500 feet of the Bridge Shelter — adopted text now available

First reading 14 September, public hearing 21 September. The ordinance itself
(`camping-impact-area-ordinance.pdf`) adds a new VMC 8.22.040(B)(1): *"Within 1,500 feet of the
nearest point of the City of Vancouver Bridge Shelter Property"* — 5313 NE 94th Avenue. It also adds
definitions of "City of Vancouver Bridge Shelter" and "City of Vancouver Bridge Shelter Property" to
VMC 8.22.030, and renumbers the existing impact areas 1–4 to 2–5.

**New facts not in the 14 September brief:**

- **Effective date: 1 November 2026.** Not on passage.
- The recital states the Council considered **1,000, 1,500 and 2,000 feet** and found 1,500 "reasonably
  addresses the identified conditions without unnecessarily restricting additional areas otherwise
  available for lawful nighttime camping."
- The recital confirms the legal gap the ordinance closes: the existing 1,000-foot buffers attach to
  "safe stay communities", and the Bridge Shelter "does not fall within the definition of a Safe Stay
  Community and is therefore not subject to that existing buffer."
- The buffer is **in force at all hours**, unlike the 6:30am–9:30pm daytime prohibitions in
  subsections (C) and (D).
- Ordinance M-3323 (1997) established the chapter; M-4379 (2022) last amended it.

Everything in the 14 September brief stands: 1,500 feet is 162 acres, a quarter of a square mile;
the staff report concedes the ordinance "will cause people currently camping within the proposed
buffer who decline shelter placement to relocate"; and it still does not say how many people that
is. The shelter has **120 beds**.

### 2. Swifts Terrace infill subdivision — appeal

PRJ-169657 / LUP-85734 / APL-86163. A **2.3-acre site at 2410 NE 54th Street** divided into **18
residential lots**, R-9 zoning, R-9 on three sides and a park to the west. Hearing Examiner approved
15 July; appeal filed 24 July. **Staff recommended denying the appeal and upholding the approval.**

The appellant's four grounds, per staff's own summary: neighbourhood compatibility; vesting under
the previous comprehensive plan and zoning code; failure to comply with VMC 20.740.130 critical areas
protection; and failure to comply with RCW 58.17.110(1) of the Subdivision Act.

**The left read is not the obvious one.** Three of the four grounds are process; the first is
"neighbourhood compatibility by approving infill development", which is an objection to infill as
such. 18 homes on 2.3 acres under VMC 20.920 (Infill Development) and 20.927 (Narrow Lot
Developments) is roughly 7.8 units/acre — modest, but it is more housing on land already served, and
blocking it displaces the same households further out. **Support the denial of the appeal.** The
critical-areas ground is the only one that deserves independent weight, and whether it has merit
cannot be settled from a nine-slide staff presentation.

At the same time, do not overclaim: these are 18 market-rate lots. Nothing in the file commits a
single one to any income band, and `context/HOUSING-vancouver-wa.md` §3 is the standing caution —
zoned capacity has not been Vancouver's binding constraint. This item adds homes. It does not
address affordability and should not be described as if it did.

### 4. Fire District 5 supplemental budget ordinance

**$258,408** added to the Fire Fund for FY2026, creating **7.0 limited-duration positions** on
three-year terms, with **Fire District 5 supporting 100% of actual costs** from Q4 2026. An emergency
is declared so it takes effect immediately on passage; acts taken before the effective date are
ratified.

The seven: 1.0 Administrative Assistant 3, 1.0 Data Analyst 1, 1.0 Fire Emergency Services Manager,
1.0 Fire Professional Standards Program Manager, 1.0 Material Control Coordinator, 2.0 Support
Specialist 2. Three new classifications are added to the non-union exempt salary schedule.
**None of the seven is a firefighter** — this is the administrative apparatus of a service contract
with a neighbouring fire district, not response capacity. Fully cost-recovered, so no General Fund
exposure unless the district stops paying; §5 of the presentation notes funds not needed "will not be
expended and will not be charged to Fire District 5."

Context: the 2027-28 budget preview names **"Regional Fire Authority assessment"** as future work.
Seven limited-duration positions inside Vancouver Fire, paid by a fire district, on three-year terms,
is what the groundwork for an RFA looks like.

## Watch — the 2027-28 budget, previewed in workshop

Fourth workshop of the year (27 April, 13 July, 24 August, 21 September). **The dates are the
actionable part:**

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| **1 October 2026** | City Manager's Recommended Budget published online |
| **12 October** | Operating Budget workshop |
| **26 October** | Capital Budget workshop |
| **2 November** | **Council Consent of Budget** |
| **16 November** | Public hearing and adoption |

"Council Consent of Budget" on 2 November means a two-year budget appears on a **consent agenda** —
the block that passes without debate unless a councilmember pulls it — two weeks before the only
public hearing. Whatever the procedural intent, that is where to look.

**The forecast.** The revised 2027/28 forecast slide is labelled **"$61.9M Deficit"** with **$13.1M**
of one-time initiatives for 2028 shown separately. **Treat the $61.9M with care:** the slide's axis
does not survive `pdftotext`, and the accompanying memo says the revision "foundationally changed the
future years to reduce the deficit impact… the deficit forecast is reduced in **2029-2032**." That
strongly suggests $61.9M is a multi-year cumulative figure over a forecast horizon to 2032, not a
biennium gap — but the document does not say so in words, and I could not read the chart. **Do not
quote $61.9M as the 2027-28 deficit without checking the published budget on 1 October.**

**The $13.1M of one-time initiatives**, itemised identically in the deck and the memo: Fire vehicle
replacements $3.1M · Firstenburg rooftop solar $3.0M · Chkalov Lot 2 fleet parking $2.9M · Arts Hub
Phase 1 design and construction $2.3M · Police vehicle replacements $0.7M · Annexation planning
professional services $0.4M · Comprehensive planning (professional services and a limited-duration
FTE) $0.3M · Fire equipment and training $0.2M · Police equipment $0.2M.

**Two revenue items placed "For Council Discussion":**

1. **HB 2442 — "Local sales tax of .01% to fund local children and family services."** Programmed for
   2028. Staff's recommendation is to **offset current Children's Justice Center costs (rent and four
   detectives) in 2028**, with "capacity for other programming — subject to Council direction."

   **The rate is correct as written: 0.01%, not 0.1%.** Verified against the enacted bill —
   ESHB 2442, ch. 221, Laws of 2026, §202: *"The rate of the tax imposed by the city or county may not
   exceed 0.01 percent of the selling price"* (cached at `data/wa-legislature/hb2442.pdf`). One
   hundredth of one percent — ten cents on a $1,000 purchase. This repo's standing gotcha is that the
   City has previously described a 0.1% tax as 0.01%; here it has not. The rate really is that small.

   **The left-critical read is the use, not the rate.** §202(2) restricts the money to *"providing
   **additional** services that assist children and their families, including: Child care; perinatal
   support services; before-school and after-school based youth services that address mental, social,
   behavioral, and physical health; workforce capacity building; shelter and rental assistance; and
   client transportation."* Staff propose spending the first tranche on rent and **four detectives** at
   an existing facility. Whether backfilling existing law-enforcement posts is an *additional* service
   assisting children and families is a question the statute's own wording invites, and it should be
   asked before the 2028 programming is set.

2. **Utility tax increase from 28.90% to 28.99%** — the deck calls it ".09%", meaning 0.09 percentage
   points. A utility tax is regressive: it falls on water, sewer, refuse and energy bills as a flat
   proportion regardless of income. Small, and worth naming for what it is.

**Also in the recommended budget**, per the highlight slides: increased public defense funding; fire
stations 3, 6 and 8; Interstate Bridge Replacement support; annexation preparation in partnership
with Clark County; Bridge Shelter close-out and maintenance; Heights development; affordable housing;
a **rental registration program**; a Performing Arts Center study; a five-year disparity procurement
study; an ADA transition plan; EV charging pilots; police headquarters **land acquisition**; and new
officers funded from Proposition 5, a grant, and the public safety sales tax (a separate 0.1% tax
under RCW 82.14.345 / HB 2015 — **not** the same as the HB 2442 tax above, and not the same as the
TBD transportation tax). Under Boards and Commissions the deck marks a **Youth Coordinator as "Not
Recommended"**, deprioritised in favour of a Boards and Commission Coordinator.
