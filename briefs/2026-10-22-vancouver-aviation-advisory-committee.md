## Bottom line

**The City revised the committee's charter after the committee pushed back, and the revision fixes
two of the three worst edits flagged on 9 September.** The airport manager's veto over the committee's
own business is gone. The National Park Service clause and grant review are back, though grant review
comes back weaker. **What survives: the airport manager sets every agenda, can call extra meetings,
and can now cancel meetings.** The "best interests of the airport" loyalty clause also stays. Written
comment is due by **5pm Wednesday 21 October**.

## The rest of the draft, unchanged from 28 August

- The mandatory list of ten subjects becomes *"topics identified and requested by the airport manager
  or city council. Examples may include…"*.
- §A.3, *"General financial oversight of the airfield, including all proposed fees, **commercial
  leases**… and rental agreement formats"*, narrows to *"Adjustments to airport use fees, lease rates,
  and rental rates"*. That narrowing matters because the City's own 2018 audit follow-up is still
  working on *"oversight of subleasing on land leases"* (9 Sep minutes, item 3).
- Nine members become seven. The tenant cap goes from 3 to 2. Consecutive full terms go from 3 to 2.
- §B adds *"will effectively and respectfully work to advance the best interests of the airport and the
  City of Vancouver"*. This is not in the adopted text, so it is **an insertion**.
- §G: bylaws need city manager **approval**, and the path to Council ratification is gone.

## The left read

**The meeting-frequency change is weaker than it looks.** "Every other month" plus a power to cancel
any meeting, as long as two in a row are not cancelled, lets the airport manager cancel every
alternate meeting. That leaves **as few as three meetings a year, fewer than the four the current
quarterly rule guarantees.** (Derived from the text; it assumes the manager uses the power fully.)
Read with the manager setting every agenda, the committee meets when and on what the department
chooses.

**Credit where it is due.** The City listened on the veto, the Park Service and grants. Officers can
now be removed by their colleagues, and the minimum meeting schedule nominally rises. The tenant cap
still tightens a real conflict of interest. Someone who wants the master plan to move quickly can
support most of this draft. The **agenda power, the cancellation power and the loyalty clause** are
the parts that turn an advisory committee into a department's stakeholder group.
