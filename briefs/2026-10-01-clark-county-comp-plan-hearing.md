## Bottom line

**Clark County's own planning staff say, in a memo written for this hearing, that Camas, La Center
and Ridgefield already have more zoned housing capacity than their 20-year allocation and are
*still* asking to expand their urban growth boundaries onto de-designated farmland — and that "there
is a need for a discussion that all jurisdictions have undertaken some reasonable measures before
concluding that expansion is necessary"** (Exhibit 4, p. 3). Six days earlier the County told the
Department of Commerce, in writing, that de-designating agricultural land on proximity-to-growth
grounds "historically has not been found to be defensible" by the Growth Management Hearings Board.
Those two documents are the strongest case against the expansions in the record, and both were
written by the County.

**Written comment is still open.** The County is compiling a document titled *"Public Comments
9/16/2026 to 10/1/2026"* — comments are being taken right up to the hearing.

## Watch

### 1. The farmland offset failed 2–5 — and the County has now promised Commerce a weaker version

The Planning Commission voted on 3 September on an amendment replacing comprehensive plan policy
**1.1.24** with a countywide Transfer of Development Rights (TDR) programme. The full text is in
Exhibit 1 (Decision Table, item 33, pp. 5–6). Its teeth were three clauses:

- **UGA expansions that include de-designated agricultural lands "shall be subject to an urban
  holding designation until applicable TDR requirements are satisfied"** — i.e. you cannot build on
  the converted farmland until you have paid for its replacement;
- permanent conservation of **at minimum one acre of agricultural land for each acre converted**,
  with **no net loss** of agricultural resource land;
- the programme **"fully operational no later than December 31, 2027"**, with conservation easements
  or an equivalent mechanism as the fallback if TDR credits are unavailable.

It also contained its own escape hatch, which is worth reading plainly: *"Failure, delay, or
insufficient participation in the TDR program shall not result in removal, reduction or reversion of
an adopted UGA expansion."* Even the strong version would not have taken the expansions back.

**AYE – 2; NAY – 5. Motion failed.** The de-designations that the offset would have covered had
already passed, in the same sitting.

**Then, on 17 September, Community Planning Director Oliver Orjiako wrote to the Department of
Commerce:** *"We intend to work towards a TDR policy that is separate from urban growth expansion
efforts so that there is an appropriate buffer in preserving resource lands while accommodating
urban growth."* (County response to Commerce, p. 1.)

Read the two side by side. The failed amendment tied TDR *to* the expansion via the urban holding
designation — that linkage was the entire enforcement mechanism. The letter promises Commerce a TDR
policy explicitly **separate from** the expansions. That is the version with the holding designation
removed. If the Council adopts a "TDR policy" on 1 October, the question to ask is whether anything
stops a converted acre being built on before a replacement acre is conserved.

**This is being shaped on Wednesday 23 September at Council Time** — see the separate brief for that
meeting. There are no published materials for that discussion.

### 2. What the County has already conceded to the state

The 17 September letter to Commerce is unusually candid and should be read in full (9 short
paragraphs, `data/clark-county/2026-10-01-comp-plan/county-response-to-commerce.txt`). The
admissions:

- *"we acknowledge further de designations of agricultural lands based solely or primarily on
  proximity to urban growth that otherwise meet the definition of agricultural lands historically
  has not been found to be defensible by the Growth Management Hearings Board."*
- The County is *"conducting targeted audits of the draft plans and documentation supporting
  expansion"* — i.e. the evidentiary record for the expansions was not complete when the Planning
  Commission voted on them.
- It *"will review policy 1.1.24 to ensure consistency with state statute"* — the policy whose
  strengthening failed 2–5.
- It will *"explicitly evaluate the cumulative loss of agricultural and forest economies in Clark
  County resulting from the proposed UGA expansions rather than evaluating each parcel in
  isolation."* That evaluation is **not in the hearing record**.

**Five of the nine map items de-designate agricultural resource land, and every one passed on a
narrow Planning Commission vote** (Exhibit 1, pp. 1–2):

| # | What it does | PC vote, 3 Sep |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Camas UGA: de-designate ag land, expand east for housing | 4–3 |
| 6 | La Center UGA: de-designate ag land, expand north for employment | 5–2 |
| 7 | La Center UGA: de-designate ag land, expand southwest for employment | 4–3 |
| 8 | Ridgefield UGA: de-designate ag land, expand north for employment and housing | 4–3 |
| 9 | Ridgefield UGA: de-designate ag land, expand southwest for housing (the Maul property) | 5–2 |

A one-vote swing on the seven-member Commission reverses items 5, 7 and 8. The Council is not bound
by any of them.

### 3. The Vancouver UGA lost about 24,500 units of planned capacity — and where the cut landed is a choice

Exhibit 4 sets out three amendments the County made to the unincorporated Vancouver UGA to get it
back inside its control total (p. 2):

1. **Reverse the proposed upzoning in the 179th St/Fairgrounds area back to current zoning**, except
   existing Urban Medium going to R-24/R-36 and one MX property to R-50. ≈30% of the reduction.
2. **Reduce the assumed housing density in the HWY 99 sub-area** from **75 to 40 dwellings/acre**.
   ≈8% of the reduction. (The HWY 99 Overlay has *no* maximum density in code.)
3. **Reduce the assumed density in the R1-5, R1-6 and R1-7.5 zones** from a flat 14 du/ac to a tiered
   **12 / 10 / 8** du/ac. ≈62% of the reduction. These zones currently *allow* up to 21.8 du/ac.

Only the first is a change to the zoning map. The other two are changes to **modelling assumptions**
in the Vacant Buildable Lands Model — the code is untouched, the number on paper moves. That
distinction matters: the plan's housing capacity is being managed downward by changing what staff
assume will get built, not by changing what may be built.

**The arithmetic, derived from the memo's own figures** (the memo does not present it this way):

| | Units |
|---|---|
| Vancouver UGA capacity before amendments (Exhibit 4, p. 2) | 104,819 |
| Vancouver UGA allocation | 76,664 |
| City of Vancouver, after its own VBLM refinement (footnote 1: from 61,616) | 47,518 |
| Unincorporated Vancouver UGA, after County amendments | 32,830 |
| **Vancouver UGA, after both** | **80,348** |
| Total reduction | **24,471** (≈23%) |
| — of which the City's own refinement | 14,098 |
| — of which the County's three amendments | **10,373** (≈24% of the unincorporated UGA) |

That reconciles with the memo's own "approximately 24%" and "more than 10,000 housing units", and
confirms the 24% is of the *unincorporated* UGA, not the whole.

**Two errors in Exhibit 4 worth knowing before you quote it.**
- p. 2 calls 104,819 *"the combined countywide housing capacity"*, then says it is *"28,155 over the
  combined allocation for the Vancouver UGA of 76,664."* 76,664 + 28,155 = 104,819 exactly. The
  arithmetic makes 104,819 a **Vancouver UGA** figure; the sentence labels it countywide. The label
  is wrong.
- The Ridgefield note on p. 4 says *"Even with adjustments have 1,329 excess housing units"* while
  Table 3 immediately above it gives **1,379**. A 50-unit discrepancy in the same document.

### 4. The affordability claim rests on zoning, not on rent

Exhibit 4, Table 5 (p. 6) is the plan's demonstration that the unincorporated Vancouver UGA has
capacity at every income band. It claims **19,419 units of capacity in the 0–80% AMI band against a
need of 19,370 — a surplus of 49 units.**

But look at what generates that capacity. The 0–80% row is the sum of the **zoning districts**
R-24, R-36, OR-24, OR-36, R-50, OR-50, MX, the Hwy 99 Overlay areas, the commercial districts, and
an assumed 11% of R1-5/R1-6/R1-7.5 capacity. Nothing in any of those zones requires a rent. **The
model treats "multifamily zoning" as a proxy for "affordable to households under 80% AMI."** It is
not one, and the plan contains no mechanism that makes it one.

The countywide allocation (Exhibit 4, Table 2) asks for **12,852 non-PSH units plus 7,491 permanent
supportive housing units below 30% AMI** — 20,343 units, about 20% of the 103,695 total, plus 3,986
emergency beds. Table 5 does not break that band out at all; 0–30% is folded into a single 0–80%
bucket. A zoned-capacity model cannot distinguish a unit affordable at 25% AMI from one affordable
at 75% AMI, and the deepest band is precisely the one zoning cannot deliver — it needs subsidy.
There is no funding plan in the record for the 20,343.

This is the same finding as `context/HOUSING-vancouver-wa.md` §3 (capacity is not the binding
constraint) and §7 (the subsidy gap), reached here from the County's own table. Note the direction
of the argument: it is a reason to doubt the *affordability* claim, **not** a reason to oppose the
zoning. The zoning is necessary and insufficient.

### 5. The residential code changes are mostly state law and mostly good

The 15 September Residential Code Updates memo (JET Planning) is short and worth reading. Almost
everything in it is marked "State compliance" — the County has, in its consultant's words, "little to
no discretion to modify locally":

- Off-street parking **minimums exempted** for passive-house, modular and mass-timber residential
  (RCW 36.70A.817); tandem parking allowed; gravel parking allowed for up to six spaces
  (RCW 36.70A.622).
- Passive-house retrofits may exceed height limits and project into setbacks by **eight inches** for
  insulation (RCW 36.70A.810/.812).
- Façade-modulation and Highway 99 upper-storey stepback exceptions for affordable housing, passive
  house, conversions, modular and mass timber (RCW 36.70A.815).
- **Park trailers ("park model RVs") and tagged tiny houses on wheels permitted within manufactured
  home parks** — recorded as "Implement Planning Commission direction", not state compliance. This
  is a local choice and a good one for MHP residents; it legalises a housing type already present.

The manufactured home park overlay passed the Planning Commission **7–0**.

## Money and land

No dollar appropriation is before the Council at this hearing. What moves is land and the
development rights attached to it: five agricultural de-designations feeding four UGA expansions in
three cities, with no adopted replacement obligation. The impact fee schedules (CCC 40.620.010,
40.630.010–.060), the fire and school impact fee updates, and the Capital Facilities Financial Plan
are all part of the same adoption package and were heard separately on 15 September.

One agency-comment change is a money item hiding in a memo: Public Works asked on 9 September for an
amendment to the **traffic impact fee formula in CCC 40.620.010** "due to the removal of the Traffic
Impact Fee Technical Document, to ensure the pass-by factor is applied accurately and consistently."
The technical document that justified the formula has been removed from the plan; the formula is
being patched in response. What the net effect on fee levels is, the memo does not say.
