# Vancouver Parks & Recreation Advisory Commission — special meeting and retreat, Wednesday 23 September 2026

**Briefed after the meeting.** When the 22 September run checked, this meeting had no agenda. Its
agenda and a 40-page packet were published afterwards, and the 23 September gather at 19:05 picked
them up. The meeting ran 4:00–6:00pm the same day. It is recorded here because the packet sets out
work toward a **permanent countywide parks property tax, with a possible ballot measure in fall
2027**, and that is not a one-meeting story.

- the PRCS retreat deck, pp.21–40.

Page numbers are the packet's own "Page N of 40".

## Bottom line

Nothing to act on this week. But the **Greater Clark Parks District** — a permanent parks property
tax that now covers only unincorporated urban Vancouver — **is being lined up for expansion across
the whole county**. The timeline is a public opinion survey after the November 2026 election and a
possible fall 2027 ballot. How it is structured will decide who pays and who controls the rate
thereafter.

## Watch

### A countywide parks tax is being prepared, and the chosen vehicle is the permanent one

**What the task team found.** The team was 2025–26, with councillors from the county, Vancouver
(Diana Perez), Camas, Battle Ground, Washougal and Ridgefield, plus staff including La Center.

The **need is real, and the numbers are the county's own.**

- **Staffing.** Clark County Parks runs **37 operations and maintenance staff for 7,166 acres**.
  That is **0.69 FTE per 10,000 residents**, against an NRPA national median of **3.6** for
  agencies serving 500,000+ and **8.6** across all agencies (Fig. 2, p.12). Those are 81% and 92%
  below; checked: 0.69/3.6 = 19%, 0.69/8.6 = 8%.
- **Unfinished work.** Task tracking shows **33% of daily assigned tasks cannot be completed**
  (p.12).
- **Reserves.** Regional parks have drawn on reserve funds for **seven consecutive years** (p.12).
- **Who covers operations.** Park-generated revenue covers about **12%** of operating expenses and
  the general fund about **23%** (p.11).
- **Vancouver's own parks department** lost **35.5 FTE (37.5%) between 2007 and 2025**, while
  population grew by over **47,000** (19 August minutes, p.4).

Every agency in the county is below its national median for parks operating spend per capita
(Fig. 5, p.15).

**What is being proposed.** The report weighs two vehicles (pp.16–18):

| | Expand the Metropolitan Park District (RCW 35.61) | Regional parks levy (RCW 36.68.525) |
|---|---|---|
| Term | **Permanent** — "does not require periodic renewal votes" | Six years, then renewal |
| Voter threshold | Petition signed by **20% of registered voters** in each area joining, then **simple majority** | **60%** supermajority |
| Rate | Set **annually by the governing body** (for the GCPD, the County Council) | Up to 60¢ per $1,000; Council may raise up to 1%/yr without a vote |
| Estimate | ~13¢ per $1,000 countywide ≈ **$15M/yr** | not estimated |

**The direction of travel is settled.** The retreat deck's slide *"Clark County Sustainable Funding
Update"* (p.28) says: **"Focus is on expansion of existing MPD"**. A *"Public opinion survey after
election with results in early '27"*. The report's last line (p.19): **"Possible ballot measure in
Fall 2027."**

**The left-critical read, stated fairly.**

- **Parks are underfunded, and the alternative is closures.** The report says in terms that without
  new money the county *"will eventually have to make difficult decisions to close parks"* (p.10).
  Opposing the revenue without offering one is not a position.
- **But the vehicle was chosen to minimise democratic friction.** Of the two options, the MPD is
  the one that avoids the 60% threshold, has no sunset, and lets the governing body set the rate
  every year. The levy's six-year renewal is described as a drawback because it "would likely
  require some level of investment in public education every six years" (p.18). That renewal vote
  is also the only point at which voters get to judge how the money was spent.
- **It is a property tax, and Washington's tax mix is already the most regressive part of the
  picture.** `context/HOUSING-vancouver-wa.md` §26 records that the lowest-income households pay
  15.7% of income in excise and property taxes and the highest 4.4%. Renters pay property tax
  through rent. That is a question about who pays, not a reason to leave parks unfunded.
- **The 1% cap is already eating the existing district.** The GCPD was set up at 27¢ per $1,000 and
  is now about **13¢** because of Washington's 1%-a-year limit on revenue growth (p.16). That is the
  same I-747 constraint `context/` §26 identifies for the City. An expanded district would erode the
  same way, and the report does not say what rate ceiling it would carry.
- **The equity problem the task team itself raised.** A levy on top of the GCPD would make
  unincorporated-Vancouver residents pay two parks taxes (p.18). That is part of why the MPD
  expansion is preferred. It is a real point, and it cuts in the MPD's favour.

**What to ask for, when there is somewhere to ask:** a governing board other than the County Council
sitting ex officio; a published rate ceiling; revenue-sharing terms with the cities written into the
interlocal agreements *before* the petition drive, not after; and an accountability baseline. The
report's own market-research list includes *"accountability benchmarking and baseline data"* (p.19).
Hold them to it.

### Vancouver's parks comprehensive plan — and park impact fees on non-residential development

The PRCS comprehensive plan update runs **consultant selection winter 2027 → adoption spring 2028**
(deck, p.40). The 19 August minutes (p.4) say it will include **"updating the impact fee program,
including non-residential fees"**. Charging commercial development a park impact fee, instead of
loading it all onto housing, is **a genuinely good idea**. It shifts part of the cost of growth off
new homes. Watch for the fee levels and for whether the residential fee falls as the non-residential
one appears.

## Money and land

- **Countywide MPD:** ~**$15M/yr** at ~13¢ per $1,000 (task team report, p.16). That implies a
  countywide assessed value of roughly $115B; this is derived here, not stated in the report.
- **Oakbrook Community Park:** fire vandalism, **over $100k** of damage to the slide and surfacing.
  Repairs by year end (19 August minutes, p.7).
- **Vancouver Arts Hub:** the operating model shares cost between the City and a single nonprofit
  operator. The City is to *"support operating costs with operator"*. The current operator is
  Christine Richardson of The Arts Centered, and operator selection is a next step. Phase 1
  construction starts in 2028 (minutes, pp.5–6). **No dollar figures are in the packet.** The cost
  analysis was "shared" verbally and not attached.
- PRAC voted unanimously on 19 August to send a letter supporting an **RCO planning grant**
  application (minutes, p.4). The amount was not stated.
