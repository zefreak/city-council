## What is on the table

Read the fuller analysis in `briefs/2026-09-03-clark-county-planning-commission.md`, which covers
the same proposal in the context of the hearing that decides it. In short:

- **A moratorium on redevelopment of existing parks**, declared August 2025 and renewed **through
  February 2027**. The zoning is what replaces it; if the overlay is not adopted, the moratorium
  lapses in February 2027 with nothing behind it.
- **A new MHP overlay district (proposed CCC 40.250.120)** applying inside urban growth areas only.
  Permits parks, transitional housing and permanent supportive housing; requires a use exception to
  rezone for anything else.
- **New standards for new parks (proposed 40.260.140)**: no minimum density, maximum matching R-24,
  removal of the current 5–50 acre park size limits, parking at one space per dwelling and zero for
  units under 1,200 sq ft.
- Map of the proposed overlay:
  `gis.clark.wa.gov/portal/apps/instant/basic/index.html?appid=95d08cd911ce403e83ac7f08d479bb76`

**The two weaknesses to press on**, both from the draft text itself: the affordability clause
(subsection C.2) points at a subsection containing no affordability standard, and the use exception
(E.2, "not economically viable") is the route out that every appreciating-land park owner will
take. Bellingham's comparable overlay adds local provisions for notice, opportunity to purchase and
relocation assistance; Clark County's draft does not.

## Money and land

Nothing is appropriated. What is at stake is 34 parks and 2,033 homes, 81% of them inside urban
growth areas, on land the comprehensive plan is simultaneously upzoning to 24–36 units per acre.
No county funding for resident-ownership conversion appears in the package — the consultant memo
lists "exploring financial mechanisms" as a possible county role and stops there.
