## Bottom line

**A Monday afternoon facilitated retreat, held on a day the Council does not normally meet, at
which the five councillors set their policy priorities for the coming period from a staff-compiled
list whose first three entries are affordable housing, behavioural health access and
homelessness.** No vote, no public comment, no minutes beyond an audio recording — and it is the
meeting that determines what reaches a votable agenda later. The facilitator's own framing is
that the Council will "prioritize the issues" and "allocate limited resources."

## Watch

### What staff put on the list, and what that tells you

The deck is *"Prepared by Nancy Pionk, Facilitator"* and is built from individual interviews with
councillors conducted beforehand — so the list below is a synthesis of what the five of them
already said privately, not a staff proposal.

**"Major Issues Facing Clark County", in the deck's order:**

1. Affordable Housing
2. Mental/Behavioral/Public Health Access and Resources
3. Homelessness
4. Land use and planning (reducing sprawl and loss of agricultural land)
5. Concurrency — infrastructure is not keeping up with development
6. Unfunded mandates (i.e. public defense)
7. Escalating need / Diminishing Resources
8. Climate change

**"Opportunities":** supporting county small businesses; innovative land use planning; supporting
economic opportunity; enhancing the public health system.

**"Suggested Outcomes"**, the deck's own table:

| Issue | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Affordable Housing | Increase supply. Supply meets/exceeds income bands and housing unit goals. |
| Homelessness | Use meaningful data that shows decrease. |
| Mental/Behavioral Health | Use meaningful data that shows concerns/needs addressed. |
| Land Use | Less sprawl, more farmland preserved, miles travelled, climate emissions, integrated planning |
| Community satisfaction, livability | Cost of living / tax burden decreased, community feels safe |

**The left read.** Two of these are good and one is a trap.

*Good:* "supply meets/exceeds **income bands**" is the HAPT / HB 1220 framing — planning by income
band rather than by unit count. That is the right unit of measurement and it is the one under
which Clark County's own numbers look worst.

*Good:* "less sprawl, more farmland preserved" sits directly against what the Council is about to
do on 13 October, which is approve six agricultural de-designations having declined to require
one-for-one replacement. If this priority is adopted on Monday, it is quotable against the comp
plan vote three weeks later. Worth noting in the record for exactly that reason.

*The trap:* "cost of living / **tax burden decreased**" placed under community satisfaction, in
the same session as "escalating need / diminishing resources" and "seek and leverage funding."
A priorities list that commits to reducing the tax burden and to closing a housing and behavioural
health gap has not yet had the argument it is going to have. Which of those two survives contact
with the 2027 budget is the thing to watch, and it will be decided in sessions like this one
rather than at a hearing.

Also on the list of suggested values: *"Ensure support for public safety"* — the only service
named specifically, and worth tracking against where the money actually goes.

### The decision method is on the agenda, which is unusual and worth noticing

A third of the published packet is a handout titled **"Using Consensus"**, setting out a five-point
straw-poll scale from *"1 — I can say an enthusiastic yes"* to *"5 — I do not agree with the
decision and will actively block its movement."* The handout is explicit that a group must decide
in advance whether it is using *"pure"* consensus or *"modified"* consensus with a fallback such as
voting, and that *"it is important to define precisely the circumstances under which the fallback
method will be used."*

The agenda's first item is *"Discussion Expectations — What discussion and decision processes will
we use for this conversation?"*

This is a five-member council, sitting as a body, adopting a decision procedure for itself in a
meeting that takes no public comment. Nothing about it is improper — no binding action is taken at
a work session, and finger-scale straw polls are ordinary facilitation. But the practical effect
of consensus in a room with a 3–2 majority is that dissent registers as a "4" that does not block,
and no roll call exists afterwards to show who was where. **Ask for the audio.**

### The reflection exercise is where the real content is

The agenda's individual exercise asks councillors: *"Looking back at past County council policy
decisions, which policy decisions have had the greatest positive impact?"* and *"Which have created
challenges or unintended consequences that the County is still dealing with today?"*

Whatever the five of them name in answer to the second question, in the same fortnight as the comp
plan adoption and the third extension of the site-specific rezone freeze, is the most useful thing
that will be said at this meeting. It will exist only on the audio recording.

## Noted

- **Facilitator: Nancy Pionk.** The deck does not state whether she is under contract to the
  county, and no contract for facilitation services appears on the 15 September consent agenda or
  in any recent one I have read. Worth a records request if the fee matters.
- **The published times do not agree.** The weekly calendar and the agenda say **2:00pm**
  (agenda: 2 PM welcome, 5 PM adjourn); the facilitator's deck cover says **"September 14, 2026
  1-4 PM"**. The 2 September Council Time minutes settle it — County Manager Kathleen Otto stated
  the meeting *"will be on Monday, September 14th, from 2pm to 5pm with a preference for in-person
  attendance,"* and the Council asked for the Training Room. **2:00–5:00pm is right; the deck is
  wrong.**
- **The agenda PDF is headed "September 14, 2025."** A year typo on the county's own published
  agenda for a 2026 meeting. Harmless, but it is the kind of thing that makes a document hard to
  find later.
- **A Monday meeting is unusual for this Council** — the ordinary days are Tuesday (regular
  meetings) and Wednesday (Council Time and work sessions). This one was noticed properly: it
  appears on the Weekly Calendar published the preceding Thursday and was announced at Council
  Time on 2 September. It is not a short-notice special meeting under RCW 42.30.080.
- Council: Sue Marshall (Chair, District 5), Glen Yung (1), Michelle Belkot (2), Wil Fuentes (3),
  Matt Little (4).
