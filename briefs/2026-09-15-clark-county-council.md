## Bottom line

Two public hearings, and the quiet one is the second. **A resolution extends the suspension of
Clark County's annual site-specific comprehensive plan amendment cycle to 30 October 2026 — the
third such extension, taking the freeze to three years and one month, with no site-specific rezone
approvable before roughly late October 2027.** Alongside it, eight impact fees move in opposite
directions, county development fees rise 6–42%, and this is a **6:00pm evening meeting with Open
Public Comment on any county business** — which makes it the best-attended broad-scope slot of the
month.

## Act on this

### Open Public Comment is on the agenda, and its scope is *any* county business

This is a regular Tuesday Council meeting held in the evening. Under the Council's Rules of
Procedure §VII.L the Tuesday slot is three minutes, once, and it is **not limited to the agenda** —
the only routine opening to raise something the Council did not put on its own agenda. Note the
agenda places Open Public Comment **before** both hearings, which is the reordering the draft Rules
of Procedure amendment proposed; that draft went to hearing on 1 September. Time may not be yielded
to another speaker without majority Council approval, so twenty people at three minutes is a
different instrument from one person at an hour.

Written comment: `www.clark.wa.gov/councilors/public-comment`, or post to Clark County Council,
c/o Rebecca Messinger, PO Box 5000, Vancouver WA 98666-5000.

**The thing to raise in that slot, if you raise anything:** the Council's comprehensive plan
adoption hearings are **1, 2 and 5 October**, set at Council Time on 26 August in an item added to
the agenda that morning and recorded only in the minutes. As of 4 September they appear on **no
published notice** — the county's own comp-plan events calendar ends at 15 September, and the
5 August news release still says 6 October with adoption 13 October. The Council Time meeting on
9 September, where this would normally be confirmed, is cancelled for lack of quorum. **15 September
is the last scheduled public meeting before those hearings begin**, and Open Public Comment is the
only place to say so on the record.

### Public hearing 1 — Development fees, effective 1 January 2027

Ordinance amending CCC 6.110A.010, 6.110A.040, 6.140.020-1 and 6.140.030-1. Staff: Chad Dragon.
This is the item that has had the least public process: a Council work session on **5 August**, a
presentation to the Development Engineering Advisory Board on **3 September** (which "signalled its
support… through a vote"), and a legal notice in the Columbian on **28 August**. No community
outreach beyond that is claimed in the staff report.

**What it does.** Community Development ran a cost-of-service study in 2024; these fees move Land
Use Review and Wetland & Habitat Review to **100% cost recovery** — staff wages and benefits,
department and countywide overhead — plus a **2.5% technology fee** on the two planning-fee tables.
The stated aim is to cut General Fund subsidy of both programmes. Before the 2024 study there had
been "no significant increases in either program's fees since 2009."

| | Average increase | Extra revenue, 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Land Use Review | **8%** | $134,326 |
| Wetland & Habitat Review | **7%** | $28,783 |
| **Total** | | **$163,109** (Fund 1011) |

Most lines rise 6%. The outliers, from the comparison table in the hearing packet:

| Fee | Now | Proposed | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final site plan inspection by planner | $1,157 | $1,642 | **+42%** |
| Wetland/habitat 6.110A.040.1.F | $8,126 | $10,460 | **+29%** |
| Conditional use permit (Type III) | $8,291 | $10,636 | **+28%** |
| Planned unit development / master plan (Type III) | $8,291 | $10,636 | **+28%** |
| Plat alteration (Type III) | $8,291 | $10,636 | **+28%** |
| Zone change | $14,776 | $17,515 | **+19%** |
| Variance (Type III) | $14,297 | $17,007 | **+19%** |
| **Subdivision, base fee up to 16 lots** | **$15,661** | **$18,453** | **+18%** |
| Post decision review (Type III) | $15,808 | $18,609 | +18% |

The staff presentation gives the reason for the largest jumps and it is not padding: the two
hearings examiners' hourly rate went from **$200 to $275 on 1 May 2026**, a 38% increase, "to bring
the rates up to market."

**The left read.** Cost recovery on planning fees is the defensible default — the alternative is
the General Fund, meaning county taxpayers subsidise the review of private development
applications, which is a transfer from residents to developers. That argument is strong and it is
the county's argument.

The two places it strains:

1. **The subdivision fee is a flat base fee up to 16 lots.** $18,453 whether you plat 3 lots or 16.
   Per lot that is $1,153 at 16 and **$6,151 at 3** — a fixed cost that falls five times harder on
   small infill plats than on a large one. Same shape as the flat impact-fee problem Washington's
   proportional-impact-fee law was written to address. If the Council wants the fee to recover cost
   without penalising small builders, a per-lot band is the fix and this is the hearing to ask for it.
2. **A 2.5% technology fee on top of 100% cost recovery is, by construction, more than 100%.** The
   staff report's worked example is explicit: boundary line adjustment costs $687 to deliver, the
   proposed fee is $704. It is a small surcharge for a real purpose, but the ordinance recites
   "100% of current costs of service" and then charges 102.5%, and the two recitals sit in the same
   document.

**Also in this ordinance, and not mentioned in the staff report's summary:** Table 6.140.020-1,
the list of building components eligible for *deferred* submittal, is cut hard. **Fire sprinkler,
fire alarm systems, exit illumination, emergency call systems, electrical systems, plumbing
systems, HVAC systems, auxiliary power systems, irrigation, carports, bleachers, signs, specialty
retaining walls, and wooden/steel/composite floor or roof trusses** all come off the list.
Deferred submittal is what lets a builder pull a permit and start while those component designs are
still being engineered; removing them means the full design must be in at initial submittal. For
multifamily and commercial work that is a real change to the construction timeline, and **no part
of the staff report explains it.** Two flat residential fees are also deleted — "simple garage,
carport, patio cover or deck" ($350.50) and "business move-in only" ($436.50) — which sends those
jobs back to valuation-based pricing. Whether that is up or down depends on the valuation and the
staff report does not model it.

### Public hearing 2 — Annual review suspension extension, and what it actually means

Resolution **2026-09-01**, staff contact Bart Catching. On the agenda this sits at the bottom of
the school-and-fire-fees hearing block, in one line, and it is the item most likely to pass
unremarked.

**The chronology, from the resolution's own recitals:**

| Date | What |
|---|---|
| Dec 2025 | RCW 36.70A.130(5)(b) deadline for Clark County to complete its periodic update |
| 6 Sep 2022 | Res. 2022-09-03 suspends the annual site-specific amendment cycle, **1 Oct 2023 – 30 Sep 2025** |
| 23 Sep 2025 | Res. 2025-09-01 extends it to **30 Sep 2026** |
| 15 Sep 2026 | Res. 2026-09-01 would extend it to **30 Oct 2026** |
| mid-Oct 2026 | 2025 periodic update "now expected to be approved" |
| 31 Oct 2026 | Pre-applications may be submitted under CCC 40.560.040(A)(2) for the 2027 cycle |
| ~late Oct 2027 | Earliest a final adopting ordinance can pass — "no sooner than one year after the adoption of the 2025-2045 comprehensive plan" |

So: a periodic update statutorily due **December 2025** is expected **mid-October 2026**, about ten
months late; the freeze on site-specific amendments will have run **three years and one month**
when this extension expires; and because approval cannot come sooner than a year after the comp
plan is adopted, **no site-specific comprehensive plan amendment or rezone can actually be granted
until roughly late October 2027 — four years after the suspension began.**

**Both halves, because this genuinely points both ways.**

*Against:* four years is a long time to close the only route by which a specific parcel's
designation can be corrected outside a once-a-decade update. Someone whose land is mis-designated,
or who wants a site rezoned for housing, has had one opening since 2023 — the periodic update
itself — and the written record on that closed at the Planning Commission hearing on **3 September
2026**. The consequence of the delay is not neutral: the longer the update runs late, the longer
the freeze it justifies.

*For:* the annual site-specific cycle is also the mechanism through which urban growth boundary
expansions and rural rezones get picked off one parcel at a time, outside any comprehensive
analysis of capacity or infrastructure. RCW 36.70A.130(2)(a) genuinely limits a county to
considering revisions once a year, and running a parallel amendment cycle against a pending
periodic update is exactly what it forbids. The suspension is legally required, not a policy
choice; the Prosecuting Attorney has approved the resolution as to form. **The criticism belongs on
the ten-month delay, not on the suspension.**

Worth noting on the record: the resolution as drafted already recites, in the past tense, that
"the Council took public testimony from interested parties, considered all the written and oral
arguments." That is normal pre-drafting for a document meant to be signed the same night, but it is
what it is — the finding is written before the hearing.

### Public hearing 2 — school and fire impact fees (unchanged from the 2 September version)

Fees are paid at building permit issuance on new residential construction in unincorporated Clark
County, and may fund only capital projects needed to serve *new* growth — not salaries, not
maintenance, not capacity needed for existing development.

| District | Single family | Multi-family | Planning Commission, 16 July |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Evergreen** (CPZ2025-00004) | $6,432 → **$0** (−100%) | $3,753 → **$0** | 7–0 approve |
| **Hockinson** (CPZ2025-00005) | $7,790 → **$15,174.54** (+95%) | $3,434 → **$1,720.12** (−50%) | 5–2 approve |
| **La Center** (CPZ2025-00006) | $3,501 → **$7,536.00** (+115%) | $3,104 → $3,045.74 (−2%) | 7–0 approve |
| **Washougal** (CPZ2025-00007) | $0 → $0 | $0 → $0 | 7–0 approve |
| **Woodland** (CPZ2025-00008) | $5,900 → **$10,545.93** (+79%) | $5,900 → **$13,664.57** (+132%) | **4–3 DENY** |

Evergreen also zeroes its ADU fee, $938.25 → $0. Figures from
`data/clark-county/2026-09-15/school-impact-fees.txt`, lines 42–45, 1068–1070, 2056–2058,
3080–3082 and 4254–4256.

**Fire impact fees — new, where there were none.** The Council authorised fire districts to seek
these in February 2024 (ORD 2024-02-03); none submitted plans at the time. Four now have. Note the
agenda assigns each its own case number — **CPZ2025-00010 through -00013** — where the digest
listing repeated -00010 for all four.

| District | Single family | Multi-family | Non-residential |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fire District 3** (-00010) | $0 → $992 | $0 → $505/unit | $1.36/sq ft |
| **Fire District 6** (-00011) | $0 → $791 | $0 → $563/unit | $0.91/sq ft |
| **Fire District 10** (-00012) | $3,975–$4,714 (see below) | $3,192–$4,823/unit | $2.49–$3.24/sq ft |
| **Clark-Cowlitz Fire Rescue** (-00013) | $0 → $654 | $0 → $308/unit | $0.63/sq ft |

The Planning Commission recommended all four **5–2**. Fire District 10 is the outlier by a factor
of five and its staff report presents **two methods, not one recommendation** — "Standard Method"
and "Growth Share Method", each with and without a sprinkler discount under RCW 82.02.100(B).
Single family runs $4,569 (standard) or $4,714 (growth share) undiscounted, $3,975 / $4,101 with
sprinklers. **Which method the Council is being asked to adopt is not stated in the part of the
report read here**, and the gap between District 10 and Clark-Cowlitz — $4,569 against $654 for the
same kind of house — is the first question to ask. No comments from any agency or the public had
been received on District 10's proposal as of the staff report.

Collection trigger: under CCC 40.630.090 **no fire impact fee may be collected until the district
signs an interlocal agreement with the county**, so adoption is not the last step.

**The left read, in both directions.** *Against the Woodland and Hockinson increases*: an impact
fee is a per-unit charge on new housing, partly capitalised into price and partly deterring
marginal units. A $15,174 single-family fee in Hockinson and a **$13,664 per-unit multi-family fee
in Woodland** are large against the cost of the unit, and the multi-family figure being *higher
than* the single-family one is backwards from any density-supporting position. Three commissioners
voted to deny Woodland's fees; that is the strongest signal in the package.

*For them*: the alternative funding source for school and fire capital is a levy or a bond, which
falls on *existing* residents including renters through pass-through, and which needs supermajority
approval that rural districts often fail to get. A fee legally restricted to growth-caused capacity
is not the regressive option. Evergreen's zero is not generosity to developers either — the
district told the county in writing (LeAnne Bremer, Miller Nash, 13 May 2026) that "upon adoption,
the District will **not be entitled** to collect school impact fees," because it has surplus
capacity and declining enrollment. Charging a fee with no eligible project would be unlawful.

*The question spanning both*: Evergreen's own capital facilities plan says enrollment declines for
six years but that the City's and County's 20-year plans "could result in significantly more
housing" and enrollment "is likely to gradually increase." Zeroing the fee for 2026–2032 means any
capacity built when that growth lands is paid for by levy, not by the development that caused it.
The district can amend its plan annually; **whether it will, and on what trigger, is the thing to
ask on the record.**

## Consent agenda

Passes as a block on a single motion, no debate, unless someone asks for an item to be pulled
before the agenda is approved. All seven staff reports read.

1. **Warrants, 08/01–08/31/2026: $26,340,860.41.** One month of county payments, approved without
   itemisation on the agenda.
2. **Minutes, 1 September 2026.**
3. **Board of Equalization — reconvene past 21 October 2026.** Routine in form; the number inside
   is not. The BOE "has received approximately **600 petitions** to date for the assessed property
   values in 2026" and cannot hear them until the Assessor certifies values around 17 September. The
   statutory four-week session (24 Sep – 21 Oct) will not cover them, so RCW 84.48.010 approval is
   needed to sit longer. Same request was made in 2024 and 2025. **600 assessment appeals is a
   readable signal about where valuations went**, and the appeal route is open to anyone, including
   small landlords whose costs pass through to rent.
4. **Arts Commission — repurpose $4,458** from Festival & Event Expenses to "Arts Talk Live", a free
   public event series. No new budget capacity. Fine, and a useful benchmark: this is the county
   arts commission's entire discretionary event budget, against Vancouver's $7m/year cultural fund.
5. **Temporary road closure, NW Division Ave and NE Creston Ave, up to 40 days.** Advanced
   Excavating Specialists LLC building **the City of Vancouver's West Vancouver Phase 1 Sanitary
   Sewer Improvements**. Full-width excavation; contractor to maintain resident and emergency
   access. Sewer capacity is a housing-capacity constraint, so this is more than a traffic notice.
   Notification to residents under RCW 47.48.020 is required before work starts.
6. **Noxious weed control, Dept. of Ecology / Washington Conservation Corps, WCC-26021 — not to
   exceed $101,440.** Up to 17 weeks of crew in year 1 at **$1,585 per crew-day**, about **3,840
   hours**, roughly $26/hour paid to the state for AmeriCorps members who receive a living
   allowance well below that. Done annually since 2014. **The flag is the wording, not the
   amount:** the request authorises the County Manager to sign "and amendments to **extend or
   increase** the agreement cost", with year 2 to be added in summer 2027 — an open-ended
   pre-approval of future spending, granted on consent.
7. **Interlocal agreement with the City of Ridgefield for public works maintenance and projects.**
   RCW 39.34. Five-year term with options to extend **up to fifteen further years in five-year
   increments, each subject to Council approval**. Fully reimbursed by Ridgefield. A twenty-year
   outer horizon on a consent item; the per-extension Council approval is the safeguard and it is
   real.
