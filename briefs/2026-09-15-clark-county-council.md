## Bottom line

Eight impact fees move in the same hearing and they move in opposite directions: **Evergreen
School District's fee goes to zero, three rural school districts' single-family fees rise 79–115%,
and four fire districts get a fee where they have never had one.** The Planning Commission split
on three of them. This is an evening hearing, which is unusual and makes it easier to attend.

### What the fees do

**School impact fees.** Fees are paid at building permit issuance on new residential construction,
in unincorporated Clark County. They may fund only capital projects needed to serve *new* growth —
not salaries, not maintenance, not capacity needed for existing development.

**Fire impact fees — new, where there were none.** The Council authorised fire districts to seek
these in February 2024 (ORD 2024‑02‑03); none submitted plans at the time. Four now have.

| District | Single family | Multi-family | Non-residential |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fire District 3** | $0 → $992 | $0 → $505/unit | $1.36/sq ft |
| **Fire District 6** | $0 → $791 | $0 → $563/unit | $0.91/sq ft |
| **Clark-Cowlitz Fire Rescue** | $0 → $654 | $0 → $308/unit | $0.63/sq ft |
| **Fire District 10** | $3,975–$4,714 (see below) | $3,192–$4,823/unit | $2.49–$3.24/sq ft |

The Planning Commission recommended all four **5–2**. Fire District 10 is the outlier by a factor
of five and its staff report presents **two methods, not one recommendation** — "Standard Method"
and "Growth Share Method", each with and without a sprinkler discount under RCW 82.02.100(B).
Single family runs $4,569 (standard) or $4,714 (growth share) undiscounted, $3,975 / $4,101 with
sprinklers. **Which method the Council is being asked to adopt is not stated in the part of the
report read here**, and the gap between District 10 and Clark-Cowlitz — $4,569 against $654 for the
same kind of house — is the first question to ask. No comments from any agency or the public had
been received on District 10's proposal as of the staff report.

Note the collection trigger: under CCC 40.630.090 **no fire impact fee may be collected until the
district signs an interlocal agreement with the county**, so adoption is not the last step.

### The left read, in both directions

**Against the Woodland and Hockinson increases**: an impact fee is a per-unit charge on new
housing. In a market where supply is the binding constraint it is partly capitalised into price and
partly deters marginal units — a $15,174 single-family fee in Hockinson and a **$13,664 per-unit
multi-family fee in Woodland** are large numbers against the cost of the unit, and the multi-family
figure being *higher than* the single-family one is backwards from any density-supporting position.
Washington's new proportional-impact-fee law exists precisely because flat per-unit fees fall
hardest on small units. Three commissioners voted to deny Woodland's fees; that is the strongest
signal in the package.

**For them**: the alternative funding source for school and fire capital is a levy or a bond, which
falls on *existing* residents including renters through pass-through, and which requires
supermajority approval that rural districts often fail to get. A fee on new development that is
legally restricted to growth-caused capacity is not the regressive option here. Evergreen's zero is
not generosity to developers either — the district told the county in writing (LeAnne Bremer,
Miller Nash, 13 May 2026) that "upon adoption, the District will **not be entitled** to collect
school impact fees," because it has surplus capacity and declining enrollment. Charging a fee with
no eligible project would be unlawful.

**The question that spans both**: Evergreen's own capital facilities plan says enrollment declines
for six years but that the City's and County's 20-year plans "could result in significantly more
housing" and enrollment "is likely to gradually increase." Zeroing the fee for 2026–2032 means any
capacity built when that growth lands is paid for by levy, not by the development that caused it.
The district can amend its plan annually; **whether it will, and on what trigger, is the thing to
ask on the record.**

## Money and land

The eight fee schedules above are the whole of it. No contracts, no land, no appropriation — but
these are the numbers that govern what every new home in unincorporated Clark County pays for
schools and fire for the next six years.
