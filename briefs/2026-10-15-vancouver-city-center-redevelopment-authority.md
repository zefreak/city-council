## Watch

### Item 7 — Development Fee Update (briefing; Council action on traffic impact fees Q1 2027)

What the deck (att-5208) shows:

- **Vancouver's fees are low, by staff's own numbers.** Impact fees and SDCs on a 50-unit project
  total **$10,963 per unit**, the lowest of seven jurisdictions in ECONorthwest's comparison. Portland
  is $18,776, Camas $26,833 and Beaverton $33,081 (slide 12). All fees combined come to **2.87–3.65%
  of development cost** on the two sample apartment projects (slide 5).
- **The planned changes** (slide 7): update traffic impact fees and water/sewer SDCs; **add a park
  impact fee on commercial development**; add engineering-review and inspection fees; and index all
  fees to CPI. Traffic impact fee action is in Q1 2027, effective Q3 2027. Parks, SDCs and review
  fees follow in Q3 2027 (slide 16).
- **The capital list behind it** (slide 9): a $300M PFAS water programme, a $300M sewer solids
  programme, $345M in transportation, including the 32nd Street bypass at $86M, and a $166M parks plan
  with 800+ acres to acquire.
- **The framing.** Slides 10, 13 and 14 argue that increases *"could push total fees above State
  average and hinder new development"*, that the market *"is not conducive to absorbing significant
  fee increases"*, and they list *"Pause new increases until market conditions improve"* as an option.

**The left read, both halves.** Impact fees are a real cost on new housing, and they fall hardest on
small projects; the deck's point that middle housing pays proportionally more (slide 13) is correct.
But the project's own housing analysis finds that zoned capacity is not the binding constraint
(context §3) and that the plan prices its affordability gap without funding it (§6). The alternative
to growth paying for its pipes is existing residents, including renters, paying through utility rates.
**The commercial park impact fee is genuinely good.** It moves part of the parks cost off housing,
and the deck itself says commercial development *"does not share burden of new open space
investments"*. **The ask:** take the commercial park fee and the affordable-housing exemptions
(slide 14 lists them as an option, *"as allowed by statute"*) together, and do not let "pause"
become the default. Fire permit and review fees already rose **98–152%** between 2024 and 2026 on the
sample projects. That is cost recovery that has already happened, and the deck does not dwell on it.

### Item 6 — Downtown parking: fines up, and boots and impound for repeat unpaid tickets

From att-5209:

- **Fines, unchanged since 2017:** overtime $30 → **$40**, improper parking $35 → $40, prohibited
  parking $40 → **$65**, plate violations $50 → $65 (slide 14).
- **A scofflaw programme.** A vehicle with **4+ citations unpaid for 45+ days** goes on a list, then
  can be booted and impounded. Staff count at least 15 notices and 77 days before impound, with appeal
  and payment plans *"at every step"* (slides 16 and 22). 1,110 vehicles would qualify; they are under
  2% of cited vehicles and carry **62% of delinquent fines**. Out-of-state and no-plate vehicles hold
  47% of unpaid fines.
- **Seven-day paid parking north of Evergreen** (Phase II), with high- and low-demand zones and
  *"affordable on-street parking options for residents and employees"* in the low-demand ones.

**The left read.** Charging for curb space and keeping fines current are defensible. The **boot and
impound** step is where this bites. **The deck never mentions people living in vehicles.** For them an
impound is the loss of their shelter, and 4 unpaid tickets is a low bar for someone parked downtown
with nowhere else to go. Payment plans help only people who can pay. **Ask for a hardship waiver and
an explicit exclusion, or at minimum a referral to outreach before a booting, for vehicles in use as
a residence.** Ask for it before the code is drafted, not at the hearing.

**Timing conflict.** This deck says *"Adoption of new code by City Council (Dec 2026)"*, with the
scofflaw programme live in September 2027. The rolling page's diary, taken from an earlier Council
calendar, has the Title 19 scofflaw code on **consent on 9 November** and a hearing on 16 November.
**Which is right is not known.** Watch both.

### Item 4 — Active Ground Floor Study "adoption"

BAE's study (att-5210) recommends five near-term actions. The two that matter:

- **"Leverage publicly-owned sites"**: offer City land *"at discounted rates, either through a
  low-cost ground lease or fee simple sale"*, and work with **Clark County and the Vancouver Housing
  Authority** to identify their surplus sites for *"industry target"* development. VHA land is housing
  authority land. If it is in play for ground-floor commercial, the question is what housing it is not
  being used for. **Ask that any discounted disposition of public land carry affordability or
  community-benefit terms**, and that VHA land stay first in line for housing.
- **Public ownership of ground floors, and better for it.** It recommends the City buy the ground floors
  of new buildings, or locate City services in them, and lease the space to local businesses; and
  subsidise childcare downtown. That is social ownership of commercial space, and it is a genuinely
  good idea.

Also recommended: waivers from the Downtown Design Guidelines and Active Ground Floor overlays for
"target industries", and *"strategic acquisition"* of private sites. **What "adoption" commits the
City to is unclear.** No staff report or resolution is attached. In July the board asked for
flexibility *"rather than taking an overly prescriptive approach"* (minutes).
