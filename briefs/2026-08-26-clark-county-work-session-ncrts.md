## Bottom line

A waste facility is being sited in north Clark County and the decision is being shaped in the one
meeting format that **takes no public comment at all**. Phase I and II of the siting study were
presented today; by the time this reaches a votable item, the candidate sites will already be
chosen.

## Watch

**Work sessions are the quiet channel.** Rules §VIII: work sessions exist to give the Council
"pertinent policy-making information" and "a final review of matters to come before the Council at
a regular meeting or public hearing," and "**There is no public comment during Work Sessions.**"
Minutes are an audio recording only, uploaded at the end of the session. So the only ways in are:

1. **Listen to the audio recording**, posted to the county website after the session.
2. **Read the two decks** — Parametrix (the consultant) and the county's own — linked above.
3. **Raise it at a Tuesday Council meeting** under open public comment, which is the broad-scope
   slot and takes any matter of county business whether or not it is on the agenda. The next one
   is **Tuesday 1 September, 10am**.

**Why siting is the whole question.** A transfer station is a genuine public good — the
alternative to one in north county is longer hauls, higher tipping costs and more illegal dumping
— and it should be said plainly that the county needs the facility. The fight is never over
whether, it is over *where*, and siting studies reliably land facilities next to the households
with the least capacity to object. Phase I and II typically means screening criteria and a
narrowed candidate site list; **which criteria were used, how they were weighted, and which sites
survived are all in the decks I could not open.** Those are the three things to check.

**Timeline is unknown.** Nothing on the agenda says when a preferred site is selected, when SEPA
review starts, or whether a public hearing is scheduled. Until a hearing is noticed there is no
formal comment right on this at all.

## Money and land

same solid-waste programme is **an inference, not a finding** — the staff report is unread.
