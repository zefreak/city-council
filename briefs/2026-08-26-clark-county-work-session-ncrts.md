## Bottom line

The shortlist is down to **four sites out of an original 158**, and the consultant's recommendation
is that further scoring will not separate them — so the final cut to two will be made on **which
landowners are most likely to sell**, not on the 35-criteria scoring system. Staff are asking
Council to authorise **Phase 2 and an Environmental Impact Statement**, and — this is the useful
part — **to hold a public hearing on the siting**. That hearing is the first formal comment right
this project has ever had.

## Act on this

**A contract amendment and a public hearing are both being recommended, and neither is scheduled

county business** — the next one is **Tuesday 1 September, 10am**.

## What the study actually found

**The funnel.** 158 possible sites (aggregating up to five parcels each) → **15** on primary
screening → **7** on detailed review → **4** recommended for further consideration.

| Stage | Test applied |
|---|---|
| 158 → 15 | Not residentially zoned · not in a flood zone · at least 20 usable acres · within 1 mile of essential utilities · FAA-compliant |
| 15 → 7 | Desktop studies: wetlands/habitat, geotechnical, cultural and historic resources, traffic |
| 7 → 4 | Scored against **35 siting criteria** |

**The four sites recommended for further consideration: 12/13, 116, 137 and 141.** Dropped: sites
**11 and 127**, both inside the **City of Ridgefield** and zoned commercial/employment; and site
**117**, lowest-scoring with "the second highest cost of varying cost centers."

**The facility.** A **1,500-ton-per-day** transfer station on a **20 usable acre** site. Built
around 2032, on a stated **60+ year** horizon (the county's table projects 2032 → 2062 → 2092).

**Why the county says it needs it.** All three existing transfer stations are in south county and
"operate at or near capacity"; Central and West Van were built in **1992** and Washougal in
**2009**, and all three hit their MSA end in **2032**. Drive times from the north end are the case:
La Center 25 minutes to Central, **Yacolt 31 minutes to Central and 51 to West Van**, before
traffic or wait times.

## The two things worth flagging

### 1. The final cut is being made on willing sellers, not on score

This is the finding. Parametrix states (slides 5, 8, 9):

> "Sites with 'potentially' willing sellers were identified (**5 out of 7**)."
>
> "Further site study in a focused area site screening is **not likely to score sites 12/13, 116,
> 137 and 141 differently**."
>
> "Recommend moving to an environmental assessment (EIS) for the **two sites with the strongest
> likelihood of willing sellers**."

Read those together: the consultant says the four finalists are effectively tied on the merits, so
the tiebreak is landowner willingness. That is not corrupt and it is not irrational — a site whose
owner will not sell costs years and possibly condemnation — but it means **a 35-criteria scoring
system built over two years stops governing at the last and most consequential cut**, and the
facility lands where a landowner wants a transaction. If you have one question for this project,
it is: *what are the four sites, and who owns them?*

Note also what "further consideration" now excludes: a **focused area site screening** phase has
been skipped on the grounds that it would not change the ranking. That is a defensible engineering
judgement and also one fewer public stage.

### 2. Ownership is explicitly undecided — and public operation is on the list

The county deck, slide 8, is unusually clear and worth quoting because it is an opening rather than
a warning:

> "The siting process does not determine who will own or operate the facility. Multiple ownership
> and operating models remain available, including: **private ownership and operation (current
> model)**; **public ownership with contracted operation**; **public ownership with public
> operation**. Future ownership and operational structures would be evaluated separately from this
> siting study."

So the current model is private, and public ownership with public operation is on the county's own
list of live options for a 60-year piece of essential infrastructure being built with public
siting work. **"Evaluated separately" is where that gets decided, and no date, forum or process for
that evaluation appears in either deck.** For anyone who cares about public ownership of essential
services, this is the decision to get in front of — and it is currently unscheduled and unwatched.

## Correcting the earlier version

The earlier brief wrote that "siting studies reliably land facilities next to the households with
the least capacity to object." On the evidence of these decks, that framing does not fit the
primary screen, which excluded **residentially zoned** land at the very first cut, along with flood
zones. That is a better screen than the earlier version assumed, and it should be said.

The qualification stands, though, and it is not a small one: **"not residentially zoned" is not the
same as "not near residents."** Rural, agricultural and industrial parcels adjacent to homes pass
that screen, and the 35 criteria and their **weightings are not published in either deck** — the
scoring table is an image with no figures in the text layer. Whether proximity to housing is
scored at all, and how heavily, is unknown.

## One argument in the decks worth noticing

Parametrix's closing slides argue against a regional (multi-county) alternative: "**Regionalization
of solid waste facilities and services in Washington has not occurred for good reasons**" and works
only "in areas with sufficient population such as Portland Metro," with reference travel distances
to the Cowlitz County stations at Woodland and Longview (Battle Ground to Longview is 35 miles each
way). Read that as the alternative being closed off — it is the case against *not* building, and it
is made by the firm paid to find a site. The "good reasons" are not stated.
