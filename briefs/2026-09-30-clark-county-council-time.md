## Act on this

### Item 5.2 — SAVE Act Letter Request

**What it is.** The attachment is a **blank National Association of Counties (NACo) template**:
`[COUNTY NAME]`, `[NUMBER] registered voters`, `[$AMOUNT]` and so on are all unfilled. Nothing
published says which councilor asked for it or what the County's own figures would be.

The letter asks US Senators to oppose putting the SAVE America Act (H.R. 7296 / S. 3752) into the
FY 2027 NDAA (H.R. 8800) or into a reconciliation package. It makes three arguments, all quoted
from the template:

- **Timing.** The Act takes effect on passage with no transition period, weeks before the
  3 November election. By then ballots are printed and equipment has been tested.
- **Money.** It carries no federal funding. NACo puts the national cost at **~$510M per election
  cycle**, which it calls 11.3× the $45M of FY 2026 HAVA funding.
- **Criminal exposure for poll workers.** It creates penalties for registering an applicant who
  fails to present documentary proof of citizenship, without saying which election workers are
  liable.

It also cites research "highlighted by the Bipartisan Policy Center": **9%** of eligible voters
lack, or cannot easily get, documentary proof of citizenship.

**The left read.** This is a documentary-proof-of-citizenship requirement. It would fall hardest on
people whose papers don't match their current name — mostly married women — and on low-income,
elderly, rural and naturalised voters. Opposing it is plainly good. The template makes the case
through administrative cost rather than voting rights. That framing is chosen to win votes from
conservative county officials, and it is the right one for this room. Note its fallback ask: *"if
Congress moves forward … 18 to 24 months before requirements take effect … strike criminal
penalties."* That concedes the requirement could be acceptable if funded and delayed. A comment can
support the letter while asking the Council not to soften it further.

**How to act.**

- Three minutes at the start of the meeting. Say "item 5.2".
- Or send written comment in advance: https://clark.wa.gov/councilors/public-comment
- The ask is simple: **send the letter, and fill in Clark County's own figures** — the number of
  registered voters and the cost to the Auditor. A template sent with its blanks filled in carries
  more weight than a signature on NACo's text.

**Firm vs not.** Every figure above comes from NACo's template. None was checked against the bill
text, NACo's own analysis, or the BPC research. The bill numbers and the claim that H.R. 8800
carries the Act are also unverified.

### Chair Marshall is drafting a comp plan amendment on cluster development on resource lands

This comes from the **23 September minutes**, which are up for approval here. Marshall added an
item that morning, **"Cluster Development on Resource Lands"**, which did not appear on the
published agenda. She and Deputy County Manager April Furth explained how clustering works on
resource lands. Community Planning Director Oliver Orjiako gave its history. Marshall *"explained
why a change to Cluster Development is necessary in the Comprehensive Plan"* and *"stated she would
work with Oliver Orjiako on a potential amendment."*

**Direction: unclear, and no text exists.** The draft plan's Rural and Natural Resources Element
(Chapter 3) currently lets agricultural and forest land cluster lots at their base densities:

> "Clustering of parcels is allowed consistent with platting and zoning requirements and the Clark
> County Code" (policies 3.4.4 forest, 3.5.4 agriculture).

The chapter says clustering on agricultural land rests on RCW 36.70A.177 and was added in the 2016
update. Base densities are **AG-20**, **FR-40** and **FR-80** (1 unit per 20, 40 and 80 acres).

The minutes do not say whether the change would tighten clustering or loosen it.

- **Tightening** would reduce residential lots carved out of farmland and forest. That is
  anti-sprawl.
- **Loosening** would increase them.

**This is a quiet-channel item twice over.** It was added the morning of a meeting, and a Council
amendment can be moved at the 1 October hearing without ever appearing in the Planning Commission
record. Watch for it at the hearing. If it is moved, ask that its text be read into the record
before the vote. See `briefs/2026-10-01-clark-county-comp-plan-hearing.md`.
