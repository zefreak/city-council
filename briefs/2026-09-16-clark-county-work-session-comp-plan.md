## Wednesday 16 September 2026, 9:00am

Public Service Center, 6th Floor **Hearing Room**, 1300 Franklin St, Vancouver WA · hybrid via
WebEx (webinar 2491 559 8171, password `BOCC` / `2622`). **Work session — no public comment
is taken.** All three published documents read and cached at

## Bottom line

**This is the last public meeting before the County Council opens its adoption hearing on the
entire 2025–2045 comprehensive plan, and the hearing is three weeks away with no notice of it
published anywhere on the county's own comp-plan events calendar.** The Planning Commission's
recommendation arrives with one large hole in it: the multifamily zoning consolidation for the
unincorporated Vancouver UGA **failed 3–4**, while the density standards that go with it passed
4–3, so the Council is being handed the rules for zones the Commission declined to put on the
map. And the enforceable version of the farmland Transfer of Development Rights policy was voted
down **2–5 after** every agricultural de-designation had already been approved.

## Watch

### The zoning consolidation failed 3–4, and its rulebook passed 4–3

Item 11 would have collapsed five multifamily and office-residential zones in the unincorporated
Vancouver UGA into three, in each case upward:

| From | To |
|---|---|
| R-12 | R-24 |
| R-18, R-22 | R-36 |
| R-30, R-43 | R-50 |
| OR-15 | OR-24 |
| OR-18, OR-22 | OR-36 |
| OR-30, OR-43 | OR-50 |

Staff's stated purpose (minutes p. 30, Jose Alvarez): *"part of the housing strategies to try to
accommodate more housing and also the affordability with the multi-family zoning and to meet our
requirement to provide housing for at all income levels."*

**It failed 3–4** — Halbert, Bergthold and Chair Johnson aye; Fadness, Barca, Harroun and
LeDuc Montgomery nay. But items 63 (*40.200.040 Minimum and Maximum Calculations*) and 65
(*40.220.020 Residential and Office Residential Districts*) — the Title 40 text that sets the
minimum and maximum densities **for those same consolidated zones** — both passed 4–3.

So the recommendation the Council receives on 16 September says: adopt the density standards,
do not adopt the map that uses them. Nobody at the hearing reconciled that. It is the single most
likely thing to be resolved quietly in Council deliberation on 13 October, because it reads as a
technical inconsistency rather than a policy choice.

**What the fight is actually about: minimum densities.** Commissioner Fadness (p. 30):

> *"the R-30 upzoning to R-50, I'm all for the upzoning, I think it's great to give the options,
> but upzoning R-30 to then have a minimum of 40 units per acre it's just not feasible by the
> market."*

Commissioner Harroun, summarising the developer position (p. 35): *"it's keeping the minimums and
then increasing the maximums, so they were, they were all for increasing the maximums but keeping
the minimums."*

**The left read.** Raise the ceiling and keep the floor low and you have upzoned nothing that has
to happen — a parcel zoned R-50 can still be built at twelve units to the acre, or held. Minimum
densities are the provision that makes an upzone produce units rather than land value. That is
the part the development community is asking to remove, and the reason it is worth watching is
that it will be argued as a technical feasibility fix.

**The honest counter-argument, from `context/HOUSING-vancouver-wa.md` §3 and the README:** zoned
capacity is *not* the binding constraint on housing production here, and has not been. The city's
own record puts the near-term constraint on state gap financing — eight Vancouver projects with
over 630 units stalled for want of it, and zero Housing Trust Fund awards to Vancouver projects in
2025. Minimum densities matter for what gets built on land that is going to be built anyway; they
will not by themselves produce affordable units, and a brief that says otherwise is overselling.
Both things are true.

### Item 11A — the Commission's fallback is "listen to the developers' board"

Having failed to adopt item 11, the Commission passed **5–2**:

> *"Recommend to the County Council to take a serious look at the DEAB's recommendations as it
> pertains to the land use planning and their recommended changes."*

DEAB is the **Development Engineering Advisory Board** — Harroun described it on the record as
*"the leaders of the development community"* (p. 33). Three things about how that document reached
the Commission:

- DEAB approved its letter **"a few hours before"** the hearing (p. 30) and it arrived the
  afternoon of 3 September, by email from a Mr Golemo.
- Chair Johnson: *"it's very unprofessional for DEAB to drop something on us at the last minute,
  so they lose credibility through me by that"* (p. 33).
- Commissioner Barca: *"It's not realistic to get such a lengthy document hours before the hearing
  and expect us to have an opportunity to go through it"* (p. 32).

**The DEAB letter is not in the published work session packet.** The Council is being formally
recommended to take a serious look at a document that is not in front of the public, roughly 7–8
pages plus recommendations by Harroun's count, closer to 20 by Barca's. It should be requested.

### The farmland TDR: the enforceable version lost, and it lost last time too

The Land Use Element (item 33) passed **5–2 as presented**, keeping staff's existing policy
1.1.24. Commissioner LeDuc Montgomery's replacement — a countywide TDR program **operational by
31 December 2027**, an **urban holding designation** on de-designated land until TDR requirements
are met, **at minimum one acre permanently conserved for each acre converted**, and a fallback to
purchased development rights or permanent easements if the program is not standing up — failed
**2–5** (Barca and LeDuc Montgomery aye).

Two details that make this more than a losing amendment:

1. **The de-designations were voted first.** Item 33 was approved at the top of the evening;
   Montgomery's amendment came back and failed *after* items 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 28 — every UGA
   expansion onto designated agricultural resource land — had already passed. By then the
   Commission had approved the conversions and declined the mechanism that was supposed to offset
   them.
2. **The Council stripped this once already.** Chair Johnson, on the record (p. 56):
   *"I was concerned with why wasn't the TDR, like why did that get removed when we did push that
   forward, it wasn't staff, it was the Councilors said, no, they don't want that."* Montgomery
   (p. 15): last round *"the Council agreed with the de-designation but took out our
   recommendation about tying it to a TDR."*

So the live question on 13 October is not whether the Council accepts Montgomery's language — the
Commission did not send it — but whether it keeps even the weaker 1.1.24 that did pass 5–2. The
precedent is that it did not.

The agricultural de-designations themselves passed narrowly: Camas east 4–3, La Center north 5–2,
La Center southwest 4–3, Ridgefield north 4–3, Ridgefield southwest (the Maul property) 5–2, La
Center/Clark PUD 5-acre parcel 7–0. Barca and LeDuc Montgomery voted no on all of them, having
said in advance they would, for the stated reason that without no-net-loss the de-designations are
not defensible under the GMA.

### The Capital Facilities Plan passed 5–2 over a concurrency objection

Commissioner Barca, voting no on Chapter 6 (p. 62):

> *"many of our projects from our previous comp plan have been stacked on to the concurrency on
> this comp plan, the half a billion dollar transportation package I don't believe is realistic and
> it has the appearance that Public Works has been approving projects that would not be passing in
> the context of the test of concurrency."*

LeDuc Montgomery agreed, citing *"a lot of testimony from folks about concerns of new development

2–5; the motion to approve then passed 5–2. **The half-billion figure is Barca's characterisation
in deliberation, not a figure I have verified against Appendix G** — see "What I could not check".

## Where something genuinely good happened

### The Manufactured Home Park Overlay passed 7–0, and residents are why

Item 29 creates a **Mobile Home Park Overlay over 41 named parcels** of existing manufactured home
parks in the unincorporated Vancouver UGA, subject to new development regulations. Item 68
(*40.250.120 Manufactured Home Park Overlay*) and item 74 (*40.260.140 MHP Standards and
Requirements*) carry the code. All passed; 74 passed 5–1 with one abstention, amended to include
**park models and tiny homes approved by Washington L&I** as replacement units, and to fold in
cottage development requirements rather than maintain a separate standard.

Commissioner Halbert read the purpose clause into the record (p. 49–50): intended to *"preserve
the existing manufactured home parks and protect long-term affordability, prevent the displacement
of existing residents… by limiting redevelopment of manufactured home parks while allowing the
continued repair, replacement and expansion of manufactured homes."* He added:

> *"if it wasn't for so many of the residents that were here two weeks ago, I don't think we would
> have looked into it as diligently as we did."*

Commissioner Fadness commended the three residents still in the room at the three-hour mark.

**Say this plainly: this is a win, it is the direct product of people turning up to a hearing, and
it is the thing on this agenda most worth defending through 13 October.** Halbert's own caveat is
the right one — *"maybe it doesn't prevent everything or protect them from everything that could
happen in the future, but it's certainly a step in the right direction."* An overlay limits
redevelopment; it does not fix rent, and it does not stop a park closing under state closure
procedures. The county's 14 September manufactured home park information session is the companion
event (`briefs/2026-09-14-clark-county-manufactured-home-parks.md`).

### Other items that landed well

- **Item 73** — *40.260.076 Emergency Shelters, Transitional Housing, Emergency Housing and
  Permanent Supportive Housing* — passed 7–0, in a block of twenty items with no discussion.
  I have the vote but **not the text**; the code language is not in this packet.
- **Item 70** — *40.260.060 Co-Living Housing* — passed 7–0.
- **Item 20** — a site-specific request to rezone three parcels from Community Commercial to UM
  R-36 was **denied** 5–2. That is a housing-capacity request refused; the reasons are in the
  minutes at p. 43–45 and I have not summarised them here.
- **Item 14** — mixed-use minimum non-residential share cut from 20% to 10% of a site, 7–0, which
  allows more housing on mixed-use land and feeds through to the Vacant Buildable Lands Model.

## Money and land

Nothing is appropriated at a work session. What moves here is land and future obligation:

- **Agricultural resource land de-designated**, across Camas, La Center and Ridgefield UGA
  expansions — acreages are in the FEIS and the decision table's map exhibits, which are not in
  this packet.
- **41 parcels** brought under the manufactured home park overlay.
- **20 parcels** lose the Freight Rail Dependent Use Overlay (item 25), because SB 5820 passed
  30 March 2026 and Clark County no longer qualifies. Council-directed at Council Time on 3 June.

  fees at items 92–100 are the ones being heard separately on 15 September — including **Woodland
  School District, where the Commission voted 4–3 to DENY** on 16 July.
