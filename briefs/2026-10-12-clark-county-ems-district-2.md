## Act on this

Nothing here needs a body in the room, but there is one clear point to make if you are making it.

### Separate Business 1 and 3 — AMR's 2027 rates, and the contract amendment

**The rates** (staff report, 238371). They take effect 1 January 2027 and must be filed with the
Insurance Commissioner by 1 November:

| 911 rate (with exclusivity) | 2026 | 2027 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| ALS emergency base | $2,647.55 | **$2,912.31** | +10% |
| BLS emergency base | $2,229.52 | $2,452.47 | +10% |
| Mileage, per loaded mile | $43.61 | $47.97 | +10% |
| Standby, per hour | $205 | $225.50 | +10% |

- **The contract caps the increase at 10% and uses August Seattle CPI-U as the guide.** That CPI-U was
  **4.4%**. AMR says its costs rose **13.7%**. Its "Cost increase support — 2026 forecast vs. 2025
  actuals" page is an image, and the packet text carries no figures from it, so **the 13.7% cannot be
  checked from what is published**. The Admin Board says it *"evaluated the cost detail by category"*
  and *"feels the increases are reasonable"*.
- **Who pays.** The staff report says the increase *"will be felt most by 16.8% of the payors in the
  non-contracted insurance and private pay/self-pay groups"*. Medicare and Medicaid pay fixed rates, so
  the full increase lands on the uninsured and the out-of-network. Nobody chooses to need an ambulance.
- **The comparison table is the staff's own.** EMSD2's 2027 ALS rate of $2,912.31 now sits **above the
  City of Vancouver's subsidised $2,895.07**. Camas is $1,500. Portland-area and Spokane rates are much
  higher.
- **The "budget implications: none" line** is true only for the district. The cost falls on patients.

**The amendment** (238381) adds a fourth condition that excuses AMR from response-time fines:
*"PSAP provides an incorrect scene address to Contractor"*. On its face that is fair; a crew sent to the
wrong address should not be fined. Two points are worth noting. In Q2 the district **denied** an
"Access: Address Change" exemption request (dashboard, 17 June 2026), so this writes into the contract
what the district had been refusing. And the amendment moves the CPI reference month from August to
July and the rate-methodology meeting from September to August, which is earlier review. The amendment
also updates the response-time fines (now $7,194–$14,387 per zone per quarter) for inflation.

**The left read.** The rate increase is permitted by a contract the district signed, and the Admin
Board's comparison is honest. The weak point is that a 10% increase, the maximum, is justified by a
cost table the public cannot read. **Ask:** publish AMR's cost breakdown before approving the maximum;
state what AMR's charity-care or self-pay discount policy is (the packet does not say); and attach
the "explore ways to reduce costs" work the Admin Board promises for next year to something with a
date.

**How to comment.** The agenda puts "Public Comment" under "Other", **after Separate Business**, so
spoken comment comes after the votes. Raise a hand with \*3 on WebEx. No written-comment address is
given for EMSD2; the County's general form at https://clark.wa.gov/councilors/public-comment is
linked from the listing, but whether EMSD2 board members receive what is sent there is **not known**.
