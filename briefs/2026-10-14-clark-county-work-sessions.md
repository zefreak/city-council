## Watch

### 2027–2032 Transportation Improvement Program and 2027 Annual Construction Program (doc 238221)

- **$260.8M over six years** (slide 7): urban road projects $110.7M (43%); Sidewalk/ADA $47.9M (18%);
  bridges $42.6M; preservation $30.5M; safety $23.7M. The 2027 programme is **$38.9M**. Sources: Road
  Fund 36%, grants 29% (only $36.7M secured), traffic impact fees 18%, REET2 14%.
- **How projects are ranked** (slide 6): safety 33%, economic development 29%, mobility 24%.
  **Multimodal access, meaning bike, transit and trails, is worth 6 points.** Support for economic
  development alone is worth 20. Concurrency points go to *"de-facto moratorium and moratorium
  areas"*, so road capacity is scored to unlock development. That is a choice built into the formula,
  and the place to contest it is the 3 November hearing. It is too late to do so for this year's list.
- **Credit where due:** sidewalk and ADA money is close to a fifth of the plan, and the 2025–26
  completions include the NE 68th St and Highway 99 sidewalks.
- Risks named by staff: a *"diminishing Road Fund available for capital"* and volatile impact-fee
  revenue (slide 21).

### Parks — fees, free days and the 2027–2032 Capital Improvement Plan (docs 238216, 238211)

- **CIP: $89.8M over six years**, $22.2M in 2027. Legacy Lands acquisition is the largest element
  (27.6%), followed by new development, enhancements and major maintenance. Park impact fees fund 39% of
  2027.
- **The sentence that matters** (draft CIP): *"Parks and Nature is **purposefully slowing new park
  development** to avoid overburden of our operations team."* That is despite the PROS plan finding
  *"deficiencies across all categories"* of level of service. The County has land and capital money
  for parks but **not the operating money to run them**. That is the case for the regional levy,
  which the deck schedules for a *"Potential Regional Levy Vote (Q3)"* in 2027 (slide 4). It matches
  the countywide parks district flagged on 23 September.
- **Fees for 2027** (slide 5): the processing fee doubles, $25 → $50; site visits $50 → $75;
  early opening $20 → $75 an hour; pre-event maintenance $35 → $75 an hour. **Vendors at events pay
  "$50 or 10% of gross"**, and for-profit events move to revenue-sharing. Event fees themselves are
  unchanged. The vendor take hits small community and cultural festivals hardest. Vancouver's own
  parks charge vendors nothing (same slide).
- **Good:** the major-maintenance scoring rubric gives points for impact on people of colour, children,
  over-65s and households below the poverty line (CIP Appendix B).
- The 2027 free parking days calendar (slide 6) is an image and could not be read.

## Update, 9 October — the stormwater plan is now attached

The presentation was posted after the 7 October run (doc 238301, cached at
`data/clark-county/2026-10-14-ws/238301.txt`). **It is routine and mostly good.** It funds 32
projects for **$23.5M over 2027–2032**: $12.97M capital repair, $5.74M water quality (concentrated on
lower Salmon Creek tributaries and the Hwy 99 corridor), $1.63M habitat, $864K reforestation and $600K
property acquisition. Funding is the Clean Water Fund ($9.3M, 40%), REET 2 ($8.7M, 37%) and state
grants ($5.5M, 23%), plus an existing $3.4M state loan at 0.4%. The deck says the programme *"does not
construct stormwater facilities to support new development"*. It addresses existing development only,
so this is not a hidden growth subsidy. It is adopted by resolution, with **no public hearing**
(slide 3). The Planning Commission hearing is on 15 October and Council adoption on 3 November.
Nothing to act on. The earlier "flagged on title alone" note above is superseded.
