## Bottom line

**The written-comment record on Clark County's entire 20-year comprehensive plan closes at the
end of this hearing — tomorrow night.** After that the Planning Commission deliberates and votes
its recommendation on all 101 decision items, and the next opening is the County Council hearing

### What is actually being decided — 101 numbered items in four blocks

Staff have restructured the decision table (memo from Director Oliver Orjiako dated 3 September,
Exhibit 1 updated 27 August) so the Commission can "make motions in batches." That is the quiet
channel here: **a single motion can dispose of a whole block.** The blocks:

- **Map Amendments (1–29).** Items 1–10 are urban growth area changes at Battle Ground, Camas,
  La Center, Ridgefield and Yacolt. **Five of them de-designate agricultural resource land** —
  Camas east (item 5), La Center north and southwest (6, 7), Ridgefield north and southwest (8, 9,
  the latter "referred to as the Maul property"). Items 11–15 are density changes *inside* the
  Vancouver UGA: item 11 collapses five residential and office-residential districts into three
  (R‑12→R‑24, R‑18/R‑22→R‑36, R‑30/R‑43→R‑50, and the OR equivalents), item 14 cuts the mixed-use
  minimum non-residential share from 20% to 10%, item 15 counts Highway 99 sub-area residential
  capacity as mixed use in the buildable-lands model. Items 16–29 are site-specific requests.
- **Comprehensive Plan chapters and appendices (30–61)** — including Chapter 2, the Housing
  Element (item 34).
- **Code amendments (62–91)** — Title 40. Includes **40.250.120 Manufactured Home Park Overlay**
  (item 68), 40.260.140 MHP standards (74), 40.260.060 Co-Living Housing (70), 40.260.076
  Emergency Shelters / Transitional / Emergency / Permanent Supportive Housing (73), 40.340.010
  Parking (80), and the impact-fee mechanics at 40.620–40.630 (86–91).
- **Impact fees (92–101)** — school and fire fees, on which the Commission *already voted* on
  16 July and the table records "no additional action is required."

**The left-critical read on the map block.** Items 1–10 and items 11–15 pull in opposite
directions and will likely be voted in the same sitting. The expansions convert farmland at the
edge; the upzones add capacity inside the existing Vancouver UGA at up to R‑50. If capacity is the
justification for the expansions, the upzones are the argument against them — that is the pairing
to name in testimony, with the numbers from Appendix K (Density Targets) and Exhibit 4 (Memo on
Allocation and Capacity), both linked from the Planning Commission page. Item 14's cut to the
mixed-use non-residential minimum is the one most likely to pass without comment and it changes
what "mixed use" means countywide.

### The manufactured home park overlay — item 68, and it is largely good

This is the item worth supporting, with two specific fixes. Sourced from the consultant memo
(Elizabeth Decker, JET Planning, 22 June 2026, cached at
`data/clark-county/comp-plan/mhp-memo.txt`) and the 15 July 2026 draft code
(`proposed-40.250.120-mhp-overlay.txt`).

**The scale.** Clark County has **34 manufactured home parks with 2,033 homes**. 25 parks (74%),
holding 81% of the homes, sit inside urban growth areas — mostly the Vancouver UGA. Fewer than
half the parks (16) are in zones that currently permit them; the rest are nonconforming. Average
park density is 6.8 homes per gross acre against a proposed R-zone maximum of 24–36, which is
precisely why the land is worth more empty. Residents own the home and rent the ground, and
manufactured homes are difficult or impossible to move — the memo's own framing, and it is right.

**What the overlay does.** Permits only manufactured home parks, plus transitional housing,
permanent supportive housing, family day care, adult family homes and ordinary residential
accessories. Overlay rather than base zone, so existing base zoning survives and no spot-zoning
problem is created. A park owner wanting to redevelop must apply for a **use exception** and
demonstrate no reasonable use or that permitted uses are "not economically viable." Council
declared a moratorium on park redevelopment in **August 2025, renewed through February 2027**, to
buy time for exactly this. Case law from Tumwater and Kenmore establishes the tool as lawful.

**Two things to say at the hearing.**

1. **Subsection C.2 has no affordability standard in it.** The draft permits "Residential uses
   permitted in the base zone, if developed as affordable housing consistent with **subsection E**"
   — but subsection E is the Use Exception, and it contains no AMI threshold, no term and no
   definition of affordable. Vancouver's equivalent zone, which this is modelled on
   (VMC 20.410.120), specifies permanently affordable at 60% AMI or below. Either the
   cross-reference is wrong or the standard was never drafted. **Ask staff which, and ask for
   60% AMI and a term to be written in.** As it stands the clause is a door into the overlay with
   no lock on it.
2. **Use exception E.2 — "not economically viable due to the property's location, site conditions,
   or surrounding development" — is the escape hatch**, and it is the argument every owner of a
   6.8-unit-per-acre park on land zoned for 24–36 will make. The memo concedes the point: advocates
   describe this step as "slowing redevelopment pressures, though not directly boosting MHP
   viability." E.3 lets an owner rezone by proposing affordable housing with "relocation assistance,
   right to return policies… **or other assistance**" — no floor, no standard, no definition.
   Bellingham's version, per the memo's own case-study table, carries local provisions for notice,
   opportunity to purchase and relocation assistance. Clark County's does not. That is the
   concrete ask.

**Rural parks get nothing**, deliberately. The memo (§IV.A) says rural parks "face less
redevelopment pressure as nonconforming uses" and "should retain their existing zoning." That
reasoning is defensible on redevelopment risk, but the same memo notes nonconforming status
means "no expansion such as additional dwellings or modifications beyond maintenance and repairs
are permitted, which can limit MHP flexibility and future viability" (UDC 40.530.010.F.5). The
9 rural parks are protected from redevelopment by being frozen. That is a different thing from
being preserved, and it is worth putting on the record.

**Also decided elsewhere, not here:** the memo recommends **against** a transfer-of-development-
rights programme (§IV.G) and notes that **no Clark County park has converted to resident or
nonprofit ownership**, despite resident organising at four parks. State TOPO rights exist
(RCW 59.20.305, .325) and residents have found the 70-day window unworkable against private
investors. County support for conversions — funds, not just zoning — is not in this package.

## Money and land

No dollar figure attaches to this hearing directly. What moves is land: five agricultural
de-designations and UGA expansions at four cities, against upzones inside the Vancouver UGA. The
impact-fee block (92–101) carries real money but was already recommended on 16 July — see the
15 September Council brief.
