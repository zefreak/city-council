### Item 7 — Public hearing: resolution opposing IL26-001 and IL26-638

Staff report 196-26, pp. 150–151. Resolution, pp. 152–154. Initiative texts, pp. 155–169.

**What it does.** One operative section: *"The City Council hereby expresses its opposition to
Initiative No. IL26-001 and Initiative No. IL26-638 on the November 3, 2026 General Election
Ballot."* It spends no money and binds no one. On 14 September the Council asked staff to bring it
back. The staff report lists no advantages and no challenges *"in the interest of remaining
neutral."*

**Why it is a hearing.** RCW 42.17A.555 generally bars using public facilities to support or oppose
a ballot measure. It exempts a vote by an elected body at an open meeting, provided the notice names
the measure and the public gets *"an approximately equal opportunity for the expression of an
opposing view."* So the initiatives' supporters are guaranteed a slot. *I could not cache the
statute text this run (leg.wa.gov returned a script-rendered page). The wording quoted is from
memory of the statute and should be checked before anyone quotes it.*

**The resolution is worth supporting.** The case against IL26-001 is laid out in the 14 September
brief (`briefs/2026-09-14-vancouver-city-council.md` §3). In short, it strikes:

- the current statute's protected-class anti-discrimination right;
- the right to file a bullying complaint on a child's behalf;
- the language-access notice;
- the records shield where a parent is a criminal defendant and the child is the named victim.

**Before you testify, check which recitals the text supports.** Some match the initiative closely.
Others go further than it does. Opponents at the podium will go for the weakest one.

- **Well supported.** The recitals on abuse victims' records (pp. 152–153). The resolution says the
  initiative covers only *"active criminal investigation"* and not cases where parents are *"charged
  with or convicted"*. That matches the deletion found on 14 September: the protection for a parent
  who is a criminal defendant with the child as victim is gone. Social work and disciplinary records
  also drop out of the shield.
- **Inference, not text.** *"Initiative No. IL26–001 would also require schools to 'out' LGBTQ+ kids"*
  (p. 153). Nothing in IL26-001 mentions gender identity, names or pronouns, apart from the
  anti-discrimination list it strikes. The argument runs through its broad access to *"any other
  student-specific files"* and the notification rights. That is a reasonable reading, but it is not
  a clause you can point to.
- **Overstated.** *"Only girls will be subjected to the medical sex certification and **genital
  exams**"* (p. 153). IL26-638 §3(2) requires a provider to verify sex *"relying only on …
  reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup, or normal endogenously produced testosterone levels."*
  Anatomy is one of three routes the provider may use. The initiative does not mandate a genital
  exam. The universal-burden point is the stronger one, and it is textual: *every* student who
  enters female-only athletics needs the certification, not only trans students.

**Practical:** register by noon to testify remotely, or by 6:30pm in person. A short, specific
testimony citing the struck text will carry further than a recital of the resolution.

### Item 5 (consent) — HB 1491 affordability ordinance: first reading now, hearing 12 October

Staff report 195-26, pp. 109–110. Presentation, pp. 111–120 (dated for 12 October). Ordinance,
pp. 121–126. Background: `briefs/2026-09-08-vancouver-planning-commission.md` and
`context/HOUSING-vancouver-wa.md` §32c.

**What it does.** It amends two sections of the new Title 20, adopted by Ord. M-4533.

- **VMC 20.530.040(C), the binding part.** On "impacted" parcels within a quarter-mile walkshed of
  the 51 BRT stations, new housing must carry a **50-year covenant** with one of: 10% of units at
  60% AMI; 20% at 80% AMI; or 10% at 80% AMI where at least 10% of units have two or more bedrooms
  (p. 123). Developments that claim an MFTE and meet the threshold may have transportation impact
  fees cut by 50%.
- **VMC 20.450.030(I)(2), the low-income impact-fee waiver.** It adds an exception to the current bar
  on combining an impact-fee waiver with an MFTE: *"unless located within a bus station area"*
  (p. 122). I cannot confirm from the PDF that this clause is the insertion. The text extract carries
  no redline marks, and the online code still shows the pre-M-4533 numbering. But the clause only
  makes sense as an addition. If it is one, it lets station-area affordable projects stack the two
  incentives, and that is good.

**The map question from 8 September is now answered, and the answer is "administrative".** The
ordinance says the *"HB 1491 Impacted and Exempt Parcels map … is available at the Community
Development Department Permit Center, and the Department website"* (p. 123). The map is referenced
but not adopted. It has no exhibit, no parcel list and no ordinance number. Staff chose which parcels
fill the statutory 25% exemption, and they picked those *"most likely to redevelop"* (14 August memo).
**That choice sits on a webpage staff can change without a vote.** That is the ask for 12 October:
**adopt the impacted/exempt parcel list as an exhibit to the ordinance**, so any change to it needs
an ordinance.

**Staff's case against the law deserves a fair hearing.** The staff report calls HB 1491 an
inclusionary mandate *"not offset by the incentives"* that *"will likely discourage development in
the areas near transit where it is desired"* (p. 110). The presentation says it *"will likely result
in less housing production on impacted properties"* (p. 118). That fits the context file. The City's
own 2025 Housing Report says completions ran at under half of target, and rents are forecast to grow
below inflation (§32ca). In that market a 50-year set-aside can stop a marginal project being built.
**The counter:** the City has not tried the law. Staff say *"no local development proposals or
inquiries have been submitted in the impacted properties"* since the state model code took effect in
June (p. 110). Three months with no applications shows nothing about the mandate either way. Yet the
exemption design already shields the parcels where projects are most likely to come forward.

**Drafting errors to point out before a second reading:**

- The recital says the Council held its hearing on *"October 12, **2025**"* (p. 121).
- *"codified under RCW. and"*: the RCW citation is left blank (p. 121).
- **No severability clause and no Section 3.** The title says *"providing for severability"*. The text
  goes from Section 2 to *"Section 4. Effective date"* (p. 124).
- **The deadline has changed between documents.** The staff report says compliance was due *"June 30,
  2026, six months after the date of its required Comprehensive Plan update adoption"* (p. 110). The
  14 August memo to the Planning Commission gave it as 31 December 2025 (8 September brief). One of
  them is wrong, or they measure different things.

Effective **11 November 2026** (p. 124). The Planning Commission voted **4–0, "with no comments
received"** (p. 109). Nobody has testified on this at any stage.

### Budget work session: the children-and-families tax is withdrawn, and a peer-employment contract is set to end

Presentation, pp. 18–30. City Manager memo of 25 September, pp. 31–35. FTE appendix, pp. 36–42.
Youth representation proposal, pp. 43–44. The recommended budget publishes **1 October**.

1. **The children-and-families sales tax is withdrawn** (memo, p. 33). On 21 September this project
   asked whether paying rent and four detectives at the Children's Justice Center counts as an
   *"additional"* service under ESHB 2442 §202. The Council asked the same question. The memo:
   *"The Council had questions about the allowability of the use of this sales tax to fund existing
   programming at the Children's Justice Center and had overall concerns about the financial burden
   of a new tax."* Staff will return with *"a more comprehensive legal analysis of allowable uses."*
   Without it, the out-year deficit has *"worsened slightly"*.
2. **Talkin' Trash is assumed to end in June 2027.** The FTE appendix adds two Utility Maintenance
   Workers (PWOM01028). They are *"tasked to work with the current Homeless Encampment Response
   Coordinator and HART"*, picking up *"encampment-originated litter"* and *"assisting scheduled
   HART-coordinated cleanup efforts."* They are funded from the Solid Waste Utility *"under the
   assumption that the City's contract with SHARE for the Talkin' Trash program is discontinued in
   June 2027"* (p. 42). SHARE's own page says the programme *"provides employment opportunities for
   individuals who are experiencing housing instability or transitioning from living outdoors"*, with
   case management and job placement for the crew. It also says the crew *"no longer responds
   directly to outdoor encampments."* So the City would swap a peer-employment programme for
   municipal workers attached to encampment cleanups. **This sits in a staffing appendix and is not
   mentioned in the deck or the memo.** It costs about $73,025 per worker in 2027 (nine months) and
   $102,027 in 2028. What SHARE's contract costs, and how many people it employs, is not in the
   packet.
3. **Homeless outreach drops from 9 positions to 1.** Nine limited-term homeless outreach posts run
   in 2027, funded by a Lynch Family Fund grant: six extended, two new, plus a shelter-operations
   coordinator. Only one runs into 2028. That is $1.12M in 2027 against $135K in 2028 (p. 39). **The
   2028 drop follows the grant ending; it is not a decision about need.** Ask now what happens to
   outreach in 2028.
4. **Police get 23 of the 41 new regular positions** (p. 24): 16 officers on the HB 2015 grant, which
   *"has not yet been awarded"* (p. 23), plus Prop 5 support staff. The memo checks the new 0.1%
   public safety sales tax (RCW 82.14.345) against the Cultural Arts Tax, which has the same 0.1%
   rate and is projected at **$6.97M for 2026**. The safety tax is budgeted at **$6.74M (2027) and
   $7.05M (2028)**, with $6.5M allocated in 2028 (p. 32). That is the public safety tax, **not** the
   TBD transportation tax, which is also 0.1%.
5. **The youth coordinator is still out, and the free alternative is not aimed at youth.** The
   $137,000/yr coordinator is *"not included"* (p. 33). The cost-neutral alternative puts *"young
   adults (ages 20–30)"* on two-thirds of boards and commissions by 2029 (p. 43). The forum on
   28 September was scheduled so that **high school students** could come. **Nobody under 20 is
   eligible under the alternative.** It is a fair point to make at Community Communication, and again
   at the 12 October operating workshop.
6. **Utility tax 28.90% → 28.99%**, raising **$218K** to fund one Climate Action Framework position
   (p. 34). Small, and the money is earmarked for something good.

### Title 19 scofflaw programme: $100 of parking fines becomes $643 and a towed car

Presentation, pp. 46–58. Draft VMC 19.15, pp. 59–64. **Consent on 9 November and a public hearing on
16 November**, the same night as the budget hearing (p. 58).

**What it does.** A vehicle with **four or more parking tickets unpaid after 45 days** goes on a
Scofflaw List, after a mailed notice with at least 30 days to respond. A listed vehicle can be booted,
or fitted with a windshield device. **After two business days it can be impounded** (§§19.15.010–.030).

**Staff's own worked example (p. 55)** is four $25 tickets:

| | |
|---|---|
| Fines | $100 |
| Late fees | $100 |
| Collections fee "17.5%" | $42.42 |
| Interest "12%" | $29.09 |
| Scofflaw fee | $25 |
| Boot fee | $50 |
| Impound | $297 |
| **Total** | **$643.52** |

**The "17.5%" is 21.2% of the debt.** $42.42 is 17.5% of $242.42. That is the debt *plus the fee
itself*, so the fee is charged on itself. The 12% interest is charged on the same grossed-up sum, and
it is a full year of it. The slide's "Fines and Fees to CoV, $271.51" also fails to reconcile: the
City's own lines (fines, late fees, scofflaw and boot fees) sum to **$275.00**. These are small sums,
but they are the slide the Council is using to judge proportionality, and they should be right.

**The left-critical read.** Four parking tickets is not much for someone who parks on the street
because they have no driveway, or who lives in their vehicle. The draft makes **no provision for
ability to pay, no exemption for vehicles used as residences, and no mention of either in the
deck.** I searched pp. 46–64 for "resid", "homeless", "income" and "waiv": nothing beyond the
non-resident compact. The draft does offer payment plans and a hearing before listing. That is a real
safeguard, and the code as drafted shows care. **But once a car is booted, a payment plan no longer
helps.** §19.15.020(G) releases the boot only on payment *in full*. Impounding a car someone sleeps
in makes them unsheltered that day.

**Staff's case, fairly stated.** Only **52%** of citations are paid within 45 days, against 81% within
30 days when this was last proposed in 2016 (p. 53). Staff say the emphasis is *"on increasing
voluntary compliance, not boot revenue"* and offer metrics to match (p. 57). Seattle's and Spokane's
early figures show most of the effect at the grace-period stage, not at the boot (p. 56).

**Ask for:** an ability-to-pay waiver or reduction; payment plans that stay available after
immobilisation; an exemption or diversion route for vehicles used as dwellings, tied to the Safe
Parking programme; and the slide's arithmetic corrected. **9 November is consent,** so get this in at
Community Communication on 5 October, or before 9 November.

## Money and land

- **SE 1st St vacation, $259,103.** The $13/sq ft is not an appraisal. It is the unit price the City
  used in **October 2023** to credit the same owner Transportation Impact Fees for land dedicated to
  the road project (p. 97). Using one price for both directions keeps the City consistent. It also
  means three years of land appreciation in a fast-growing employment area are not counted. Asking
  for 100% compensation is the right call, and RCW 35.79.030 does not require any. The question is
  whether a 2023 negotiated figure is the right base.
- **Section 30 pump station, $1.26M from the wastewater capital budget.** The City is building
  wastewater capacity to serve private development, as the 2019 HP Inc. development agreement
  requires. The staff report does not say whether connection charges or a latecomer agreement
  recover the cost from the parcels served. If they do not, ratepayers carry it. Worth asking.
  CTC Section 30 LLC, the applicant in item 4, owns land in the same area.

  is named. This is one to watch for a purchase on a later consent agenda.

## Correction, 7 October — the family-sized option

This brief described HB 1491's third option as 10% at 80% AMI where *"at least 10% of units have two or
more bedrooms."* That is the City ordinance's wording, and **it is not the law.** 3SHB 1491 §3(7)(a)(ii)
requires family-sized units *"with more than two bedrooms"*, which means three or more. The Title 20
ordinance is therefore looser than the statute it implements. See `briefs/2026-10-12-vancouver-city-council.md`.
