## Bottom line

**Item 9 remains the most consequential housing item on any agenda in this cycle, and the city is
holding the hearing without publishing the code text.** The packet shows staff have exempted the
entire downtown *plus* a discretionary 25% of remaining parcels chosen as "**most likely to
redevelop**" — that is, the exemption was aimed at precisely the parcels where the affordability
mandate would otherwise have applied to real projects. The City Council hearing to adopt is
**12 October**.

## Act on this

### Item 9 — Public Hearing: Title 20 update, affordability requirements for HB 1491 compliance

Presenter: Bryan Snodgrass, Principal Planner. Sources: memo "Zoning Code changes to implement
HB 1491 housing affordability mandates — Memo in Lieu of Workshop," 14 August 2026, packet pp.
38–42; Exhibit A station area and exemption maps, pp. 43–53; presentation, pp. 54–62.

**What HB 1491 (2025) requires.** Within a quarter-mile *walkshed* of Bus Rapid Transit stations:
FAR averaging at least 2.5 (plus 1.5 more for all-affordable buildings, and a mass timber
incentive), no off-street parking requirements, a 50% impact fee reduction for projects using the
20-year MFTE, a 20-year MFTE offer, and **50-year affordability** — 10% of units at 60% AMI, or 20%
at 80% AMI, or 10% at 80% AMI with at least a tenth having two or more bedrooms.

**Two constraints in the statute that the earlier brief did not carry**, both from the memo:

- The affordability "must be provided in **each individual building** and do not allow for a master
  planned community to provide all the affordability for a larger development in one or some of the
  buildings." Staff argue this challenges feasibility because standalone affordable housing has
  better financing options than mixed-income buildings. That is a real point and it cuts both ways:
  per-building requirements are also what prevents affordable units being concentrated in the worst
  building on the worst corner of a site.
- Cities may exempt parcels zoned on **1 January 2025** at FARs meeting the new thresholds, **and**
  a further **25% of otherwise non-exempt parcels** in each station area, if they allow an
  additional 0.5 FAR (3.0 total).

**What staff have done with that discretion.** The memo, p.3:

> "Staff analysis indicates that all parcels located in the Vancouver City Center Vision subarea and
> associated CX Plan District in the previous zoning code met the minimum FAR threshold and are thus
> exempt… In addition, staff identified parcels making up to 25% of otherwise non-exempt station
> area that are **anticipated as most likely to redevelop** and added them to the exemption list."

**That sentence is the finding.** The statutory 25% is discretionary — the city could have applied
it anywhere, or not at all. Staff applied it to the parcels most likely to actually be built on.
The practical effect is that the affordability mandate is heaviest where development is least
likely and lightest where it is most likely. Staff would say that is the point: their stated view,
in the same memo, is that HB 1491 is "effectively a **partially funded inclusionary zoning
requirement**" — 50 years of affordability offset by at most 20 years of MFTE — that "may
discourage residential or mixed-use development close to transit, pushing development activity away
from our most targeted growth areas."

That argument deserves engaging rather than dismissing, but note what it is: an argument about
**volume**, deployed against a statute aimed at **depth and tenure**. Trading one for the other is a
policy choice, not an arithmetic result. And the choice has been made administratively — the
exemption map, which is where the choice lives, **is not proposed to be codified**. The memo says
staff "does not envision codifying this map, but will post it to the City's website"; the
presentation (p.57) says instead that "detailed mapping of non-exempt properties [is] to be
referenced and linked in code." **Those two statements are not the same thing** and the difference
matters — a map referenced in code changes by ordinance, a map posted on a website changes by
staff decision. Ask which it is.

**The questions to put on the record.** Which parcels are inside the 25%, and on what evidence of
"most likely to redevelop"; whether the exemption map is codified or administrative; whether the
downtown exemption removes the area where transit-adjacent development would otherwise
concentrate; and whether the 50-year term survives intact.

**A drafting discrepancy worth naming.** The memo says the affordability language goes in
**VMC 20.410** (Zoning Districts) and the impact fee reduction in **VMC 20.450**. The staff
presentation's sample motion (p.59) asks the Commission to "Amend Vancouver Municipal Code (VMC)
**20.530** to include minimum housing affordability thresholds." Three different section numbers
across two documents in the same packet. Without the staff report there is no way to tell which is
right, and the Commission is being asked to vote a motion citing a section the memo does not
mention.

**Vancouver is out of compliance right now.** The memo, p.2: the city's deadline was
**31 December 2025**; "we didn't adopt until June 1, 2026." Commerce has finalised a **state model
code that preempts local zoning for non-compliant jurisdictions**, and staff "understand, and are
communicating with applicants, that **the state model code applies to impacted, non-exempt parcels
until the City adds the affordability components to its code**." Vancouver is also "the only city
currently subject to HB 1491." So the practical question at the hearing is not whether there is an
affordability requirement near BRT — there is one today, the state's — but whether Vancouver's
replaces it with something narrower.

Separately, because Vancouver's code does not use FAR at all, the city has asked Commerce for a
**Substantially Similar Determination** translating its standards into FAR equivalents. If that is
refused, the rest of the compliance case changes. It has not been decided.

**The station geography**, from the Exhibit A methodology (p.43): 51 BRT stations along the C-TRAN
Vine, with eastbound and westbound platforms treated as separate stations unless within 200 feet,
which consolidates them to **41 station points**. Quarter-mile walksheds were generated on the
pedestrian network with ArcGIS Network Analyst, not as straight-line buffers, and overlapping
parcels were assigned to a single station using Thiessen polygons. Maps are grouped into five areas,
A–E, each with a "stations" and an "exempt status" sheet.

### Item 8 — Public Hearing: Evergreen Public Schools capital facilities plan, and a school impact fee of $0

Staff report dated 8 September, packet pp. 9–11; Evergreen CFP 2026-2032, pp. 14–36; covering
letter from LeAnne Bremer, Miller Nash, 13 May 2026, pp. 12–13.

**The substance is a fee schedule change**, which the agenda title does not say. Table 36 of the
Comprehensive Plan (p.283) is amended:

| District | Single family | Multi-family |
|---|---|---|
| Battle Ground | $10,760 | $3,845 |
| Camas | $6,650 | $6,650 |
| **Evergreen** | **$6,432 → $0** | **$3,753 → $0** |
| Vancouver | $2,876 | $2,486 |

Evergreen's board adopted the CFP on 10 June 2025; it concludes there is adequate capacity at all
grade levels through the six-year window (1 January 2026 – 31 December 2032) and that enrollment
will decline. The district's counsel wrote to the City and County on 13 May 2026 that "upon
adoption, the District will **not be entitled** to collect school impact fees." Vancouver City
Council already passed an interim control ordinance and moratorium on Evergreen fee collection on
**8 June 2026**, affirmed at a hearing on **3 August 2026**. This item formalises it.

**This is legally compelled, not a giveaway.** Impact fees under RCW 82.02 may fund only capital
needed for *new* growth; with surplus capacity there is no eligible project, so a fee would be
unlawful. The GMA's once-a-year limit on comp plan amendments is met through the RCW 36.70A.130
exception for capital facilities amendments concurrent with a budget action.

**The question worth asking anyway.** The same packet (p.10) notes an August 26 Columbian article
on the district's "upcoming process for **consolidating existing facilities**," while the district's
own letter says the City is planning for "at least 38,000 additional housing units (about 44% more
than the City has now)" and that enrollment "is likely to gradually increase as new families move
into new and existing residential areas." So: capacity is surplus now because the district is
shrinking and about to consolidate buildings, and the growth that would fill them arrives inside the
20-year plan but outside the 6-year fee window. **Whatever capacity is then needed will be paid for
by levy or bond — by existing residents — rather than by the development that caused it.** The
district can update its CFP annually. On what trigger, and whether the City will ask for one, is the
question. Note also that a consolidation programme means school *closures*, which is its own thing
and is not before this Commission.

### Item 5 — Workshop: Proportional impact fees

Memo dated 8 September from Bryan Snodgrass — **an item-level attachment that is not in the packet
PDF**, cached at `data/vancouver/2026-09-08-pc/memo-proportional-impact-fees.pdf`. Workshops take no
formal action; this is where the framework is shaped before it is votable.

**Vancouver's current fees**, from the memo:

| Fee | Range |
|---|---|
| Park impact fee (PIF) | $6,130 single family / $4,480 multi-family, across three park districts |
| School impact fee (SIF) | $2,786 single family / $2,486 multi-family, Vancouver SD; Evergreen going to zero |
| Traffic impact fee (TIF) | $247–$439 **per vehicle trip**, across three districts |

**The Vancouver school fee is given as $2,786 here and as $2,876 in the item 8 staff report** —
two documents in the same packet, $90 apart. One is a transposition. Not resolved here; worth asking
which figure the Comprehensive Plan's Table 36 actually carries.

**What is proposed.** New state law requires residential impact fees to be proportional — by square
footage, bedrooms or trips — so smaller units pay less. Staff want to calibrate on **square
footage**, using a small number of brackets rather than a per-square-foot rate, illustrated as
0–1,500 / 1,500–2,500 / 2,500+ sq ft. **ADUs are statutorily capped at 50% of the fee on the main
unit.** Working with **ECOnorthwest**; parks and schools first, transportation last because trip
generation is already modelled.

**The design constraint to watch: staff intend it to be "revenue neutral."** That means every dollar
taken off small units is added to large ones. That is a genuinely progressive redistribution — small
units are the affordable ones — and it is worth supporting. But revenue neutrality is a choice, not a
requirement, and it is being set now, in a workshop, with no vote: the same reform could be used to
*raise* total capital funding, or to cut it. Also note the data question — staff are choosing between
the American Community Survey and the American Housing Survey, "the later likely most appropriate
because it has square footage information." The bracket boundaries are where the money is, and they
will be justified by whichever survey is picked.

**The interaction nobody is required to disclose.** HB 1491's mandated offsets include a **50%
impact fee reduction** for projects using the 20-year MFTE. A proportional redesign changes what
that 50% is calculated against. Items 5 and 9 appear separately on the agenda and are not
independent.

**Timeline:** follow-up Planning Commission workshop with specific recommendations anticipated
**13 October**; hearings "likely at the end of the year."
