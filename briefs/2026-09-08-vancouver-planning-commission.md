## Bottom line

**Item 9 is the most consequential housing item on any agenda in this cycle.** Vancouver is
deciding *how* to comply with HB 1491's affordability mandate near transit — and City staff have
already argued on the record that the law is "partially funded inclusionary zoning" and pursued a
carve-out. This hearing is where the size of that carve-out gets set.

## Act on this

### Item 9 — Public Hearing: Title 20 Update, affordability requirements for HB 1491 compliance

Presenter: Bryan Snodgrass, Principal Planner, Community Development.

**What HB 1491 (2025) requires.** Within a quarter-mile walkshed of Bus Rapid Transit stations,
**50-year affordability**: 10% of units at 60% AMI, or 20% at 80% AMI, or 10% at 80% AMI where at
least a tenth are two-bedroom or larger. Cities must offer offsets — a 20-year MFTE exemption, a
50% impact fee reduction, minimum FAR, density bonuses, no parking minimums.

**Why Vancouver specifically.** The city has **51 stations along two existing Vine BRT routes** —
one of very few jurisdictions outside Puget Sound with high-capacity transit. A law written with
Sound Transit light rail in mind lands unusually hard here, which is why the compliance choices
matter more than they would elsewhere.

**What staff have already said**, from the 10 August 2026 council deck *HB 1491 Code & MFTE
Changes* (Quinton and Ko), recorded at §32c of the housing analysis in `context/`:

> "HB 1491 is partially funded inclusionary zoning" — 50 years of required affordability against
> at most 20 years of offset. Staff argue the other incentives are *already available citywide*,
> so they offset nothing at the margin; that partially funded IZ lowers returns, **decreases
> overall production and directs development away from transit**; and that blanket requirements
> "undermine the City's redevelopment plans by discouraging mixed income master plan projects."

Staff's proposed approach is **exclusions**: areas already zoned with minimum FAR such as
downtown, plus roughly **25% of parcels near stations**.

**The questions worth asking at the hearing.** Which parcels land inside the exclusion and which
outside; whether "already available citywide" is a reason to weaken the mandate or a reason to
strengthen the offsets; whether the excluded downtown area is where transit-adjacent development
would otherwise concentrate; and whether the 50-year term survives intact in Vancouver's version.

Staff's production argument is not obviously wrong and should be engaged rather than dismissed —
but note it is an argument about *volume*, while HB 1491 is aimed at *tenure and depth*. Those are
different objectives, and trading one for the other is a choice, not an arithmetic result.

### Item 5 — Workshop: Proportional Impact Fees

Presenter: Bryan Snodgrass. Workshops take no formal action, but this is where the framework gets
shaped before it becomes votable. Directly connected to the sibling `impact-fees/` research.

Note the interaction with item 9: HB 1491's mandated offsets include a **50% impact fee
reduction**. A proportional-impact-fee redesign changes what that 50% is calculated against, so
these two items are not independent even though they appear separately on the agenda.

### Item 8 — Public Hearing: Updated Evergreen Public Schools Capital Facilities Plan

Presenter: Bryan Snodgrass. A district capital facilities plan is the legal basis for school
impact fees — adopting the plan is what allows the fee to be charged and sets its justification.
Takes public testimony.
