## The left-critical read

Three things are worth separating here, because they point different ways.

**a. A commission having competent counsel is not, in itself, bad.** A body that drafts policy and
negotiates contracts without legal advice makes expensive mistakes, and small public bodies are
routinely under-lawyered. The scope in the engagement letter is ordinary general-counsel work.

**b. The structure is the issue, not the lawyers.** No cap, no term, tenth-hour billing, travel
billed at full rate, and every phone call chargeable is the most permissive shape an hourly
agreement takes. Public bodies commonly cap these — a not-to-exceed figure per year, revisited on
renewal. This one has nothing to exceed. The Commission can terminate at will, which is a real
protection, but termination requires somebody to be watching the invoices, and no reporting
requirement to the Commission or to Council appears in either document.

**c. The "public development authority" line is the part I would push on.** The engagement letter
calls the Commission "a public development authority." The City of Vancouver's own website lists
its PDAs under Boards and Commissions as the **City Center Redevelopment Authority, Downtown
Redevelopment Authority, Public Facilities District Board and Transportation Benefit District** —
the Culture, Arts and Heritage Commission is not among them; it is described as a body that "works
with the Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services Department." Those two descriptions are not
compatible. Either the firm's letter is loose drafting, or the Commission's legal status is
something other than the advisory body the website describes.

It matters because a PDA is a separate legal entity that can hold money, contract, sue and be
sued — and an advisory commission is not. **Retaining independent general counsel, separate from
the City Attorney's Office, is what an entity with its own legal personality does.** I have not
resolved which characterisation is correct and I am not going to guess. It is a fair public
records question: *under what authority was the Commission chartered, and what is its legal status?*
