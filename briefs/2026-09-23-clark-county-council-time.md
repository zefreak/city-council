## Bottom line

**Item 5.1 is "TDR Discussion" — four words, no staff report, no attachment, eight days before the
Council opens the comprehensive plan hearing.** Transfer of Development Rights is the mechanism that
would have required a replacement acre of farmland for every acre converted by a UGA expansion. The
Planning Commission rejected the enforceable version **2–5** on 3 September, *after* approving the
de-designations it was meant to offset. On **17 September** the County told the Department of
Commerce it "intend[s] to work towards a TDR policy that is separate from urban growth expansion
efforts." This is where that gets shaped, at an informal meeting with no published materials and
comment limited to three minutes on an item nobody outside the building has seen.

## Act on this

**Item 5.1 — TDR Discussion.** Public comment opens after roll call and amendments, before old
business. Three minutes. Name the item number.

The thing to ask for is documentary, not rhetorical: **what enforcement mechanism replaces the urban
holding designation?** The failed 3 September amendment (full text in the comp plan Exhibit 1
Decision Table, item 33) made UGA expansions on de-designated agricultural land "subject to an urban
holding designation until applicable TDR requirements are satisfied" — you could not build until the
replacement acre was conserved. A TDR policy "separate from urban growth expansion efforts", as
promised to Commerce, is that policy with the linkage taken out. If there is no holding designation
and no substitute, the offset is voluntary.

Second ask: **a date and a deliverable.** The failed amendment set 31 December 2027 for a fully
operational programme, with conservation easements as the fallback. A discussion item with no
deadline attached is a discussion item.

See the 1 October hearing brief for the full record and the other three questions.

### Item 5.2 — Hispanic Heritage Month proclamation, added 9/21 at 8:00am

A late addition, posted two days before the meeting. Substantively uncontroversial and worth
supporting; noted here because **the mechanism** matters: Rules of Procedure §VII.P allows items to
be added to the Council Time agenda and posted as late as Friday noon, and this one went on the
Monday morning before a Wednesday meeting. The same channel that carries a proclamation carries
anything else. A revised Clark County agenda is worth re-reading, not skimming.

### Items 7.1 and 7.2 — two Parks work session requests

Both are **9-step planner forms**, the gate a topic passes through before a work session is
scheduled. Council approves or declines scheduling; there is no vote on substance and no public
comment at the work session itself, so this is the point at which to say anything.

**7.1 — 2027 Parks & Nature Fee Update and "Free Days" calendar** (Ross Hoover, Parks & Nature
Division Manager; Eric Christensen). Requested 11 September, desired Fall 2026, 30 minutes. What
Council would be asked to do at the end: *"Motion to approve the 2027 Parks 'Free Days' calendar and
provide direction on fee schedule; Adopt 2027 Fees."* The form names the tension itself — *"Balance
between revenue needs and equitable access"* — and records "Policy Implications: None", which is not
true of a fee schedule. **Park entry fees are a regressive charge on access to public land.** Clark
County implemented regional park fee increases in 2025; this would set 2027 rates and the no-fee
days that offset them. The number of free days is the whole equity question and the form does not
propose one.

**7.2 — Parks & Natural Areas Comprehensive Plan Update: draft Trails Chapter** (Ross Hoover, Evelyn
Ives, Jill Roszel; prepared by Jill Roszel, 14 September). Desired October. Council would give
"informal approval" of a draft Trails Chapter outline and a draft Regional Trails Map, authorising
staff and consultants to develop content and run community engagement. The form is honest about
where the argument will be: *"Potential differing views may arise around funding allocations, rural
vs. urban investment balance, or land acquisition needs."* Note the sequence — **Council gives
informal approval to the outline and map first, and community engagement happens afterwards.** If
the regional trails map is settled before engagement, engagement is consultation on a drawn line.
Ask for the draft map to be published before, not after, the work session.

Vancouver is separately funding a **Parks Comprehensive Plan Update** in its 2027-28 budget
(City Manager's recommended budget preview, 21 September). Two adjacent parks plans on parallel
tracks; nothing in either document says they are coordinated.

### Item 9.1 — executive session, pending litigation, 15 minutes, no after action

RCW 42.30.110(1)(i). One session this week. For scale: on **16 September** the Council went into
executive session at 1:25pm and came out at **3:40pm** — four separate sessions and eleven
consecutive extensions, all "no potential after action" (minutes, p. 2). That is two and a quarter
hours of a weekly public meeting spent in private with nothing reportable afterwards. It is lawful.
It is also most of the meeting.
