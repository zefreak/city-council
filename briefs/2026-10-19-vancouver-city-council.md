### Item 4 — Public hearing and final vote: MFTE changes under HB 1491 and HB 1494

Staff report 197-26. Deck: attachment 5150. Ordinance: attachment 5153. The substance is in
`briefs/2026-10-12-vancouver-city-council.md`, item 1, and is not repeated here.

**What changed since first reading: nothing.** With page furniture stripped, the attachment 5153 text
is identical to the first-reading ordinance in the 12 October packet. The deck is still the one dated
12 October.

1. **The tenant income-growth protection is still not in the code.** "150%" appears only in a
   recital (*"income has increased since initial eligibility up to 150% of AMI"*). The amended VMC 3.22
   never states it. The statute is self-executing: HB 1494 §2(9) lets the household stay eligible
   *"until their adjusted household income exceeds 150 percent of the established income limit"*.
   That is **150% of the income limit, not 150% of AMI**. For an 80% AMI unit it means about 120% of
   AMI, so the recital overstates it. **Ask for the statutory wording to be written into VMC 3.22.**
2. **The family-sized option still reads "at least 10% of the restricted units are family sized units
   with more than two bedrooms"** (VMC 3.22.050(B)(2), attachment 5153, line 635 of the text). That is
   10% of a 10% set-aside, about 1% of the building. The statute's *"10 percent of the units"* most
   naturally means all units. **That is my reading of the statute, not a settled point**, and Commerce
   guidance would decide it. The deck says something different again: *"10% of units @ 80% AMI if at
   least 10% of units are 2 bedroom or larger"*.
3. **The recitals still say first reading was on 5 October and the hearing on 12 October.** The
   actual dates are 12 and 19 October. The error is clerical, but it sits in the findings of an
   ordinance with an immediate effective date.

**What is good in it, stated plainly.** New residential target areas need an anti-displacement
finding (new VMC 3.22.030(A)(6)). Ownership projects must record covenants. The 20-year station-area
option asks for more affordability, for longer, than the 12-year option beside it. **The fixes asked
for here would make a reasonable ordinance accurate. They are not grounds to oppose it.**

**Staff's case.** The station-area option is mandated by HB 1491, and staff expect *"few
developments, if any"* to use it (staff report, 12 October packet p. 52). Administrative approval
without a hearing is a statutory requirement, not something the City chose.

### Workshop — Homeless Situation Report, Q3 2026 (attachment 5190)

A quarterly report from the Homeless Assistance and Resources Team (HART). It takes no vote. Comment
on it falls under Community Communication.

- **Deaths.** *"People we have lost since December 2025 Homeless Memorial"*: July 4, August 1,
  September 1, so **23 year to date**. The table's Q3 column says **7**, but the monthly figures add
  up to 6. One of the two numbers is wrong. Ask which.
- **Police time.** Events involving unsheltered homelessness make up **8.7% of police events** but
  **15.6% of officer hours** year to date (7,435 events, 15,167 hours). That is the City's own
  measure of how much of the homelessness response runs through policing.
- **Fire data is still missing.** The report says Fire *"hopes to have ability to report by end of the
  3rd quarter of 2026"*. This *is* the Q3 report, and the data is not in it.
- **Exits to housing from the shelter sites in Q3:** 29 in total. Homeward Bound Safe Park accounts for
  17, The Outpost 4, Hope Village 4, 415 West 3 and Kiggins Village 1. **Two people across all five
  sites obtained employment.**
- **Community Court:** 235 people cited or referred, 65 opted in, 41 graduated, and 7 obtained
  temporary housing.
- **Bridge Shelter:** substantially complete, with a temporary certificate of occupancy. Furniture
  arrives in October, and there is a **community open house on 28 October, 4–7pm**. **The report gives
  no date when beds open.** That matters because **the 1,500-foot camping ban around the shelter takes
  effect on 1 November regardless** (adopted ordinance, per `briefs/2026-09-21-vancouver-city-council.md`).
  **The question for Community Communication:** will the 120 beds be open and taking referrals before
  the ban is enforced, and how many people currently camp inside the buffer? The City has never
  published that second number.
- No new emergency orders and no new property closures this quarter.

### Item 5 — Public hearing and final vote: Cultural Access ("Inspire Vancouver") conflict rule

Attachment 5162. **The text is identical to first reading.** It bars grants to any nonprofit where a
serving Culture, Arts and Heritage Commission commissioner would get a *"direct or indirect benefit"*.
That covers board members, employees and paid contractors, and the same roles at a fiscal sponsor.
**It is the right rule and is worth supporting.** The two gaps flagged on 12 October are still open
and worth a line in writing:

- The bar covers **commissioners only**. It does not cover the eight-member community Grant Review
  Committee that actually scores the applications.
- A commissioner can resign and keep the application in the pool, with no cooling-off period.

The recital still dates the policy amendment **20 July 2026**, while the staff report says
**25 June 2026**. The ordinance takes effect on 19 October.
