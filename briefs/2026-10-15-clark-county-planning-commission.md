### Item 2 — 2027–2032 Transportation Improvement Program (CPZ-2026-00002)

The substance is in the 14 October work-session brief. The scoring gives multimodal access 6 points,
economic development 20, and concurrency points for *"moratorium areas"*. Only $36.7M of $74.9M in
grants is secured. Two things are new tonight:

- **The sequence.** The TIP went to the **Development and Engineering Advisory Board on 3 September**
  (deck slide 12), before the Planning Commission or any public hearing. Developers saw it first.
- **The strongest written testimony in the record so far is from Heidi Pozzo** (doc 238636). It cites
  the County's own presentations, and it argues something worth repeating whatever your view of growth.
  In July the six-year road programme was shown in balance at $246.8M. On 7 October Public Works said
  the TIP would draw down Road Fund reserves *"even with a 1% levy increase."* The 20-year gap is
  *"in the range of $500 million."* RCW 36.70A.070(6)(a)(iv)(C) requires a plan to say how any shortfall
  will be funded, or how the land-use assumptions will be reassessed. **This is an argument the left can
  make in its own terms: growth at the edges of the urban growth areas has not been paid for.** The
  same point argues for scoring transit, sidewalk and safety projects above new road capacity. Pozzo
  also notes that **the project priority list does not show any project's score**. The deck's priority
  list (slide 5) is an image. Ask for the scores to be published.
- Other testimony comes from residents asking for **pedestrian safety on SE Crown Road**
  (Camas–Washougal) and for the **Lacamas Pathway Project**, under items 2 and 4.

### Item 4 — 2027–2032 Parks and Nature CIP (CPZ-2026-00004)

The CIP is unchanged from 14 October. **The point to make:** the plan says Parks is *"purposefully
slowing new park development"* because it cannot staff what it has. That is the case for operating
money, and it is the opening for the countywide parks district (see the rolling page's "Ahead"
section). Back the operating money, and ask for the accountability terms then.

## Watch

### Item 1 — Columbia River Gorge disaster-recovery code (CPZ-2026-00001)

**Genuinely good.** The change follows the 2025 Rowena and Burdoin fires. Owners whose homes are
destroyed can rebuild in place on the same footprint under expedited review, and they get **ten years**
to start instead of two plus two. They may live in an RV on their own parcel while they rebuild.
Campgrounds may host displaced owners. Cultural-resource review still applies within 500 feet of a
known site. About **7,800 acres** of Clark County lie inside the Scenic Area. The **Council work session is
21 October and the Council hearing is 3 November** (staff report p. 2).

### Item 3 — 2027–2032 Stormwater Capital Plan (CPZ-2026-00003)

Routine and mostly good: **$23.5M across 32 projects**, for existing development only (9 October update
to the 14 October brief). The largest single project in the overview is the **Heritage Farm wetland
restoration, $3,949,838** (doc 237886).
