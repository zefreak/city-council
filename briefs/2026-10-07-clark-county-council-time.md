## Bottom line

**Item 8.1 is a chance to close a loophole in the state's mobile home rent cap, and the request comes
from Councilor Yung.** The 5% annual cap has an exception. When a home in a park is sold, the landlord
may impose **one uncapped rent increase** on the buyer at the first renewal. That knocks value off
every home in the park, because a buyer prices in the jump. Asking Olympia to close it would help
park residents directly. It is worth a supportive comment, and it is worth making sure the ask is
written as *close the exception* rather than *revisit the cap*.

## Act on this

### Item 8.1 — Legislative Request: Mobile Home Parks — Rent Cap Laws

**Where it came from.** The 30 September minutes, Councilor Reports:

> *"Councilor Yung requested for Council to consider as a part of the legislative agenda concerning
> mobile home parks the rent cap laws that have gone into effect, and their exception for when a tenant
> vacates the property owner can reset the rent to market rate which causes a decrease in the mobile
> homes value. Marshall supported bringing this back as an agenda item next week."*

**What the law actually says.** Yung's description is substantially right.

- **RCW 59.20.370(1)** caps lot rent increases at **5% in any 12 months** for manufactured/mobile
  home tenancies, with no increase at all in the first 12 months.
- **RCW 59.20.380(3)** is the exception: *"If a rental agreement is transferred under RCW 59.20.073
  due to a former tenant's sale of a manufactured/mobile home, the landlord has the option to make a
  **one-time increase in an amount not limited by RCW 59.20.370** … at the time of the first renewal of
  the rental agreement after the transfer."* The landlord must tell the buyer before the transfer.
- Source: 2025 c 209 §202 (HB 1217). The cap section was amended again by 2026 c 118.

One refinement to the minutes. The trigger is the **sale and transfer of the home**, not the tenant
vacating. In a park the home usually stays on the lot and the lease passes to the buyer. That is the
mechanism by which the reset hits the seller's equity. The buyer is buying a home plus a lot rent that
can jump without limit at the first renewal, so the buyer pays less for the home.

**The left read.** This is a genuinely good ask. Park residents own the home and rent the ground under it. Their equity is the home's resale
value. An uncapped reset on sale is a transfer of that equity to the park owner, triggered by every
sale. Closing it extends the cap the Legislature already passed to the moment it currently skips.

**The risk is in the drafting.** There is no paper attached, and "Rent Cap Laws" in the title is
broad. A County legislative request could ask Olympia to:

- (a) **close the 59.20.380(3) exception** — good;
- (b) "revisit" the rent cap generally, which in a legislative agenda can become a request to loosen
  it; or
- (c) ask for a study.

**What to say (3 minutes, name "item 8.1"):** support adding the 59.20.380(3) fix to the County's 2027
legislative agenda. Ask that the request be written as *removing* the one-time uncapped increase on
sale, not as a general review of the cap. If you live in a park in unincorporated Clark County, say
so. If you sold a home, or tried to, under the exception, that is the strongest testimony available.
