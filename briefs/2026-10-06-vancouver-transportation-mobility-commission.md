## Bottom line

**The second phase of public engagement on three corridor redesigns "wraps up in early October" —
before this meeting, and before the TMC public hearing in November.** The designs remove **all**
on-street parking on NE 72nd Avenue and on 97th/98th between NE Burton Road and Mill Plain, to build
bike and small-mobility lanes. These are good projects and they are the kind that get killed at a
November hearing by people who turn up about parking. The window to put support on the record is
**now**, not in November.

## Watch — the three corridor projects

At approximately **30% design**. Near-term improvements are to be built as part of an **upcoming
pavement preservation project** — which is the important structural fact: the bike and pedestrian
work rides on paving money already scheduled, so the marginal cost of doing it now is low and the
cost of deferring it is a repaved road you cannot restripe for another cycle.

| Corridor | Extent |
|---|---|
| **NE 72nd Avenue** | NE 40th St to NE 78th St |
| **NE 86th/87th Avenue** | E Mill Plain Blvd to NE Fourth Plain Blvd |
| **97th/98th Avenue** | SE 10th St to NE 39th St |

### What is proposed

**NE 72nd Ave.** Repurpose the roadway to **two 10-foot drive lanes**; add buffered bike and small
mobility lanes with tubular markers except NE 69th–75th St (Segment 4). Leading pedestrian intervals,
crossbike markings, two-stage turn bike boxes, no-turn-on-red signs. **Speed cushions and 25 mph
speed limit signs.** Traffic signal hardware upgrades. **"All existing parking will be reallocated to
create mobility lanes."** Long term: sidewalk infill near NE 40th and 75th, lighting in all segments,
crossings at NE 43rd/62nd/69th/75th, the **funded** Walnut Grove Elementary Safe Routes to School
project, a full roadway section with shared-use path in Segment 4, and an intersection study at
NE 78th St.

**NE 86th/87th Ave.** Narrow vehicle travel lanes, widen bike and small-mobility lanes and add
buffers from NE 9th St to Fourth Plain Blvd. Crossings improved at NE 5th, 6th, 9th, 12th, 20th,
33rd. Wayfinding signs, crossbike markings, two-stage turn boxes, no-turn-on-red. Speed cushions.
**"Some of the existing parking will be reallocated."** Long term: new crossings at eight further
locations, signal improvements at NE Burton Rd and Fourth Plain Blvd.

**97th/98th Ave.** Narrow travel lanes and **repurpose turn lanes** for mobility lanes and buffers.
Crossings improved at NE 9th and 11th; pedestrian refuge island at NE 14th reconstructed. Bike boxes,
crossbike markings, no-turn-on-red, sharrows. Speed cushions. **"All existing parking between
Northeast Burton Road and Mill Plain Boulevard will be reallocated."** Long term: full roadway
construction in Segments 2 and 4, lighting in Segments 2–4, sidewalks on both sides NE 2nd–10th and
NE 14th–16th, and Segments 1, 6 and 7 (south of Mill Plain, north of Burton) which are **not** in the
pavement preservation project and therefore have no funding path yet.

### The left read

**Support these.** Two 10-foot drive lanes on NE 72nd with speed cushions and a 25 mph posted limit
is a genuine safety intervention on a corridor that runs past Walnut Grove Elementary; sidewalks on
both sides of 97th/98th and lighting in three segments are basic infrastructure that east Vancouver
neighbourhoods have gone without. Repurposing turn lanes — not just parking — is the unusual and
correct part: it treats vehicle throughput, not only kerb space, as the thing to trade.

Three honest caveats:

1. **Parking removal is the fight, and it is a real cost to some people** — residents without
   off-street parking, people with mobility impairments, and small businesses on the corridor. The
   memo says parking "will be reallocated to implement the TSP Modal Network" and does not say how
   many spaces, or whether anyone currently depends on them. **That count is the number to ask for.**
2. **Segments 1, 6 and 7 of 97th/98th are outside the pavement project** — the parts south of Mill
   Plain and north of Burton. So the corridor gets improved in the middle and left as-is at both
   ends, with the long-term work contingent on "funding as available". Ask what happens at the seams.
3. **Near-term vs long-term is doing a lot of work here.** Crossings, lighting, sidewalk infill and
   full roadway sections are almost all in the long-term column. What gets built with the paving
   money is mostly paint, tubular markers, signs and speed cushions. That is worth having and it is
   not the cross-section in the renderings.

### Engagement — what the City counts as engagement

Phase 2 methods, per the memo: an interactive map of the design concepts; small-group and one-to-one
meetings; tabling at the Walnut Grove Elementary back-to-school event and the Safe Routes to School
Summer Fair; and TMC meetings. Communications: a project webpage, 4 email newsletters, 3 social posts
each on Facebook and Instagram, **4,800+ mailers to property owners and occupants** along the
corridors, and 20 yard signs with QR codes.

Mailing occupants as well as owners is the right call and is not always done. What is missing from
the list is any Spanish-language channel named as such, on corridors that include Fourth Plain.
Project page: `tinyurl.com/covstreet25`.
