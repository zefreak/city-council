### Item 7.2 — Drinking Water Ordinance, adoption target 6 October

Requested by **Clark County Public Health** (Gerry Preston, Environmental Public Health Director;
Nikki Guillot presenting), signed 31 August 2026. From the request form:

- **Desired work session date: 30 September.** 30 minutes of staff presentation.
- **Timing consideration: "Oct 6th - Target for Drinking Water code adoption, January 1, 2027
  effective date."**
- **Desired result: "Receive Council input on Drinking Water ordinance before Oct 6 public
  hearing."**
- What it does: *"Adopt by reference the Washington Department of Health drinking water
  requirements and enhance areas of the code… create a local program"* to *"improve oversight of
  operations and maintenance and provide better technical assistance."*
- Issues to be considered: comments from the **Development and Engineering Advisory Board**, the
  Drinking Water Technical Advisory Commission, Ecology and DOH.

**Why this is on the brief.** On the face of it this is good: a county adopting state drinking
water standards by reference and building a local oversight program, which is what public health
regulation looks like when it works. The thing to watch is the compression — a single 30-minute
work session on 30 September, then a public hearing and adoption on 6 October, effective 1
January 2027. Six days between the only discussion and the vote, and the ordinance text is not
yet published anywhere I can find.

Note also that DEAB — the development industry's advisory board — is first on the list of
consulted bodies, and is the same body the Planning Commission voted 5–2 to point the Council at
on the comp plan. Whose comments shaped the "enhanced areas of the code" is worth asking.

**If you care about this: ask at Council Time on 16 September that the draft ordinance be
published before the 30 September work session, not with the 6 October hearing packet.** That is
a concrete, small, winnable ask and it is in scope for this meeting because item 7.2 is on the
agenda.

### The other four work session requests

| Item | Requester | Desired date | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | Public Works / Lands Management | **October 2026** | **Sustainable Forestry Program** — review of the 2017 Forest Stewardship Plan. Seeks Council direction on *"forest health, wildlife habitat, wildfire prevention, timber production and revenue generation."* Signed 25 Aug. |
| 7.3 | Public Works / Lands Management | **November/December 2026** | **Legacy Lands** — overview of the Conservation Futures levy and the Natural Areas Acquisition Plan, ahead of a parks system plan update. *"will guide the acquisition of conservation land… over the next 20 years."* Signed 9 Sep. |
| 7.4 | Public Health, Healthy Communities | **October/November** | **Youth Gun Violence Prevention** — requested by **Councillor Yung**, who *"expressed concerns about the increased reports of youth gun violence in the local media."* Signed 4 Sep by Andrea Pruett. |
| 7.5 | Councillor request | not stated | **C-TRAN 2045 Plan.** No document published. |

Three notes on these.

**7.1 and 7.3 are the same land, argued two ways.** Sustainable forestry is explicit that county
forest management *"generated revenue and forest products that can be reinvested in county
properties"* and asks for direction on *"timber production and revenue generation"*; Legacy Lands
is about permanently conserving land bought with the Conservation Futures levy. Both are Kevin
Tyler's, both are Lands Management, and 7.1 asks the Council to set goals for forest harvest
before 7.3 asks it to set goals for conservation acquisition. **October before November/December
is the order that matters.** If the forestry goals are set first, the Legacy Lands conversation
inherits them.

**7.4 is a genuine opening, and it is worth being in the room for.** The request names community
partners including **The Foundation** (Lester Griffin) alongside a Sheriff's deputy, and proposes
as a possible outcome *"an ad-hoc, multi-disciplinary committee to determine collective prevention
priorities."* Policy implications are listed as "TBD" — which means the frame is not set. A public
health framing of gun violence, brought by a public health department with community partners at
the table, is the version of this worth supporting; it can equally be steered toward enforcement.
The work session itself will take **no public comment** — so the moment to shape it is now, at
Council Time, while the request is still being scheduled.

**7.5 has no document.** A councillor request on the C-TRAN 2045 Plan with nothing published. The
regional transit long-range plan is a large document with real consequences for the Vine BRT
network; who asked and why is not stated.

### Item 9 — three executive sessions, 40 minutes

- 9.1 **RCW 42.30.110(1)(b)** — 15 minutes. That is *acquisition of real estate by purchase or
  lease*, where public knowledge would increase the price. No subject stated, which is normal and
  permitted; the statutory purpose must be announced before the Council goes in.
- 9.2 Pending litigation, RCW 42.30.110(1)(i) — 15 minutes.
- 9.3 Pending litigation, RCW 42.30.110(1)(i) — 10 minutes.

All three are marked *"with no potential after action"*, meaning no vote is expected on return.
Compare 2 September, where the sessions were marked *"potential after-action"* and produced the
Amadi settlement authorisation. The marking is a real signal and it is on the published agenda.

**A real-estate executive session is worth noting** in the same fortnight the Council is
finalising a comprehensive plan and being briefed on Legacy Lands acquisitions. Nothing connects
them on the record; the point is only that it is the one category of executive session where the
public finds out afterwards, if at all.
