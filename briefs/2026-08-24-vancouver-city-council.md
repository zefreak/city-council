## Act on this

### Ordinance — 0.1% criminal justice sales and use tax (public hearing)

Adds **VMC 5.98.028** under **RCW 82.14.345**, created by **HB 2015 (2025)**. First reading was
17 August; public hearing 24 August. Collection begins **1 January 2027**; the ordinance takes
effect 30 days after passage.

This is a **regressive consumption tax funding police**, and it **stacks**. The ordinance's own
recitals confirm Vancouver already receives distributions under **RCW 82.14.450** (public safety)
and has a criminal justice tax under **RCW 82.14.340**. This is a third, not a replacement.

Two things worth knowing before arguing about it:

- **It is a grant qualification, not just a levy.** Sections 1.A.1–10 are findings that VPD meets
  the Local Law Enforcement Grant Program conditions — Keep Washington Working Act compliance,
  use-of-force and de-escalation policy, use-of-force data reporting, firearm relinquishment
  practice, **25% of officers through 40-hour crisis intervention training**, 100% compliance on
  gender-based violence interviewing training, and a policy barring volunteers from enforcing
  criminal law. The Council also directs the City Manager to file a detailed staffing plan
  (officer counts, **co-response teams**, 911 response rate, case closure rate) with the Criminal
  Justice Training Commission.
- That gives an unusual handle: the tax is conditioned on accountability measures with published
  compliance thresholds. A 25% CIT rate is the floor the state accepts, not an achievement.

Do not confuse this with the **TBD transportation** 0.1% sales tax. Two different 0.1% taxes are
live in Vancouver, and the city's own documents have described the TBD one as 0.01%; it is 0.1%.

### Resolution — Affordable Housing Fund Administrative & Financial Plan revisions (**CONSENT**)

Amends the 2024–2033 AHF A&F Plan adopted September 2022 under **RCW 84.52.105**. On consent, so
no debate unless pulled. Seven changes, and they are not housekeeping:

1. **Increases per-unit funding limits.**
2. **Allows predevelopment loans**, capped at 25% of the project's AHF award.
3. **Formalises the annual set-aside for Bridge Shelter operations "and adjusts AHF outcomes
   accordingly."**
4. Tailors application processes by program type.
5. **Adds people exiting institutions without a housing plan as a priority population** — a good
   change, and worth saying so.
6. Clarifies the rent formula for shared housing.
7. Requires final cost certification and reconciliation of cost savings at project completion.

**Item 3 is the one to look at.** The plan sets Temporary Shelter at $1M/yr, of which **$400k is
set aside for the City's own Bridge Shelter**, and states plainly that "AHF outcomes are based on
the $600k available for other providers." The published goal of **33 beds/yr** is therefore
measured against $600k while $1M is spent — the City pays itself $400k and the reported outcome
target shrinks to match. That may be entirely defensible; it should not pass on consent without
someone saying it out loud.

Current allocations and goals, for reference:

| Eligible use | Annual | Goal/yr | Share | 10-yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Housing Production/Preservation | $6M | 40 direct + 215 leveraged units | 60% | 2,150 units |
| Rent Assistance & Housing Services | $2M | 250 households | 20% | 2,500 households |
| Temporary Shelter | $1M | 33 beds | 10% | 330 beds |
| Homeownership | $500k | 15 households | 5% | 150 households |
| Implementation (admin) | $500k | — | 5% | — |
| **Total** | **$10M** | **513** | | **5,130** |

Per-unit limits for production: $25k group home/shared bedroom, $100k studio, $150k 1-bed and
2-bed, $200k 3+ bed. Preservation: $20k / $50k. Shelter bed $25k. Homebuyer at 80% AMI $75k.

AHF serves households **up to 50% AMI** (80% for homeownership). Context §5 of the housing
analysis is directly relevant: a cost-rent model cannot reach 30% AMI, which is why the deepest
band depends on subsidy layering rather than fund rules.

## Money and land

### Vancouver Heights Senior Housing Partners LP — disposition and development agreement (hearing)

Heights District **Site P**, E. Mill Plain Blvd at Devine Rd, part of parcel 37909805 — one of the
first redevelopment sites in the 2020 Heights District Plan. Developer selected via March 2024
RFP: **Related/Northwest Development LLC** (California) with the **Vancouver Housing Authority**.

**On the merits this is a good deal, and the brief should say so.** 51 units for seniors 55+:

| Unit | Total | 30% AMI | 50% AMI | 60% AMI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | 15 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| 1-bed | 24 | 4 | 10 | 10 |
| 2-bed | 12 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| **Total** | **51** | **11 (22%)** | **19 (37%)** | **21 (41%)** |

**The City keeps the land.** It is a **99-year ground lease**, not a sale — no rent for the first
30 months, 3% annual increases, discounted 95% for 40 years, recalculated by appraisal in year 41
with affordability renewed. Affordability runs at least 40 years. 10-year DDA term, completion
guarantees, standard default provisions.

The subsidy, stated plainly in the City's own presentation: land valued at **$676,000**; NPV of a
market-rate ground lease over 40 years **$933,596**; NPV of the discounted lease **$58,364**. The
City is forgoing roughly **$875,000** of ground rent over 40 years to secure 51 units, 11 of them
at 30% AMI. Retaining the freehold is the part worth defending — this is not a land sale.
