## Watch

### Drinking water ordinance — the comment venue moved, and the ordinance text is not attached

**What it does, per the presentation** (Nikki Guillot, Environmental Resource Protection Program
Manager):

- It codifies local authority over **Group B public water systems** and **individual wells**.
  Group B oversight is already delegated to Clark County Public Health.
- It stands up a **long-term sanitary survey programme**.
- It adds a **technical manual**, revised every five years, as a supplement to the WACs.

Individual wells serve **25%** of county water users and Group B systems **15%** (slide 3). The
county sits on a sole-source aquifer.

**This is basically good public health work.** Codifying well oversight and adding contamination
prevention protects rural households on private wells. Those households have no utility testing
their water, and the deck names the at-risk groups plainly: children, pregnant people and the
immunocompromised.

**Two things to know.**

1. **The Council had routed this briefing somewhere else.** The **16 September Council Time
   minutes** record: *"Council supported moving forward with this work session request as part of
   a Board of Health meeting"* (`data/clark-county/2026-09-23/council-time-minutes-2026-09-16.txt`,
   line 42). A Board of Health meeting has a "Comments from the Public" slot. **The 30 September
   listing puts it on a Council work session, which has none.** Last run's page praised that
   routing because it widened access. For this item, that praise no longer holds. Whether this was
   a deliberate change or a scheduling convenience is not recorded anywhere.
2. **"No new fees" is true only going forward.** Slide 11: *"No new fees proposed. Existing fee
   initiated in 2025… BOH oversight of fee schedule in future."* So a fee already exists. The
   ordinance moves future control of the fee schedule to the Board of Health, and the fee amount is
   not in the deck. Slide 12 repeats *"No new fees or staff."*

**The draft ordinance itself is not attached.** The listing links only the presentation. The
deck's schedule is:

- 30 September — work session;
- then DEAB review, and Ecology and Department of Health review;
- **public hearing 20 October**, with the Council's adoption decision;
- effective **1 January 2027**.

The hearing is the comment opening. Ask for the ordinance text and the technical manual to be
published before it. The manual is described as *"collaborative development with community
partners"*, and the only partners named are the Development Engineering Advisory Board, the
Planning Commission, an *"informal industry advisory review"* and neighbourhood associations
(slide 10).

**Note the date.** The **solid waste code hearing is also tentatively 20 October**, at 6pm
(`briefs/2026-09-23-clark-county-work-sessions.md`). That makes two public-health-and-environment
ordinances on one evening.

### E-bikes — no proposal on the table, and the Sheriff's framing is education-first

The Council asked questions; the Sheriff's Office answered in writing
(`ebike-qa.pdf`), plus its public pamphlet. **There is no ordinance, draft or option list.**

- **What the answers say.** The serious collisions and dangerous riding involving pedestrians
  mostly come from **juveniles on e-motorcycles**. Those are machines with no pedals, over 750 W,
  or able to exceed 28 mph, which are motorcycles under state law. They are not e-bikes.
  Enforcement *"lean[s] heavily toward education"*. Repeat e-motorcycle offenders can have
  vehicles impounded, and parents can be cited under RCW 46.20.024. The pursuit policy *"typically
  restricts deputies from initiating a pursuit"*.
- **What to watch for.** A work session like this is where a county-level e-bike ordinance
  sometimes starts. Candidates would be speed limits on trails, age limits beyond state law, a
  helmet mandate, or impound powers. Any of these would fall on the people who use e-bikes as
  transport — commuters, delivery workers, kids without a car. The Sheriff's own answer is that the
  problem is a different vehicle already illegal to ride this way. **If anything is proposed, that
  is the point to make.** Nothing has been proposed.
- Also from the Q&A: Clark County has **no helmet ordinance**. The City of Vancouver *"has a helmet
  ordinance, but nothing specifically related to restrictions on e-bikes"* — that is the Sheriff's
  statement and was not checked against the VMC.
