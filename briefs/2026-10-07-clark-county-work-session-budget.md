## Bottom line

**The 2027 County budget is being built around two November ballot measures: Proposition 12, bonds
for public safety and criminal justice buildings, and Proposition 13, a levy lid lift for public safety
and criminal justice services.** The County's budget page says departments have filed "70-series"
requests for what they need **if those measures fail**. Public Defense's main request, $950,000 to
keep and attract contract defenders, is numbered **PUD-70-27AD**. That number puts it in the
fallback series, which suggests the County expects to fund it from the levy if the levy passes. The
right to counsel is being packaged with jail and policing money. **Nothing to do on 7 October. The
budget public hearing is Tuesday 1 December.**

## Watch

### 1. Public defense is short of lawyers and asking for market-rate contracts

- **PUD-70-27AD: +$950,000 a year**, General Fund, *"to retain our current public defense contracts and
  attract new contracts with a rate of pay that is in line with the market."* The deck names a
  *"lack of public defenders in Clark County"*, the new state-mandated caseload standards, and
  *"establishing a County Public Defense Office"*.
- **PUD-73-27AD: four in-house investigators**, about $454,000 (a second figure, $383,012, is printed
  beside it). Investigation is 100% contracted out today. Since 1 October 2025 those contracts carry
  sales tax. Staff say bringing the work in-house *"could make this request budget neutral."*
- PUD-90-27AD offers the investigator savings, **−$220,559**, as the department's 2.5% cut. Staff warn
  that this may mean an emergency request in the fall 2027 supplemental.

**Read.** This is the best thing in the deck, and it is the item most likely to be traded away. The
left case is simple: caseload standards are a constitutional floor, and the County is below it on
staffing. The question for the recommended budget on 3 November is whether the $950K is in the base
budget or only in the Prop 13 package. **If it depends on a levy that is mostly sold as public safety,
the right to counsel is riding on a jail-and-police vote.**

*Unclear:* the meaning of the "70" number for this request. The budget page says 70-series requests are
alternatives *"if Proposition No. 12 and/or Proposition No. 13 are not approved."* That implies the
item would otherwise be paid from Prop 13. The deck itself does not say so.

### 2. The jail is growing: J-Pod, 64 new beds

- Jail Services is *"opening of J-Pod, a new 64-bed medium security jail facility"*, and has reopened
  *"previously closed housing areas"*.
- **$519,840 a year** for medical coverage in J-Pod (JSD-15) and **$617,975 a year** for the inmate
  medical contract (JSD-12). The deck says *"the jail population is acutely sick."* It cites the
  Trueblood settlement, the ADA and *"basic constitutional care"*, and notes *"Mandatory overtime —
  16-hour shifts"*.
- **$317,334** for a two-year project manager for a **public safety training center**, firearms
  certification in particular (JSD-21).
- *"If Propositions 12 and 13 pass … several budget requests will be unnecessary."*

**Read.** The medical money is necessary on its own terms. A jail that cannot staff medical care is a
liability and a human-rights problem. But it should be read with item 1: **the County has found
capacity for 64 more beds and is still short of defence lawyers.** Bed count drives jail population.
That trade-off belongs in the 1 December testimony.

### 3. Small, cheap and good, so easy to lose

- **Juvenile Court, JUV-01: $14,000 one-time** to finish the Juvenile Detention Alternatives Initiative
  (JDAI) grant to June 2027. *"If approval is not received, Juvenile Court will end involvement with
  JDAI."* $14K keeps a detention-alternatives programme alive.
- **JUV-03: a Family Services Care Coordinator, $126,502** from the Mental Health Sales Tax. It is
  diversion at first contact, aimed at *"racial/ethnic disparity at the point of arrest"*.
- Juvenile detention averages **16 youth a day**. Probation caseloads average 24.6 against a 25
  best-practice standard.
- Juvenile Court was asked for a **$291,283** cut (2.5%) and offers $186,239. Most of that is
  unspent grant authority, plus a $35,943 cut to diagnostic evaluations, treatment and youth
  activities.

### 4. Public Health is spending reserves through 2027 and has not said what happens in 2028

*"Public Health will have reductions from State Foundational Public Health Services (FPHS) grant
allocations in 2027. Fund balance will maintain current services through 2027, but future decisions
will be needed for 2028."* No figure is given. That 2028 decision will come after this budget is
adopted.

### 5. Parks: insolvency projected in 2031, and $220K to prepare a ballot measure

The Greater Clark Parks District fund is projected **insolvent in 2031**. Public Works asks for
**$220,000** (PWK-25-26FL, MPD Operations Fund) for *"market research … to prepare for a public
measure"*, plus a **$390,661** one-time General Fund transfer to keep regional parks open. This is
the Fall 2027 parks district expansion flagged in the Vancouver PRAC brief of 23 September. **Public
money is being spent to prepare a tax campaign.** That is legal for research. Watch whether it
becomes advocacy.
