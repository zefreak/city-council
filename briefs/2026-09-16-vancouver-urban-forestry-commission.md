## Watch

### Emerald ash borer is here (staff report, packet pp. 6–7)

*"EAB identified in Vancouver area August 2026."* The staff report is blunt about what follows:
EAB is *"fatal to ash trees within approximately five to ten years after detection"*, *"there has
never been a successful effort to eradicate EAB once it becomes established"*, and the population
is *"anticipated to increase significantly over the next five to ten years."*

Vancouver has had an EAB Management Plan since 2022, which is why this is a management problem
rather than a crisis. The response is treat what is worth preserving, remove the rest, replant with
drought-tolerant and disease-resistant species, and limit firewood transport.

**The part with a distributional edge**, and it is not flagged as such anywhere in the packet: the
city is sending *"postcards to homeowners with ash street trees explaining management and treatment
options"* and doing *"neighborhood outreach regarding EAB and privately owned ash trees."*
Treatment is recurring and removal is expensive. A dying ash on private land is the owner's cost;
a dying ash in the right of way is the city's. Where the line falls — and whether there is any
assistance for owners who cannot pay — is not addressed in this report, and it is the question to
ask as the outreach goes out. Staff are separately applying for a **Washington DNR grant
specifically targeting ash trees on school sites**, which is the right instinct applied to one
category of owner.

Sightings go to the Washington Invasive Species Council (invasivespecieswa.gov,
invasivespeciesinfo@rco.wa.gov); Urban Forestry staff are the local first contact.

### The new tree code is already in effect

The staff report records that the comprehensive-plan tree code went into effect **at the end of
July 2026** and staff are now implementing it. The July minutes (packet p. 3) list what it covers:
tree preservation, redesign specifications, incorporating street trees, **minimum tree canopy
coverage**, and parking lot landscaping. Minimum canopy requirements and tree preservation rules
interact directly with infill and middle-housing feasibility on small lots — worth remembering
when the Planning Commission's HB 1491 Title 20 work comes back.

### Tree canopy assessment for stormwater

Staff and Surface Water are running a citywide canopy assessment on 2025 summer data, contractor
**Plan IT Geo**, to identify how canopy supports stormwater management under the city's 2024-2029
NPDES permit. No dollar figure in the report. The **proactive street tree program** is being run
in coordination with Transportation's pavement management, and staff state its aims include
addressing *"social/environmental justice"* — a canopy-equity claim that will be checkable once
the assessment lands.
