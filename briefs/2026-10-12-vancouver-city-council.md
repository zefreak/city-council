### Item 4 — Public hearing and final vote: HB 1491 amendments to Title 20

Staff report 195-26, pp. 108–110. The ordinance (attachment 5135) is **byte-for-byte the same file as
on 5 October**. Substance: `briefs/2026-10-05-vancouver-city-council.md` and
`context/HOUSING-vancouver-wa.md` §32c.

**New finding: the family-sized option is looser than the statute.**

| Document | Third affordability option |
|---|---|
| **Statute**, 3SHB 1491 §3(7)(a)(ii), session law p. 13 lines 3–5 (new section in ch. 36.70A RCW) | 10% of units as workforce housing *"if at least 10 percent of the units are family sized units **with more than two bedrooms**"* |
| **Title 20 ordinance** (this item), VMC 20.530.040(C) | 10% at 80% AMI *"provided at least 10% of the units have **two or more bedrooms**"* |
| **MFTE ordinance** (item 1), VMC 3.22.050(B)(2) | 10% at 80% AMI *"where at least 10% of the **restricted** units are family sized units with more than two bedrooms"* |
| MFTE deck, p. 54 | *"10% of units @ 80% AMI if at least 10% of units are 2 bedroom or larger"* |

- The Title 20 text counts a **two-bedroom** as family-sized. The statute requires **three or more**.
  That makes the cheapest compliance route easier to meet than state law allows. A developer could
  satisfy Vancouver's code and still fall short of RCW. **Ask for "more than two bedrooms" before
  final passage.** It is a one-word fix and it is the family-housing point of that option.
- The MFTE ordinance gets the bedroom count right but changes the base. "10% of the restricted units"
  means 10% of a 10% set-aside, about **1% of the building**. The statute's *"10 percent of the units"*
  most naturally reads as all units. That is arguable, and I flag it as a reading rather than a
  certainty. But the two Vancouver ordinances cannot both be right.

**Correction to this project's earlier work.** The 5 October brief described this option as
*"at least 10% of units have two or more bedrooms."* That copied the ordinance, not the statute, and
**it was wrong as a statement of the law.** `context/HOUSING-vancouver-wa.md` §32c (*"two-bedroom or
larger"*) took the same wording from the 10 August staff deck and is wrong on the same point.

**Still unfixed from 5 October** (all in attachment 5135):

- The recital says the hearing was *"October 12, **2025**"*.
- *"codified under RCW. and"*: the citation is still blank.
- Still no Section 3 and no severability clause, though the title promises severability.

**Still the main ask: adopt the impacted/exempt parcel map as an exhibit.** The ordinance still leaves
it as a webpage *"available at the Community Development Department Permit Center"*, so staff can
change which parcels fill the 25% exemption without a vote.

**Staff's case, fairly put:** HB 1491 is partially funded inclusionary zoning, and staff expect it to
push development away from transit (staff report p. 110; context §32c). That deserves an answer, not
dismissal. But it is an argument for better incentives in Olympia. It is not an argument for a code
looser than the statute.

### Item 1 (consent) — MFTE changes under HB 1491 and HB 1494: first reading; hearing 19 October

Staff report 197-26, pp. 49–52. Deck pp. 53–58. Ordinance pp. 59 onward.

**What it does.**

- **Adds a 20-year MFTE option in BRT station areas** (VMC 3.22.050). It carries a 50-year recorded
  covenant, one of the three affordability options above, and a **mandatory 50% cut in transportation
  impact fees**. **Approval becomes administrative.** Station-area exemptions are granted by the
  director, *"without public hearing,"* if the criteria are met. Ordinary target-area exemptions still
  go to Council by resolution. Staff list *"Removes City Council review and approval process"* as a
  challenge (p. 52). The statute requires it.
- **HB 1494 clean-up.** Covenants are now required on 12-year ownership projects. One non-compliant
  owner no longer cancels a whole condo's exemption. Taxing districts must be notified. **New
  residential target areas need an anti-displacement finding under RCW 36.70A.070(2)** (new VMC
  3.22.030(A)(6)). That last one is genuinely good.

**What to flag.**

1. **The tenant income-growth protection is in the staff report but not in the code.** Staff say HB
   1494 lets an MFTE tenant whose income rises stay eligible *"until their adjusted household income
   exceeds 150%"* (p. 51), and the recitals list it. **The amended chapter never says it.** There is
   no "150" anywhere in the operative text. The statute is self-executing: HB 1494 §2(9) says such a
   household *"may continue to qualify … until their adjusted household income exceeds 150 percent of
   the established income limit"* (session law p. 8). So tenants are probably protected in law either
   way. But a compliance officer working from VMC 3.22 will not see it. **Ask for it to be written
   in.** Note also that the statute measures 150% *of the income limit*, not 150% of AMI as the staff
   report says. For an 80% AMI unit that is about 120% AMI. Staff should state it correctly.
2. **The bedroom-base discrepancy** above.
3. **The recital says first reading 5 October and hearing 12 October.** The agenda says 12 and 19
   October. The ordinance history cites *"RES M-4343"* as the 2025 update and *"M-4494"* as February
   2025, which cannot both be right given the 2023 ordinance is M-4416. These are clerical, but they
   are in the findings.

**The money.** The station-area option *"will defer new residential structural property tax
collections for 20 years"*. Staff expect *"few developments, if any"* to use it (p. 52). Their own
challenge list concedes the 50% impact-fee cut *"reduces access to capital project funding for City
infrastructure surrounding transit nodes."* No dollar figure is given for either.

**Context.** The 12-year MFTE is already the softest number in the City's housing plan: 20% of a
building at ≤80% AMI, and JLARC could not tell how often it changes what gets built (context, §32
table). The 20-year station-area option asks more, over 50 years. **It is a better deal for the public
than the 12-year option it sits beside.** The hearing is **19 October**. Comment on the first reading
goes in Community Communication on 12 October.

### Workshop — Healthy and Climate-Smart Homes (pp. 5–48)

A Bloomberg Harvard fellow's study of retrofitting existing homes: buildings are 37% of community
emissions, and residential energy is the largest share. It asks Council which audience to prioritise
(homeowners, or renters in 1–4 unit buildings) and whether the City should **inform, navigate or
fund**.

- **Good, and worth backing toward "fund" for renters.** The memo says the City's Housing
  Rehabilitation Program serves **~10 households a year** with a **waitlist of 80–100 and waits of up
  to two years** (p. 22). HEAR reached 53 households in 2025. Larger apartment buildings are covered by
  the state Clean Buildings standard. **Small rentals have "few, if any, requirements"**, and that is
  where renters are least protected from heat and smoke.
- **The left-critical point:** retrofits in rentals raise the risk of rent increases or
  renoviction. The background report's own recommendation 4 is *"pathways for renters and small
  multifamily buildings **with tenant protections**"*. If the City funds landlords, ask for rent and
  tenancy conditions attached to the money. The memo also mentions the **rental registry** as an
  outreach channel.
- No money, no vote. This is a direction-setting session, so the time to argue for renters is now.

**On item 2.** It is the right rule, and the CAHC brief's guess (that the amendment concerned grant
allocations, labelled as a guess) was **wrong**. Two gaps worth a line in writing:

- The bar covers **commissioners only**, not the eight-member community Grant Review Committee that
  actually scores the applications.
- A commissioner can **resign and keep the application in the pool** (staff report). There is no
  cooling-off period, so someone who helped design year one can step off and be funded in year one.

The staff report dates the policy amendment **25 June 2026**. The ordinance recital says **20 July
2026**. One is wrong.
