any matter pertaining to county business"*. Time cannot be yielded to another speaker without a
majority vote. Ways to take part: in person (sign-in sheet), on WebEx with \*3, or in writing at

### Separate Business 2: Parametrix, North County Recycling and Transfer Station, Phase II, $1,606,526.85

Staff report PW26-252 and Amendment Two (doc 238971).

- **What it buys:** the work to narrow four sites (BS-12/13, BS-116, BS-137, BS-141) to two, then a
  full SEPA **Environmental Impact Statement** for both. The EIS is **$1,006,582** of the total. Public
  outreach is **$212,252**, about $186,200 of it subcontracted. Paid from Fund 6318, the regional
  solid waste system fund, which means **garbage ratepayers**. Figures are from the amendment's Phase 2 cost table.
- **How the cut is made:** Task 5.04. *"Based on the level of interest in selling of the property
  owners for the four sites, the Consultant will support a County decision on which sites to carry
  forward."* The 26 August brief said this would happen, and the scope now puts it in writing. Site
  BS-141 also depends on a neighbouring owner granting an easement for an access road off SR 502. If
  the owner refuses and no other layout works, *"site BS-141 will be dropped."*
- **The left read.** A willing-seller test is reasonable for buying land, but it is not a siting
  criterion. Transfer stations carry truck traffic, noise and odour for 60 years. Whoever is willing
  to sell, the people who live next door have no say in the cut. The EIS will analyse only the two
  sites that come out of it.
- **The ask.** On 26 August the County's own deck (slide 10) asked the Council to *"conduct a public
  hearing on the siting"*. **This amendment does not schedule one.** Its scope has DEIS comment
  meetings only. Ask, by name, for a Council public hearing on the two finalists *before* the EIS
  starts, not after.

**Two inconsistencies in the papers.** Neither changes the vote.
- The staff report says Phase I *"finalized a list of **seven** potential sites"*, then that Phase II
  will *"narrow the **four** candidate sites"*. Per the 26 August deck, the funnel was 158 → 15 → 7 →
  4, so the four is correct.
- The amendment's cover page gives a **"Total Contract Amount" of $1,606,526.85**. That is the Phase II
  figure alone. Either Phase I is not counted or the label is wrong. **The contract's real cumulative
  value is not stated.**

The staff report's action box is ticked **Consent**, but the agenda lists the item under Separate
Business. The same mismatch appears on Separate Business 1 and 3. It looks like a template default.

## Watch

### Public hearing: Solid Waste Code updates (CCC 2.37, 24.12), effective 1 January 2027

- **Mostly housekeeping.** The 2025 move of solid waste from Public Health to Public Works means
  compost-procurement reporting moves to the Solid Waste division and follows Ecology's deadlines.
  CCC 24.12.370–.380 (compost education and reporting) is repealed as duplicating state law and the
  Comprehensive Solid Waste Management Plan.
- **The real change: the temporary $1.43-per-ton tip fee from 2017 is repealed** (CCC 24.12.130). It
  funded Public Health's solid and hazardous waste work while state money was paused. State funding
  is back, and Public Health now does enforcement only.
- **Two questions worth asking.** (1) **Does the $1.43 come off what customers pay**, or is it
  absorbed somewhere in the system? The staff report says only that the fee is *"no longer
  warranted"*. (2) **What pays for illegal-dumping and solid waste enforcement now?** The staff report
  points to an *"interdepartmental agreement"* between Public Works and Public Health that is still
  being drafted and is not attached. Enforcement funding that rests on an unsigned internal agreement
  is easy to lose in a tight budget (see the 21 October work session brief).
- The deck also says the update *"clarifies conditions for exemptions when delays occur"* in compost
  procurement. The ordinance text is an image, so **what that exemption allows was not read.**

### Public hearing: new Drinking Water ordinance, CCC 24.22, effective 1 January 2027

Ordinance 2026-10-20 (doc 238926). It is the County's first local code for individual wells, two-party
shared systems and small **Group B** systems. The recitals count **over 900 Group B systems serving an
estimated 80,000 residents**, and say about 95% of residents rely on groundwater.

- **Broadly good public health practice.** State rules are adopted by reference. The Health Officer
  gains clear authority over new systems, modifications and decommissioning. Group B records must be
  handed over when a property is sold (§(7)).
- **Where the cost shows up later:** the ordinance lets the **Board of Health set fee schedules** and
  **require annual operating permits** for Group B purveyors (RCW 70A.125.090). Neither is set here.
  Rural mobile home parks and small rental clusters on shared wells would pay those fees, and pass
  them on. **Watch the Board of Health fee schedule, not this hearing.**
- **"Implementation and enforcement of this chapter shall be discretionary"**, and it imposes no
  *"mandatory duty"* on Public Health. That is standard language to avoid liability (the public duty
  doctrine). It also means a resident cannot compel the County to act on a failing system.
- **Detail lives in manuals the Council will not vote on.** Design standards and procedures go into
  *"technical and administrative manuals"* the Health Officer can change without the Council. That is
  efficient, and it is also less accountable.
- **The draft is not clean.** It has a template placeholder, *"[MAY INCLUDE ADDITIONAL INSTRUCTIONS
  DEPENDING ON TYPE OF CODE, FOR EXAMPLE - RCW36.70A.106]"*, a Section 4 but no Section 3, and
  "Title 24.22" where it means Chapter 24.22. It needs a clean copy before signature. *"A technical
  advisory committee has not yet been initiated"*, so the only outside review was informal input from
  industry and the DEAB.
- Written comment on the draft closed **17 October**, per the staff report. Spoken testimony at the
  hearing is still open.
