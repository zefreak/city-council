## Update, 9 October — what the ordinance says

### The decisions were taken on 5 October. Tuesday ratifies them.

The staff report says it plainly: *"The council's deliberations and decisions occurred at that
hearing. This ordinance and supporting materials reflect the council's decisions at the hearing."*
The ordinance's own findings (§I.2.b, pp. 3–4) record how the five farmland de-designations ended:

|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Camas UGA east, housing (Nevin property) | approve, 4–3 | **Denied.** No de-designation |

| 9 | Ridgefield UGA southwest, housing (Maul property) | approve, 5–2 | **Approved**, as under 20 acres and surrounded by Ridgefield |

**Two of the five were stopped, and that is a real win.** Camas was the largest. It was the item
with 987 units of capacity in the expansion area (Exhibit 4, Table 3, per the 1 October brief). It is
also only a partial result. Ridgefield was already **+1,379 units over its allocation**, and both of
its expansions passed. The Department of Commerce's 22 September letter said the existing boundaries
are *"sufficient"*. The County had itself told Commerce that this kind of de-designation
*"historically has not been found to be defensible"*. Neither point changed for items 7–9.

**Why the rush.** The ordinance recites that the Growth Management Hearings Board set a compliance
deadline of **29 October 2026** for the plan, under an amended schedule adopted on 13 August 2026. The
statutory deadline of 31 December 2025 had already been missed (recitals, p. 1).

### Act on this — the written comment is now about appeal standing, not about the vote

The votes are made, so a comment by noon on Tuesday is unlikely to change items 7–9. **What it does
is preserve the right to appeal them.** A petition to the Growth Management Hearings Board may be
filed only by *"a person who has participated orally or in writing before the county or city
regarding the matter on which a review is being requested"* (RCW 36.70A.280(2)(b)). The
participation must be *"reasonably related to the person's issue"* (subsection (4)). The statute is
cached at `data/state-law/rcw-36.70A.280.txt`. Petitions are due **within 60 days** of the County's
published notice of adoption (RCW 36.70A.290(2)). The ordinance makes UGA expansions and
de-designations effective **60 days after adoption** (§V.1.c).

- **Who this matters for:** any person or organisation that might appeal items 7, 8 or 9, or that
  might join an appeal someone else brings. If you or your chapter have not already commented on the
  farmland issue at any point in this process, **write by noon on Tuesday 13 October and name items
  7, 8 and 9 specifically.** Anyone who already commented on it earlier probably has standing
  already. That is my reading of the statute, not legal advice.
- **What to say, in three sentences:** items 7–9 de-designate agricultural land for jurisdictions
  that Exhibit 4 shows are already over their allocations. Commerce found the existing UGAs
  sufficient. The County's own 17 September letter concedes that proximity-based de-designation has
  not been found defensible.
- **Channel:** https://clark.wa.gov/councilors/public-comment, or by post to Clark County Council,
  c/o Rebecca Messinger, PO Box 5000, Vancouver WA 98666-5000. Spoken comment at the start of the
  1pm meeting is the other channel (Rules §VII.L.i). Keep a copy of whatever you send.

### Watch — the Council ordered a Housing Commission

The ordinance's last recital says the Council directed the County **"to form a Housing Commission,
form a Mineral Resources Commission, and commence work on a Public Benefit Rating System (PBRS)"**.
Housing Element policy 2.5.4 (Exhibit 1a, p. 105) describes the commission only as *"composed of
residents and representatives with diverse housing experiences and other stakeholders"*.

- **The Housing Commission is the opening.** Composition is not set. The ask, from now: **named seats
  for tenants, manufactured-home park residents and people with lived experience of homelessness**,
  set in the charter rather than left to appointment. Otherwise "stakeholders" tends to mean the
  building industry. No date or charter is published.
- **PBRS cuts the property tax on enrolled open-space land** (current-use taxation). The shortfall
  shifts to every other taxpayer in the district, and renters pay through rent. It may be worth it
  for farmland preservation, but it is a tax shift and should be argued as one. No design is
  published.

### Councilor Little's TDR policy was adopted — without the example price

### The housing code that comes with it

- **Manufactured Home Park overlay, new CCC 40.250.120 (Exhibit 15)**, adopted. It applies inside
  UGAs only. It permits parks, transitional housing and permanent supportive housing, and nothing else.
  **The escape hatch is unchanged from the draft the 14 September brief criticised.** A rezone is
  allowed on any **one** of three grounds: (1) the overlay *"does not allow for reasonable use"*;
  (2) park uses are *"not economically viable due to the property's location, site conditions, or
  surrounding development"*; or (3) the redevelopment provides housing affordable at ≤60% AMI long
  term, with a right of first opportunity for displaced residents. Ground 3 is genuinely good. Grounds
  1 and 2 make it unnecessary, because rising land value around a park is exactly "surrounding
  development". There is no relocation assistance, no notice period and no resident opportunity to
  purchase. The draft's dangling affordability clause (old C.2) is gone rather than fixed. *New*
  parks need a separate docket to get the overlay.
- **Shelter and supportive housing, new CCC 40.260.076 (Exhibit 20).** Indoor emergency shelter and
  emergency housing are permitted. **"Temporary structures are prohibited."** Whether that rules out
  pallet-shelter villages of the kind Vancouver runs as Safe Stay communities is **unclear** from the
  text, and it is the question to ask. Operators must notify everyone within 500 feet and hold
  *"more than one community meeting"*. Within 500 feet of another shelter or a school, the County may
  negotiate conditions and **deny the permit after 180 days**. That does not apply to shelters for
  youth, families or domestic violence survivors. The section cites *"RCW 29.36.70A.070(2)(a)(ii)"*,
  a drafting error for 36.70A.070. Much of this looks like it tracks state siting law, but I did not
  check which parts are mandated and which are local choices.
- **Co-living housing (Exhibit 17)** is permitted at a quarter of a dwelling unit per sleeping unit,
  per RCW 36.70A.535. That is state law and good.
- **Mixed-use zones (Exhibit 14):** the redline prints both "twelve twenty-four (12 24)" for the
  minimum density and "forty-three fifty (43 50)" for the maximum. Which is old and which is new
  **cannot be told from the extracted text** (README gotcha). The current code was not fetched: Clark
  County's code has moved to ecode360, which renders in the browser and was not reachable by `curl`.
  The exception clauses read as plain text: **no maximum density for affordable housing, transitional
  housing or PSH**, and 100%-affordable projects (average 60% AMI, 30-year covenant) exempt from the
  ground-floor requirement. Whether those are new is likewise not established.

### 2. Public hearing — Sheriff's civil service fee schedule (CCC 2.79), effective 1 January 2027

Staff report and draft ordinance (doc 238226). The redline uses `((deleted))` markup, so the direction
of each change is recoverable (README gotcha).

**What it does.** It updates the fees the Sheriff charges for serving and executing civil process,
for the first time since 2017. The ones that matter:

| Fee | Now | Proposed | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eviction writ, executed without standby** | $55 | **$135** | +145% |
| **Eviction writ, with Sheriff standby, first hour** | $95 | **$220** | +132% |
| Eviction writ, each additional hour | $50 | $85 | +70% |
| Eviction cancellation | $20 fee | **whole deposit non-refundable once the eviction is posted** | — |
| Service of summons, one person | $30 | $40 | +33% |
| Return of service | $15 | $20 | +33% |
| Serving an arrest warrant | $50 | $150 | +200% |
| Levy/execution of writ | $60/hr | $70/hr (real property), $85/hr (personal property) | +17% / +42% |
| Mileage | $0.55/mi over 20 mi | federal standard rate, $10 minimum | — |
| **Protective and anti-harassment orders** | free | **still free** (§5(e)) | — |

**The left read.**

- **Eviction fees rise fastest of any line, and there is no cost study.** The staff report justifies
  the increase only by *"the rise in labor and other costs"* since 2017. It attaches no cost analysis.
  The budget impact table is blank, and "Community outreach" reads *"None."* Inflation since 2017
  cannot explain +145% (CPI has risen by something like a third over that period; that is a rough
  figure, not checked this run). Other lines rose about 33%. **Why do eviction writs rise four times
  as fast as ordinary service?** That is the question to ask.
- **Who ends up paying.** The landlord pays the Sheriff. But Washington's unlawful detainer statute
  lets a prevailing landlord recover costs in the judgment. If the writ fee is taxed as a cost, as it
  usually is, it lands on the evicted tenant's judgment debt. *I did not verify this run how Clark
  County judgments treat Sheriff writ fees. Treat it as likely, not established.*
- **The non-refundable deposit.** At present a cancelled eviction costs $20. Under the draft the whole
  deposit is forfeit once the eviction is posted. That raises the landlord's cost of calling off an
  eviction after posting, which is exactly when rental assistance or a payment deal tends to arrive.
  It could cut either way, but it is worth asking whether the County has thought about it.
- **Genuinely good:** protective and anti-harassment orders stay free.

**Ask for:** the cost basis for each line; eviction writ fees held to the same ~33% as other service;
and the cancellation refund kept for evictions called off after posting. Three minutes at the hearing.
Written comment goes through the same portal.
