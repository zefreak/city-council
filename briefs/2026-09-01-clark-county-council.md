## Bottom line

The Council will hold a public hearing to rewrite its own rules on what the public may say to it —
**and has published no ordinance text for it.** The other two hearings both have documents, and both
are more consequential than they look: the budget supplemental hires **14 permanent deputies and
corrections officers** on a state grant that expires in 2028, after which the county's 0.1% sales tax
carries them; and the CAPER records **$3,977,040 of federal housing money that produced zero rental
units and zero units of housing for homeless people** in the program year.

### 1. Public Hearing — Rules of Procedure (ordinance amending CCC 2.09.040)

Still the item to show up for, and now for a second reason.

**No ordinance and no Exhibit "A" is attached to this agenda.** I fetched every link on the
Council's own listing row for 1 September (checked 26 Aug 2026, 18:4x): the four posted documents
are the budget supplemental resolution, the budget reports, and the CAPER staff report and
presentation. There is **nothing** for the Rules hearing. The agenda entry is three lines — "The
Council will consider an ordinance amending Clark County Code section 2.09.040 Rules of Procedure.
Staff: Kathleen Otto." That is the whole published record of a hearing on the public's own speech
rights, six days out.

The earlier version of this brief guessed that the ordinance link might have been collapsed with an
identically-labelled Staff Report link in the digest. That guess was wrong. The link is not there.

**This is itself the thing to say at the hearing.** Rules §VII.A requires Tuesday agendas posted by
5pm the preceding Wednesday; the agenda made that deadline, the substance did not accompany it. Ask
for the ordinance to be posted and the hearing continued if it is not.

What is likely at stake, read from the **draft ordinance cached in this repo** at
`data/clark-county/clark-rules-of-procedure-2026-07-27-DRAFT.pdf` — an *unadopted* markup, ordinance
number and adoption date both blank:

**a. The decorum rule is being rewritten (§VII.L.vi).** The struck-out old text barred remarks
"defamatory to County staff, uncivil, rude, vulgar, profane, or otherwise disruptive." The
replacement bars statements concerning:

- "private activities, lifestyles, or beliefs of others, including individual County employees or
  elected officials";
- "topics unrelated to the business of the County";
- **"professional duties and performance of County employees or employees of its elected
  officials."**

The third is the load-bearing one. How a department, a program or a named official actually performs
is the ordinary substance of public comment at a county council — it is how a jail medical contract,
a permit backlog or a deputy's conduct gets raised in public at all. A rule putting that category out
of bounds is a content restriction in a forum the Council itself opened. Ask what problem it solves,
and get the answer in the minutes.

**b. Point of order widens.** New text: "**Any** Councilor may ask for a point of order and when
recognized the Chair, Vice Chair or their designee may ask any person disrupting the business of the
Council to cease such conduct." Today that starts with the Chair alone.

**c. Two changes that are genuinely good, and worth saying so.** Open public comment **moves earlier**
in the Tuesday order — to immediately after Amendments to the Agenda, instead of after the hearings,
so you no longer sit through three hearings to use three minutes. And a new subsection provides that
"**Members of the public may provide public testimony on removed agenda items during Public
Comment**," closing the trick where pulling an item from Council Time makes it un-commentable under
the agenda-items-only rule. Additions to Council Time must also now be "time sensitive in nature."

**Firm / not firm.** That the cached draft exists, is unadopted and contains these changes: firm.
That the ordinance heard on 1 September *is this draft*: **still not verified, and now unverifiable
from the agenda**, because nothing is posted. The draft is five weeks old. The underline/strikethrough
reading is inferred from `pdftotext` artefacts, not from seeing the marked-up page; whether the
"professional duties and performance" clause is new or carried over is **not settled**.

### 2. Public Hearing — 2026 Mid-Year Budget Supplemental

**Read in full:** resolution (`resolution.txt`), budget reports (`budget-reports.txt`, 5 pp of
schedules plus narrative).

This is not a general mid-year clean-up. It is a **single change request package, BGT-01-26MY**, in
**one fund** — 1044, Law and Justice Support. Nothing else is in it.

| | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue change | **$1,626,298** | $0 |
| Expense change | **$1,784,856** | **$1,787,742** |
| Fund balance change | **($158,558)** | **($1,787,742)** |

*(Attachment A1, Summary by Fund, p.1.)* Of the 2026 expense, $1,046,590 is one-time and $738,266 is
ongoing (Attachment A2, p.2).

**What it buys: 14.00 new permanent FTE** (Attachment B, pp.3–4; grand total 14.00) —

- **10 Deputy Sheriff positions** (Sheriff's Office)
- **4 Corrections Officer positions** (Jail Services Department)

effective 08/2026, listed as **Regular**, not project-term.

**Where the money comes from, and the part worth reading twice.** Under RCW 43.101.540 (ESHB 2015),
the Washington State Criminal Justice Training Commission runs a **$100 million grant program
available through 30 June 2028** for hiring, retaining and training law enforcement personnel, peer
counselors and behavioral health professionals in co-response roles. Clark County has Round 1 money.
The funding split, quoted from the narrative:

> "75 percent of these costs will be supported through the HB 2015 Local Law Enforcement Grant
> Program, with the remaining 25 percent funded by the … 0.1 percent Criminal Justice Sales and Use
> Tax." … "**Upon expiration of the grant in 2028, the dedicated sales and use tax will assume
> responsibility for the ongoing costs of these Council-approved positions**, consistent with the
> long-term funding strategy approved in Staff Report No. 098-26 on April 21, 2026."

So: a state grant pays three-quarters of 14 permanent positions for two years, and in 2028 a local
0.1% sales tax — a regressive tax — picks up **100% of an ongoing cost estimated at $1,787,742 a year
in 2027, before any compensation increases.** That is the structure to ask about. It is the standard
grant-cliff shape, stated plainly in the county's own document rather than hidden, which is to the
Budget Office's credit.

**Two document problems, both small but worth having on the record.**

- The narrative gives 2026 grant revenue as "**$1,626,698**"; every schedule says **$1,626,298**. A
  $400 discrepancy inside one document. The schedules are self-consistent and should be presumed right.
- The same narrative calls the tax "**the voter-approved** 0.1% Criminal Justice Sales and Use Tax"
  in the Justification, and "**the Councilmanic** 0.1 percent Criminal Justice Sales and Use Tax"
  twice in the Cost Estimate. Those are opposite claims about whether the public voted on it.
  Councilmanic means imposed by council vote without a ballot. **I have not resolved which is
  correct** and will not guess; it is a fair question to put to staff at the hearing, and it matters
  for how the 2028 hand-off is framed.

**The hearing is largely formal, and you should know that before spending your three minutes.** The
narrative states this "implements the Council-approved appropriations authorized through the Staff
Report No.174-26 approved on July 21, 2026" and that "This request has received prior Council
approval." The staffing decision was made on **21 July 2026**; the funding strategy on **21 April
2026**. Tuesday is appropriation authority for a decision already taken. The live questions are the
2028 cliff and the 2027/2028 phases, which the narrative says will come "through the 2027 and 2028
annual budget processes" — those are the meetings to be at.

a later CAPER. **The deck does not say how much**, and that is the single most useful question to ask
at the hearing: *how much of the $3,977,040 went to Weaver Creek Commons, and how many units will it
produce?* Without that number the zero is uninterpretable — which is the strongest argument for
asking rather than for quoting the zero.

Also note **Public Services: $0**, against $240,000 estimated for public services in the 2027 round.
Worth asking what happened to the 2025 public-services allocation.

**Scope note.** The CAPER covers the **urban county — Clark County outside Vancouver city limits.**

## Watch

**SCS Engineers, Amendment 6 — and a correction to the earlier version of this brief.**

Read in full (`scs-amend6.txt`, Public Works, Solid Waste & Recycling). It is **Leichner Landfill**,
9411 NE 94th Avenue, Vancouver — a **closed** landfill the county maintains under a post-closure
plan established by **Consent Decree with the Department of Ecology**, permitted under RCW 70A.305.
The earlier brief called landfill monitoring an inference and not a finding; the staff report
confirms it, and it is not connected to the North County transfer station siting study.

**The 156% figure needs correcting.** The earlier brief derived a prior contract total of $1,204,910
from the agenda's two numbers and called the amendment "larger than the entire contract to date." The
arithmetic is right but the framing was wrong, and the staff report says why: the contract runs
**1 December 2023 – 30 November 2028**, and the increase exists because of "**discrepancies
identified during contract review**" — the ceiling was set below what the annual budgets already
authorised. The budget block states "**Action falls within existing budget capacity**," operating and
capital budget impact both **NO**. Funded from **Fund 6310, Solid Waste Closure Fund**. Annual amounts
are set by the **Leichner Landfill Oversight Committee**, which includes City of Vancouver
representatives, and are approved in the county budget each year.

So this is a **paperwork reconciliation, not new spending** — the county has been spending against
annual budgets while the contract ceiling lagged. That is still worth a question (a contract ceiling
that drifts $1.88M below actual expenditure over two and a half years is a controls failure, and
"discrepancies identified during contract review" is doing a lot of work), but it is not $1.88M of
new money and should not be described as such. **The earlier version of this brief and the artifact
overstated it.**

One oddity: the staff report is marked **☒ Consent**, but the agenda lists it under **Separate
Business**. Separate Business is the more visible placement, so the discrepancy runs in the public's
favour here; it still means the routing changed after the report was signed.

**Micromobility grant applications — the earlier read was wrong, and the correction runs the other
way.** Read in full (`micromobility.txt`, Auditor's Office and Public Works). The earlier brief
warned that "safety measures" was undefined and could mean enforcement against riders. **It does not.**
The item is about **county parks, trails and naturalized areas**, not roads, and the named activities
are:

- signage at county parks, natural areas and trails;
- a consultant to develop **youth-focused behaviour-change messaging**;
- educational materials, and summer recreation safety presentations at **up to six of Clark County's
  twelve public high schools**.

No enforcement funding, no citations, no equipment for the Sheriff. Total program cost estimated at
**up to $120,000**, scaled to whatever is raised. Public Works states it "does not have existing
budget or staff capacity" to do any of it unfunded. The Auditor's Office is in it because the
**grants program manager sits there** — that answers the earlier question; it is not an elections or
licensing role.

Approval "demonstrates council's support of the concept and submitting grant applications… It does
not imply approval of an eventual grant agreement" — any actual award returns to Council.

**Two things to keep watching anyway.** First, the **Autzen Foundation letter of interest is due
4 September and the full application 11 September** — three and ten days after the meeting, which is
why this is on the agenda now. Second, and this is the quiet channel: "council has requested a **work
session** on the topic" of motorized micromobility devices, and Public Works "is creating a new
motorized micromobility device policy." **Work sessions take no public comment at all** (Rules §VIII).
The grant funds signage and education; the *policy* — which is where restrictions on where people may
ride would live — is being drafted separately and shaped in the one format with no public way in.
That work session is the thing to watch for on a future Wednesday agenda.
