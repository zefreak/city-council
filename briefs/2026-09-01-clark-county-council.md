## Bottom line

The Council is holding a public hearing to rewrite **its own rules on what the public is allowed
to say to it** — and on the cached draft, the new decorum clause bars remarks concerning the
"professional duties and performance of County employees" and lets **any** Councilor, not just the
Chair, call the point of order that starts a removal.

## Act on this

### 1. Public Hearing — Rules of Procedure (ordinance)

This is the item to show up for. It is a **public hearing**, so testimony is taken: 3 minutes,
once, on the hearing subject, sign in at the meeting or raise a hand on WebEx, state your name for
the record. **You may not yield your time** to another speaker without a majority vote of the
Council (Rules §VII.L.ii, §VII.L.v). Twenty people at three minutes is therefore a different
tactic from one person at an hour.

What is at stake, read from the **draft ordinance already cached in this repo** at
`data/clark-county/clark-rules-of-procedure-2026-07-27-DRAFT.pdf` — an *unadopted* markup, ordinance
number and adoption date both blank, amending Exhibit "A" to CCC 2.09.040:

**a. The decorum rule is being rewritten (§VII.L.vi).** The struck-out old text barred remarks
that are "defamatory to County staff, uncivil, rude, vulgar, profane, or otherwise disruptive."
The replacement bars statements or remarks concerning:

- "private activities, lifestyles, or beliefs of others, including individual County employees or
  elected officials";
- "topics unrelated to the business of the County";
- **"professional duties and performance of County employees or employees of its elected
  officials."**

The third one is the load-bearing change. Criticism of how a department, a program or a named
official actually performs their job is the ordinary substance of public comment at a county
council — it is how a jail medical contract, a permit backlog or a sheriff's deputy's conduct gets
raised in public at all. A rule that puts that category out of bounds is a content restriction on
speech in a forum the Council itself opened, and it is the kind of provision that has drawn First
Amendment litigation elsewhere. **Ask what problem it is meant to solve and get the answer on the
record.** Note that the *stated* trigger throughout remains disruption; whether staff read the
list as an independent prohibition or only as examples of disruption is exactly the ambiguity
worth forcing into the minutes.

**b. Point of order is being widened.** New text: "**Any** Councilor may ask for a point of order
and when recognized the Chair, Vice Chair or their designee may ask any person disrupting the
business of the Council to cease such conduct." Today the interruption starts with the Chair. Four
of five Councilors gaining that trigger changes how easily an unwelcome speaker is stopped.

**c. Two changes that are genuinely good, and should be said so.**

- **Open public comment moves earlier in the Tuesday order of business** — from after the public
  hearings and bid awards to immediately after Amendments to the Agenda. If you have three minutes
  on county business, you will no longer have to sit through three hearings to use it.
- **New rule on Council Time agenda amendments** (a new lettered subsection following §VII.P):
  items may be removed or added by any Councilor or the County Manager on a Council vote, but
  additions "must be time sensitive in nature — whereby it cannot wait until the next scheduled
  Council Time meeting," and — the good part — "**Members of the public may provide public
  testimony on removed agenda items during Public Comment.**" That closes the trick where an item
  is pulled from the agenda and thereby made un-commentable under the agenda-items-only rule.

**Firm / not firm.** That this draft exists, is unadopted, and contains these changes: firm, read
from the cached PDF. That the ordinance being heard on 1 September *is this same draft*: **not
verified** — I could not fetch the Exhibit "A" attached to Tuesday's ordinance. The draft is dated
27 July 2026 and the hearing is five weeks later, so the text may have moved. Read the posted
ordinance before you testify, and if it differs from the above, the ordinance wins.

Testimony taken, same 3-minute rules. This is the year's largest routine money vote and the

county program's funding this year, this is the meeting.

### 3. Public Hearing — 2025 Consolidated Annual Performance Evaluation Report (CAPER)

The CAPER is the county's annual report to HUD on what it actually did with its CDBG and HOME
money — units produced, households served, dollars spent against dollars planned. It is the one
document each year where the county's housing *delivery* is measured rather than projected, and it
routinely passes to an empty room. HUD requires a public comment opportunity before it is
submitted; this hearing is it.

A staff report and a presentation are posted; **I read neither**. The distinction to hold onto
when you do read it, per this repo's standing rule: the CAPER reports **delivery**, not
projection, and the interesting question is always the gap between the two.

`context/` in this repo covers **Vancouver's** Affordable Housing Fund and comprehensive plan, not
the county's CDBG/HOME program — there is nothing in it to check these numbers against, and no
figure from it should be carried over. That is a real gap in the context set.

## Watch

**SCS Engineers, Amendment 6 — Maintenance and Monitoring Agreement.** On Separate Business, so it
gets its own vote and public comment. The agenda states the amendment increases the contract by
**$1,881,300**, to a revised total of **$3,086,210**. That implies a prior contract total of
**$1,204,910** — this amendment is larger than the entire contract to date, a **156% increase**.
(Arithmetic mine, from the agenda's own two figures; firm.) The agenda's justification is that it
aligns the contract "with the previously approved annual budget and project cost adjustments,"
which is a claim that the money was already authorised elsewhere — worth testing, because it is
the standard way a contract growth gets framed as bookkeeping. **What the work is, and at which
site, is not stated on the agenda and the staff report is unread.** SCS Engineers is an
environmental/solid-waste engineering firm; that this is landfill or transfer-station
post-closure monitoring is an inference and not a finding. Same week the Council took a work
session on the North County Recycling and Transfer Station siting study — whether the two are
connected is unverified.

**Micromobility safety grant applications** — Separate Business. Authorises the **Auditor's Office
and Public Works** to apply to the Autzen Foundation, BNSF Railway Foundation "and other nonprofit
organizations" for funds "in support of implementing safety measures related to motorized bicycle,
scooter and other micromobility devices." Two things unresolved and both matter:

- **"Safety measures" is not defined on the agenda.** Protected infrastructure and enforcement
  against riders are both "safety measures," and they point opposite directions for anyone who
  gets around on an e-bike because a car is not affordable. The staff report would say which; it
  is unread.
- **Why the Auditor's Office?** The Auditor runs elections, recording and licensing. Its presence
  on a road-safety grant application is not obvious from the agenda and I will not guess at it.

BNSF Railway money funding county safety programming is worth noting on its own terms — a
private railroad's foundation buying goodwill in a county it runs freight through.
