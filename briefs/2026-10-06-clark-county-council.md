## Act on this

### Separate Business 1 — HB 2060 housing benefit funds interlocal (Community Services)

**What the staff report says** (doc 237586, dated 6 October, signed 22 September):

- The agreement is between the County and Battle Ground, Camas, La Center, Ridgefield, Vancouver,
  Washougal, Woodland (Clark County portion only) and Yacolt. It governs the *"Affordable Housing For
  All Surcharge"* collected by the Auditor on recorded documents (RCW 36.22.178).
- It *"supersedes earlier agreements … by **removing outdated capital and bond-related allocations**
  and prioritizing funds for **shelter operations, transitional housing, and permanent housing**,
  guided through a public grant-application process and recommendations from the Community Action
  Advisory Board (CAAB)."*
- Eligibility stays at households **at or below 50% of area median income**. The County keeps
  collecting and holding the money, distributes it annually, and files the annual reports. A city can
  withdraw on 180 days' notice.
- **Term: 1 January 2026 to 31 January 2031.** It is retroactive by nine months.
- The draft went to the elected officials on ECHO on 30 June. *"No edits or recommended changes were
  made."*
- Budget: *"Action falls within existing budget capacity."* Fund 1937. **The dollar amount is
  blank.**

**The left read.**

- **Probably good.** Money for operating shelters and transitional housing is the hardest money in
  the system to find. Construction money comes from the state Housing Trust Fund, LIHTC and city
  levies. Operating money mostly does not. Moving a local, flexible stream onto operations fits where
  the gap is. The 50% AMI floor is kept.
- **But "removing … bond-related allocations" is the line to ask about.** If part of the surcharge was
  paying debt service on bonds that built housing, something has to pay that debt now. If it was
  reserved for future capital, then permanent-housing *construction* loses a local source, and the
  comp plan already has no funding plan for its 7,491 permanent supportive housing units (Exhibit 4,
  Table 2; see the 1 October brief). Which of the two it is cannot be told from the staff report.
- **Shelter operations versus permanent housing is a real trade-off.** The staff report lists both
  as priorities and sets no split. CAAB recommends the split each year, so the fight moves to CAAB's
  grant round, which gets much less attention than a Council vote.

**What to ask, in three minutes or in writing:**

1. **How much does the surcharge raise a year, and how much of it goes through this agreement?**
2. **What were the "capital and bond-related allocations" being removed?** Do any of them carry
   outstanding debt?
3. **Publish the agreement.** It is a five-year compact on the region's homelessness money, and only
   a cover memo is on the agenda.
4. **Set a floor for permanent housing**, so that shelter operating costs cannot absorb the whole
   stream in a bad year.

**Firm vs not.** Everything above is from the staff report. The agreement text was not attached and
was not read. No figure for the surcharge's annual yield was checked; none is in the packet.
