## Watch

### 1. Fall Budget Supplemental: 152 change requests, and a forecast that turns negative

**The supplemental itself** (deck, slides 5 and 8): 152 change requests, all recommended. The General
Fund operating budget rises **$14,067,560** against $3,069,274 of new revenue. A $7.6M capital
underspend offsets part of that, for a **net one-time draw of $4,463,598** on fund balance. Ongoing,
it costs **$199,119 a year** from 2027. *Which* requests make up the $14M is in reports that are not
yet posted.

**The six-year General Fund forecast with these recommendations** (slide 8, labelled
*"subject to change"*):

| | 2026 | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 | 2031 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating revenue | $227.6M | $221.9M | $226.9M | $232.0M | $237.4M | $243.0M |
| Operating expense | $244.8M | $238.6M | $245.4M | $252.3M | $259.5M | $267.0M |
| **Unassigned balance available for requests** | **$12.0M** | **$3.4M** | **−$15.3M** | −$35.8M | −$58.1M | **−$82.3M** |

The balance stays above the **$36.1M** minimum required by policy only until 2027. The Q3 report on
the Council Time agenda (see that brief) shows the same pressure from the other side: year-to-date
General Fund **expenses up 11.0%** against **income up 4.4%**, with CPI at 3.4%.

**Read this carefully before quoting it.**
- It is a **forecast**, built from a baseline, not a deficit that has happened.
- The deck **does not say whether Prop 13's levy lid lift is in it.** If it is not, this is the
  "measure fails" picture. That makes it a campaign-relevant document, and it should be read as one.
- **The Trends Report says the opposite about the past.** The 2025 Financial Trends Monitoring Report
  (on the Council Time agenda) finds unassigned fund balance has *"trended slightly upward over the ten
  years, indicating high flexibility"*. Both can be true. The decade was fine, and the outlook is not.
- **The structural cause is in the Trends Report too.** Inflation-adjusted General Fund revenue per
  person fell from **$455 in 2016 to $396 in 2025, "a 10-year decrease of nearly 13%"**, because
  *"population growth has exceeded revenue growth"*. Property tax is 58% of tax revenue, and the
  state's 1% levy limit caps its growth (context: `context/HOUSING-vancouver-wa.md` §8 on the same
  lid for Vancouver). **That is a statutory squeeze, not overspending**, and the left case for
  progressive state revenue options starts there.

**The left-critical read.** A shrinking General Fund is where public defense, juvenile diversion and
public health compete with the jail (7 October brief). The forecast will be used to justify cuts in
2027–28 whatever happens on 3 November. **The useful ask now:** when the supplemental reports post,
find the line items for Community Services (housing and homelessness) and Public Defense, and comment
in writing before 5pm on 3 November.

**How to comment:** https://clark.wa.gov/councilors/public-comment, or by post to Clark County Council
c/o Rebecca Messinger, PO Box 5000, Vancouver WA 98666-5000. The window is 8am 20 Oct – 5pm 3 Nov
(hearing notice, doc 238916). Send at least 24 hours ahead if you want councilors to have it before
the hearing. Anything for the record must be supplied: a link does not count.

### 2. Columbia River Gorge disaster-recovery code: still good, Council hearing 3 November

The deck matches the Planning Commission version briefed on 15 October. Owners can rebuild on the same
footprint with expedited review, and get **10 years** to start. Temporary structures (up to 320 sq ft)
and RVs are allowed while rebuilding, with ministerial review and a fresh showing of need every two
years. Home businesses are not treated as discontinued while the building is rebuilt, and campground
spaces can stay in use. **Genuinely good:** fire survivors can live on their own land while they
rebuild. The Council hearing is **3 November**.
