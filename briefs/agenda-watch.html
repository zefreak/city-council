Title 20 update: affordability requirements for HB 1491 compliance

The most consequential housing item on any agenda this cycle. Vancouver is deciding how to comply with HB 1491's affordability mandate near transit, and staff have already argued on the record that the law is "partially funded inclusionary zoning." This hearing is where the size of the carve-out gets set.

HB 1491 (2025) requires 50-year affordability within a quarter-mile of Bus Rapid Transit stations — 10% of units at 60% AMI, or 20% at 80% AMI, or 10% at 80% AMI where a tenth are two-bed or larger. Cities must offer offsets: a 20-year MFTE exemption, a 50% impact fee reduction, minimum FAR, density bonuses, no parking minimums.

Vancouver has 51 stations across two existing Vine BRT routes — one of few jurisdictions outside Puget Sound with high-capacity transit, so a law written for Sound Transit light rail lands unusually hard here.

From the 10 August council deck: staff argue 50 years of required affordability against at most 20 years of offset, that the other incentives are already available citywide and so offset nothing at the margin, and that the result "decreases overall production and directs development away from transit." Their proposed approach is exclusions — downtown and other minimum-FAR areas, plus roughly 25% of parcels near stations.

Worth engaging rather than dismissing: the production argument is about volume, while HB 1491 targets tenure and depth. Trading one for the other is a choice, not an arithmetic result.

0.1% criminal justice sales and use tax

Adds VMC 5.98.028 under RCW 82.14.345, created by HB 2015 (2025). Collection begins 1 January 2027. A regressive consumption tax funding police — and it stacks: the ordinance's own recitals confirm Vancouver already receives distributions under RCW 82.14.450 and levies a criminal justice tax under RCW 82.14.340. This is a third, not a replacement.

It is also a grant qualification. Sections 1.A.1–10 are findings that VPD meets the Local Law Enforcement Grant Program conditions — use-of-force and de-escalation policy, use-of-force data reporting, firearm relinquishment practice, 25% of officers through 40-hour crisis intervention training, and a staffing plan filed with the Criminal Justice Training Commission covering co-response teams, 911 response rate and case closure rate. That 25% is the floor the state accepts, not an achievement.

Not the same as the TBD transportation 0.1% sales tax. Two different 0.1% taxes are live in Vancouver, and the city's own documents have described the TBD one as 0.01% — it is 0.1%.

Consent — no debate unless pulled

Affordable Housing Fund plan revisions

Seven changes to the 2024–2033 AHF Administrative & Financial Plan, and they are not housekeeping: higher per-unit funding limits; predevelopment loans capped at 25% of the award; tailored applications by program type; people exiting institutions without a housing plan added as a priority population — a good change; a clarified shared-housing rent formula; and final cost certification at completion.

The one to look at is the Bridge Shelter set-aside. Temporary Shelter is $1M/yr, of which $400k is set aside for the City's own Bridge Shelter, and the plan states that AHF outcomes are calculated on "the $600k available for other providers." The published goal of 33 beds/yr is measured against $600k while $1M is spent. That may be defensible; it should not pass on consent without someone saying it out loud.

Vancouver Heights Senior Housing — disposition and development agreement

Heights District Site P, E. Mill Plain Blvd at Devine Rd. Developer selected by March 2024 RFP: Related/Northwest Development LLC with the Vancouver Housing Authority. On the merits this is a good deal, and the brief should say so.

The City keeps the land. A 99-year ground lease, not a sale — no rent for 30 months, 3% annual increases, discounted 95% for 40 years, recalculated by appraisal in year 41 with affordability renewed. Affordability runs at least 40 years.

The subsidy, from the City's own presentation: land valued at $676,000; NPV of a market-rate ground lease over 40 years $933,596; NPV of the discounted lease $58,364. The City forgoes roughly $875,000 of ground rent over 40 years for 51 units, 11 of them at 30% AMI. Retaining the freehold is the part worth defending.
