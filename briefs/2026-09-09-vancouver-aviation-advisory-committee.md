# Vancouver Aviation Advisory Committee — Wednesday 9 September 2026, 6:00pm

4500 SE Columbia Way, Marine Park Engineering Building; hybrid via Teams. Agenda and both
attachments read. Cached at `data/vancouver/2026-09-09-aac/`.

## Bottom line

A one-page redline on the agenda would rewrite the Aviation Advisory Committee's charter to
**cut it from nine members to seven, delete its power to review airport grants, delete its
general financial oversight of the airfield, hand the airport manager the power to set its
agenda and call its meetings, and make its own-initiative items subject to the airport
manager's approval.** It arrives with **no staff report** — the redline is the only document —
at the same meeting where the committee nominates a representative to the Pearson Field Master
Plan advisory committee, a plan funded by the FAA and WSDOT grants the committee would no
longer review.

## Act on this

### Public comment is taken, and this is the only meeting at which it is an agenda item

"Community Communication – Public Comment" is item 3. The City's CivicClerk portal reports
`enablePublicSpeakerSignup: false` and `enableWrittenComment: false` for this meeting — that is
the *portal's* signup being switched off, not the comment period. Comment is taken at the
meeting, in person at Marine Park or on the Teams link in the agenda. There is no published
pre-registration address for this committee and no written-comment deadline, which is itself
worth asking about.

A committee cannot amend the municipal code. **VMC 10.05.040 can only be changed by City Council
ordinance**, so this meeting is the shaping stage, not the decision — which per §4 of the brief
prompt is exactly the stage where the decision actually gets made. Nothing on the agenda says
when it goes to Council.

### Powers added or clarified

Not everything runs one way, and the brief is worth less if it pretends otherwise:

- The public works director is added to the list of officials the AAC advises.
- §A.2 broadens from "any **economic development** projects" to "any **land use** projects with
  potential to affect the airport" — a wider hook on zoning near Pearson, which matters given the
  comp plan.
- §A.4 adds **renovation** to construction and removal; §A.5 adds **updates** to plans, not just
  development of them — which on its face covers the master plan update now under way.
- A new sentence states plainly that the AAC's duties are advisory only and that it cannot
  contract or incur liability on the City's behalf. That is a true statement of the existing law
  and is not a reduction of anything.
- Shrinking nine seats to seven partly ratifies reality: the 12 August minutes record **six**
  members, and six was the full roll call.

## The left read

**The pattern is not the seat count, it is who sets the agenda.** A body that meets when the
airport manager calls it, on subjects the airport manager picks, and can only take up its own
business with the airport manager's approval, is not an oversight body. Each individual edit is
defensible; together they move Pearson Field from "city asset with a lay committee looking at the
leases and the grants" to "department programme with a stakeholder group."

**The grant clause is the sharpest one.** §A.10 currently requires the AAC to review *all* airport
grants before application and before Council acceptance. Deleting it removes the only lay review
between a federal grant application and a Council consent vote — and the timing is not neutral.
The 12 August minutes record that Council has just approved an **FAA grant, a WSDOT grant and the
Century West contract** for a Pearson Field Master Plan with an estimated **18–24 month** run,
with the kickoff meeting two weeks out. The committee is being asked to nominate its
representative to that plan's advisory committee at this same meeting, under item 2 of the action
items, with no attachment describing what that seat is.

**"Best interests of the airport" is a loyalty clause, and §E already allows removal by a
majority of Council.** Adding a duty to work "respectfully" to "advance the best interests of the
airport" gives a removal vote a hook it does not currently have. A neighbourhood representative —
a seat this section still requires — objecting to airport noise or expansion is doing their job
and would now be arguably out of compliance with §B.

**Deleting the National Park Service clause has a geography behind it.** Pearson Field sits inside
the Vancouver National Historic Reserve, adjoining Fort Vancouver National Historic Site, and the
airport and the Park Service have a genuinely entangled history. §A.6 is the only place in the
chapter that names that relationship. Nothing in the packet says why it goes.

**The counter-case, stated fairly.** Committees that can call their own meetings and set their own
agendas can also stall a department, and an airport with six volunteers and a two-year master plan
has a real coordination problem. "Advisory only" is already the law. And an airport tenants'
committee with financial oversight of the leases those same tenants hold is a live conflict — the
tenant cap exists precisely for that, and the redline tightens it. A reader who wants the master
plan to move can support most of this. The three edits that should not survive on any reading are
the **grant review deletion**, the **airport-manager veto on the committee's own business**, and
the **loyalty clause**.

## Money and land

No dollar figures in this packet. The money context is in the 12 August minutes:

  waitlist policy is being redrafted to add a **non-refundable deposit and annual renewal fees** —
  a charge on people waiting for a public facility, discussed in April and August with no policy
  document published.

## What I could not check

- **Why any of this is proposed.** There is no staff report, no memo and no ordinance draft — the
  redline is the entire record. Every reading of intent above is inferred from the text.
- **When it goes to City Council**, and whether it goes as an ordinance amending VMC 10.05.040 or
  as part of a wider boards-and-commissions rewrite. Nothing in the packet says.
- **Whether other advisory bodies are getting the same treatment.** A single-committee change and a
  city-wide template are different stories and this document cannot tell them apart.
- **The FAA and WSDOT grant amounts**, and the Century West contract value. Named in the August
  minutes without figures; not retrieved.
- **The hangar waitlist policy text**, current or proposed. Discussed at two meetings, published at
  neither.
- **Whether the redline's own numbering survives.** It renumbers §A.7–9 to 6–8 but leaves the
  deleted §A.10 unnumbered; a clean draft has not been published.
