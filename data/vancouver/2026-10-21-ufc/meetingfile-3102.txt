Urban Forestry Work Plan 2027-28:
The Urban Forestry Work Plan is periodically updated to coincide with the biennial
budget. Staff will begin updating the 2025-26 Work Plan for 2027-28 reflecting new
program elements. The Commission is encouraged to review the 25-26 Work Plan and
share any ideas and suggestions with staff. Staff anticipate sharing a draft with the
Commission at the November meeting.
2027-28 Budget Prep
The city is anticipating a budget shortfall in the General, Street and Fire Funds during
the 2027-28 budget cycle. Urban Forestry does not receive any funding from these three
funds as the program is funded by stormwater fees. UF staff does not anticipate
requesting additional funding except to renew a three-year software subscription that
would be paid in 2027. The city is required to have an approved budget by fall of 2026
for the 2027-28 cycle.

9. Urban Forestry Staff Report
Since the September Commission meeting, staff have been focused on the Old Apple
Tree Festival, fall projects, implementing the new code as part of the Comprehensive
Plan which went into effect at the end of July; response to Emerald Ash Borer, next steps
with our Urban Forest Management Plan and our workload to ensure all aspects of our
program are moving forward.

Emerald Ash Borer (EAB) identified in Vancouver area August 2026
No new EAB sightings in the Vancouver area. Staff are working on outreach, our next
round of ash removals and next spring ash treatments.

Washington Department of Natural Resources (DNR) Grant 2026
Vancouver Urban Forestry’s grant application to help support the community’s response
to EAB specifically targeting ash trees on school sites was approved by DNR.

Proactive Street Tree Program
The proactive street tree program continues to move forward in coordination with
Transportation’s Pavement Management program. UF Staff are working on replanting
plans for the streets serviced in 2026 which will be implemented as part of our fall
projects and winter 2027 projects. It is anticipated this program will reduce storm
damage in the right of way, address social/environmental justice, and improve climate
resilience.

Tree Canopy Assessment for Stormwater Management
Staff has been working with Surface Water staff to complete a tree canopy assessment
using 2025 summer data. The city selected Plan IT Geo to complete the project. The goal
is to identify how existing or future tree canopy can support stormwater management
and improve water quality to improve watershed health. Conducting a tree canopy
assessment in 2026 is the optimum time to support watershed management and water
quality improvement activities required in Vancouver’s 2024-2029 National Pollutant
Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) Stormwater Permit. The report should be
completed by December 2026.

Urban Forestry’s site visit tree inspection backlog is approx. 7 days (goal is 10 day
response time) and completed 94 site visit tree inspections during the month of
September. Development and final development inspection requests are steady.
