The purpose of this memo is to provide additional information and context to the City
Council in response to conversation and questions that were highlighted during the
preview of the City Manager’s Recommended Budget on September 21 st, 2026.

Public Safety Sales Tax – Usage and Sufficiency

The Council authorized the City to enact a 0.1% Public Safety Sales and Use Tax
pursuant to RCW 82.14.345 on August 24, 2026. The new tax goes into effect January 1,
2027.

         In the City Manager’s Recommended Budget, the Public Safety Sales Tax supports
          the ongoing expense of the 16.0 new officer positions, associated equipment and
          the Real-Time Information Center that are proposed to be initially funded by the
          ESHB 2015 2015 grant, if awarded.

         In addition to the costs mentioned above, the Public Safety Sales Tax will also
          fund an increase in public defense support, additional safety equipment, and
          technology upgrades for the Police Department.

During the work session, there was discussion about ensuring the City does not
overcommit expenses to this new resource. The potential overlap of these two resources
(Public Safety Sales Tax and HB 2015 grant) during the biennium creates an opportunity
for use of the Public Safety Sales Tax to support one-time public safety initiatives during
2027 but does not change the need to analyze the sufficiency of ongoing sales tax
revenues against new ongoing public safety expenses.

The best available data to draw on to estimate 2027 and 2028 Public Safety Sales Tax is
the City’s current year-to-date collections for the Cultural Arts Tax, which applies the
same 0.1% sales tax rate.

      Year-to-Date Cultural Arts Sales Tax (January – June) collections were $3.48
       million and are currently projected to end the year at $6.97 million.

      In comparison, Public Safety Sales Tax revenues are budgeted at $6.74 million in
       2027 and $7.05 million in 2028.

      On the expense side, the Recommended Budget allocates $6.5 million in
       expenses to the Public Safety Sales Tax in 2028. This means that the sales tax is
       under-allocated relative to current projections by about $0.5 million.

By leaving some of this resource unallocated, the City has the flexibility to A) respond if
revenues do not materialize as anticipated, or B) allow for the fact that expenses will
grow faster than revenues in the later years of the forecast period.

The City will continue to monitor revenue projections against actual performance and
provide the Council with quarterly updates.

Information on New Positions

As of 2028, the City Manager Recommended Budget includes 1,430.2 full-time
equivalent (FTE) positions, representing a 2.29% increase from the 2026 authorized FTE
count. This includes 41.0 net new regular positions and the extension of 5.0 limited term
positions, included with the goal of advancing Council priorities and ensuring efficient
City operations.

      The majority of new permanent positions – 23.0 FTE - are added in the Police
       Department. These are supported through a combination of new public safety
       revenues (Proposition 5, Public Safety Sales Tax) and programmatic revenues.

A full report on new FTE additions with position descriptions and service level impacts
can be found in the appendix. New positions are costed for 9 months in 2027 and 12
months in 2028, on the assumption that recruitment of new positions will take some
time.

Information on the Parks Deputy Director position is in the FTE report, though this is
proposed as a reclassification of an existing vacant position rather than a net new
position.

Youth Civic Engagement

The Council has expressed a goal for the City to increase youth civic engagement.
During the preview of the City Manager’s Recommend Budget, a decision point was
raised for Council around the inclusion of a 1.0 Program Coordinator FTE for the
establishment and ongoing support for a new Youth Advisory Council.

This coordinator position, which has an estimated annual cost of $137,000, is not
included in the City Manager’s Recommend Budget. This position can be added to the
budget; if that is Council’s preferred approach City staff will identify budgetary offsets
for Council consideration. This Youth Advisory Coordinator position, which would
require a specific skill set and focus on youth, is distinct from the new 1.0 FTE Boards
and Commissions Coordinator that is included in the Recommended Budget.

The City’s Engagement & Access Director has developed a cost-neutral approach to
enhance youth representation in City matters for Council consideration. The proposal
would take a phased approach to adding youth membership to select existing boards
and commissions in the coming years, and can be achieved within existing staff
resources. Importantly, this approach would exclude some boards and commissions with
specific regulatory, financial, or specialized natures. The proposal is shared in full as an
attachment.

Children and Family Sales Tax - Withdrawn
The Council had questions about the allowability of the use of this sales tax to fund
existing programming at the Children’s Justice Center and had overall concerns about
the financial burden of a new tax on City residents.

At this time, the Children and Family Sales Tax has been fully withdrawn from the City
Manager’s Recommended Budget. The biennium budget remains balanced, but the
operating deficit in the outyears of the forecast has worsened slightly without the
assumption of this revenue.

Given the realities of the City’s structural operating deficit, City staff will consistently
identify new potential revenue streams for Council consideration. Staff will follow up in
the future with a more comprehensive legal analysis of allowable uses, and an updated
revenue estimate for Council consideration.

City Utility Tax Increase and Rate Comparison
The City Manager’s Recommended Budget includes a recommendation to increase the
City Utility Tax rate from 28.9% - the rate since 2020 - to 28.99%.

State law allows cities to levy a tax on public utilities provided to community members
and businesses. The Utility Tax is a charge on gross revenue generated by Water, Sewer,
Stormwater and Solid Waste utilities. This tax is recognized as revenue to the City
General Fund. The Utility Tax is included in monthly utilities charges to customers and is
not itemized separately on customer bills.

The proposed increase is expected to increase General Fund revenues by $218 thousand,
and under the Recommended Budget would support a 1.0 FTE to focus on execution of
Climate Action Framework goals.

Boards and Commissions: Implementation of a Phased Youth Representation
Initiative (2027–2029)

Executive Summary
This proposal outlines a strategic, cost-effective framework to increase youth civic
engagement by integrating young adults (ages 20–30) into the City’s Boards and
Commissions. By adopting a phased implementation model, the City can foster
intergenerational representation and ensure policy decisions reflect the perspectives of a
critical demographic while optimizing existing administrative resources.

Proposed Initiative:
The Engagement and Access Department proposes 2/3 of the existing Boards and
Commissions (not including the exclusions below) open at least one (1) of their upcoming
recruitments to a youth member (ages 20–30) over a two-year period, from 2027 to 2028
(the next Biennium).

This approach is designed as an alternative to the creation of a full-time "Boards and
Commissions Youth Coordinator" position, leveraging existing departmental infrastructure
to maintain fiscal responsibility.

Implementation Timeline
To ensure successful integration and adequate support, the initiative will follow a phased
rollout:

   •   Phase 1 (2027): Outreach strategy development, recruitment infrastructure setup,
       and initial recruitment and appointments to the ﬁrst 1/3 of eligible boards.
       Approximate total: 5
       (Note: The Transportation and Mobility Commission already has a youth seat)
   •   Phase 2 (2028): Evaluation of Phase 1, reﬁnement of onboarding processes, with
       recruitment and appointments to the second 1/3 of eligible boards. Approximate
       total: 6
   •   Phase 3 (2029): Validate appointments have reached the target of 2/3 of total
       eligible boards, followed by a comprehensive review of the program’s long-term
       sustainability.

Exclusions
The following Boards and Commissions are excluded from this initiative due to their
specific regulatory, financial, or specialized nature:
* City Center Redevelopment Authority (CCRA)
* Downtown Redevelopment Authority (DRA)
* Cultural Arts and Heritage Commission (CAHC)
* Public Facilities District (PFD)




                                                                                        Page 43 of 169
Operational Strategy and Departmental Support:
To ensure this program is sustainable without the need for a dedicated coordinator, the
Engagement and Access Department will provide oversight and support.

   •   Quarterly Engagement: The Engagement and Access Department will facilitate
       quarterly check-ins with youth members. These sessions will provide a platform for
       feedback, mentorship, and peer networking, ensuring that youth members feel
       supported and empowered to contribute effectively.
   •   Administrative Efficiency: By utilizing the existing capacity of the Engagement and
       Access Department, the City mitigates costs associated with new staff recruitment
       and salary expenditures, while still achieving a higher standard of community
       representation.

Benefits
* Diverse Perspectives: Infusing Boards and Commissions with the voices of residents
aged 20–30 ensures that city policy considers the unique needs and future-oriented
outlook of this demographic.
* Leadership Development: This initiative acts as a pipeline for future civic leaders,
providing young professionals with hands-on experience in city government.
* Fiscal Responsibility: This model prioritizes the optimization of existing departmental
resources over the expansion of the existing workforce.

Conclusion
Integrating youth voices into our Boards and Commissions is essential for the continued
growth and vibrancy of Vancouver. This phased, structured approach provides a clear path
toward greater inclusivity while remaining sensitive to the City’s budgetary priorities.

19.15.010       Creation of Scofflaw List.
A.     A “Scofflaw List” is a list of vehicles, created and maintained by the City of
Vancouver or its designee, that have been issued four (4) or more parking tickets
or citations that remain unpaid for forty-five (45) days after the issuance of the
ticket or citation.

B.     As frequently as practicable, City staff, working in conjunction with the
collection agency contracted by the City of Vancouver or other designee, shall
prepare, maintain, and update the Scofflaw List.

C.     Notice

       1.     The City of Vancouver or its designee shall, prior to adding a vehicle
to the Scofflaw List, provide notice that the vehicle will be added to the Scofflaw
List. Such notice shall comply with the following requirements:

              a.      Notice shall be sent by first class mail to the last known
registered owner of the vehicle, as determined by the vehicle license number
and/or Vehicle Identification Number (VIN).

              b.      Notice shall be sent to the last known address of the registered
owner as provided by the Washington State Department of Licensing or
equivalent vehicle licensing agency of the state in which the vehicle is or was
most recently registered.

              c.      Notices shall include the following information:




                                                                                     Page 59 of 169
                    i.     The date and nature of each ticket or citation overdue
        and the amount due on each,

                    ii.    The total amount currently due,

                    iii.   A specific deadline for response, no less than thirty (30)
days after the date of mailing,

                    iv.    That the owner must, by said deadline, respond to the
notice by paying the total amount due, arranging a payment schedule with the
City or its contracted collection agency for payment of the total amount due, or
schedule a hearing to contest the vehicle’s eligibility to be added to the Scofflaw
List,

                    v.     That, if the vehicle owner fails to respond as described
by said deadline, the listed vehicle will be added to the Scofflaw List, and

                    vi.    That, if the vehicle is added to the Scofflaw List, it will be
subject to immediate immobilization and subsequent impoundment pursuant
to the procedures set forth in VMC 19.15.020 (Immobilization) and VMC
19.15.030 (Impoundment) until the vehicle is removed from the Scofflaw List.

        2.    If the City of Vancouver or its designee, after exercising due diligence,
fails to discover any mailing address, then notice will be deemed sufficient if it is
posted on the vehicle, personally served to the vehicle owner or driver, or
provided by any other means reasonably calculated to provide notice to the
vehicle owner or driver.

D.      When the owner of a vehicle receives notice that their vehicle will be added
to the Scofflaw List pursuant to subsection (C), they may prevent their vehicle
from being added to the Scofflaw List by doing one of the following prior to the
deadline set forth in such notice:




                                                                                      Page 60 of 169
      1.     Pay the total amount of all outstanding fines and fees due for all
parking tickets or citations,

      2.     Establish a payment schedule with the City of Vancouver or its
contracted collections agency that will result in payment of the total amount of all
outstanding fines and fees due for all parking tickets or citations, or

      3.     Request a hearing to contest the vehicle’s eligibility to be added to
the Scofflaw List.

E.    After the deadline set forth in the notice sent pursuant to subsection (C), a
vehicle may be added to the Scofflaw List under the following circumstances:

      1.     The vehicle’s owner, or their agent, fails to pay the total amount of
all outstanding fines and fees due for all parking tickets or citations,

      2.     The vehicle’s owner, or their agent, fails to establish or comply with a
payment schedule with the City of Vancouver or its contracted collections agency
that will result in payment of the total amount of all outstanding fines and fees
due for all parking tickets or citations,

      3.     The vehicle’s owner fails to request or appear for a hearing to
contest the vehicle’s eligibility to be added to the Scofflaw List, or

      4.     A determination that the vehicle was properly determined eligible to
be added to the Scofflaw List following a hearing contesting the same.

F.    A $25 administrative fee will be assessed when a vehicle is added to the
Scofflaw List pursuant to VMC 19.15.010(E). Such fee will be added to all other
outstanding fines and fees from parking tickets or citations associated with the
vehicle.




                                                                                     Page 61 of 169
G.    After a vehicle is added to the Scofflaw List, it may only be removed from
the list following the payment in full of all outstanding fines and fees from parking
tickets or citations associated with the vehicle.

H.     Any parking ticket or citation that remains unpaid for forty-five (45) days
after issuance to a vehicle currently on the Scofflaw List, or remains unpaid for
forty-five (45) days after it has been found to be committed after appeal, shall be
automatically added to the total balance of fines and fees that must be paid in full
pursuant to the requirements of subsection (G) prior to removal from the Scofflaw
List.


19.15.020    Immobilization
A.     An “Immobilization device” means a device that immobilizes a vehicle by
either clamping and locking to a wheel impeding movement of the vehicle, or by
attaching to the vehicle's windshield in such a manner to obscure the view of the
driver, thereby preventing legal operation of the vehicle.
B.      A law enforcement officer, including fully and specially commissioned
officers, or their designee, may immobilize a vehicle that is on the Scofflaw List by
installing an immobilization device designed to restrict the normal movement of
the vehicle.
C.    The person installing the immobilization device shall leave under the
windshield wiper or otherwise attach to such vehicle a notice advising the owner
that:
       1.    Such vehicle has been immobilized by the City of Vancouver for failing
to pay four (4) or more parking tickets or citations within forty-five (45) days of
their issuance,
      2.     That the release of the immobilization device may be obtained by
paying all outstanding fines and fees from parking tickets or citations associated
with the vehicle,




                                                                                   Page 62 of 169
      3.     That unless such payments are made within two (2) business days of
the date of notice, the vehicle is subject to impound, and
      4.    That it is unlawful for any person to remove or attempt to remove the
immobilization device, damage the device, or move the vehicle while the device is
attached.
D.    The owner of every vehicle immobilized pursuant to this section will be
assessed a $50.00 immobilization fee. Such fee will be added to all other
outstanding fines and fees from parking tickets or citations associated with the
vehicle.

E.     Any individual who tampers with, damages, attempts to remove, or does
remove any immobilization device without authorization shall be liable for all
costs associated with repair, replacement, and/or reinstallation of the device.
F.     No parking restriction otherwise applicable to the vehicle applies while the
vehicle is immobilized by an installed immobilization device under the provisions
of this chapter.
G.     Before the vehicle may be released from immobilization, the vehicle owner
or an agent of the owner shall pay all outstanding fines and fees from parking
tickets or citations associated with the vehicle. Upon such payment, the vehicle
shall be removed from the Scofflaw List, and the immobilization device shall be
promptly removed from the vehicle by a law enforcement officer, fully or specially
commissioned officer, or their designee, during city business hours.

H.     Any parking ticket or citation that remains unpaid for forty-five (45) days
after issuance to a vehicle currently immobilized due to its inclusion on the
Scofflaw List, or remains unpaid for forty-five (45) days after it has been found to
be proper after appeal, shall be automatically added to the total balance of fines
and fees that must be paid in full pursuant to the requirements of subsection (G)
prior to release from mobilization and removal from the Scofflaw List


19.15.030    Impoundment



                                                                                   Page 63 of 169
A.     A law enforcement officer, including fully and specially commissioned
officers, may cause the immediate impoundment of any vehicle that has been
immobilized for two (2) or more business days pursuant to VMC 19.15.020 if the
owner of the immobilized vehicle fails to cause the removal of the vehicle from
the scofflaw list pursuant to VMC 19.15.010(G) within two (2) business days of the
date the vehicle was immobilized.
B.      The owner of any vehicle impounded pursuant to this chapter is responsible
for all costs and fees associated with the impound and storage of said impound.
The impounding and/or redemption of a vehicle impounded pursuant to this
chapter does not result in the removal of such vehicle from the Scofflaw List. Any
vehicle that is impounded pursuant to this chapter and subsequently redeemed
will remain on the Scofflaw List until payment in full of all outstanding fines and
fees from parking tickets or citations associated with the vehicle are made
pursuant to the provisions of this chapter.
C.     When a vehicle is impounded pursuant to this chapter, law enforcement
officers, including fully and specially commissioned officers, shall use the uniform
impound authorization and inventory form provided for by administrative rule by
the Washington State Patrol pursuant to RCW 46.55.075.
D.    The impound of any vehicle pursuant to this Chapter may be contested
pursuant to RCW 46.55.120.

Present Situation
A Transportation Planning study for the Upper Main Street Safety and Mobility project was completed
in September 2024 which provided recommendations that led to the final design of this project.
During the planning study from 2023 to 2024, the City conducted two community engagement phases
to gather community input on traveling experiences and have people provide locations where
improvements could be made throughout the corridor. Outreach included five public workshops and a
public hearing for final design recommendations with the Transportation and Mobility Commission.

This project will implement the Upper Main Street Safety and Mobility study for the portion between E
39th Street and NE 45th Street by replacing one northbound travel lane with a two-way protected
cycle track on the east side of Main Street. The project will also add a two-way protected cycle track
on E 40th Street (between Main Street and F Street) for a safer bike route experience and to connect
to the future F Street Greenway. ADA ramps at 39th and Main, 40th and Main, 39th and F St, and
40th and F St. will be replaced with new ADA-compliant curb ramps and have ADA-accessible push
buttons installed where applicable. North of 45th Street, the project will connect to the existing
Discovery Trail.

                                        Staff Report: 194-26

To:       Mayor and City Council

Subject
Right-of-Way Vacation - SE 1st Street (CTC property)

Key Points

    • Right-of-way vacation of approximately 19,931 square feet of unimproved right-of-way.
    • The right-of-way area is a remnant of the recently completed SE 1st Street corridor
      improvement project. Proposed vacation eliminates unused and unneeded right-of-way.
    • There are no existing utilities within the proposed vacation area.
    • Staff recommends Council require full compensation for the vacated area.


Strategic Plan Alignment
Transportation and Mobility – a safe, future-ready and convenient transportation system.

Present Situation
Staff received a request from CTC Section 30 LLC, which owns the property surrounding the subject
area, to vacate a portion of right-of-way associated with SE 1st Street, located on the northeast
corner of NE 184th Avenue and SE 1st Street. The right-of-way is located in the Southeast Quarter of
Section 30, Township 2 North, Range 3 East, Willamette Meridian.

The vacation area is a portion of the original right-of-way dedicated to the county for SE 1st Street
and used as part of the old roadway. The design and construction of the roundabout at the
intersection with NE 184th Avenue was offset southward from the otherwise linear alignment of the
SE 1st Street corridor. The vacation area was created by this southward offset of the roundabout.

The vacation area now contains no public street improvements. Staff have reviewed the City’s
Transportation Systems Plan and determined the City doesn’t need this right-of-way for future
transportation purposes and vacation would have no adverse impact on the City’s transportation
assets.

                                                                                              Page 96 of 169
Staff contacted all utility owners with facilities potentially located within the subject area. The City has
no public utilities within the area of the proposed vacation. Comcast, Lumen, Ziply Fiber, Clark Public
Utilities (CPU), and NW Natural were contacted and they all verified that their facilities within the
vacation area were moved or abandoned as part of the SE 1st Street improvement project. There will
be no easements reserved within the vacated area.

Generally, under VMC 11.05.130, the property associated with a vacated street belongs to the
abutting property owners, one-half to each, subject to established property rights. In this case, the
vacation area is a remnant of the recently completed SE 1st Street corridor improvement project and
the applicant is the sole owner of the property that borders it. Therefore, the entirety of the vacated
area will be conveyed to the applicant.

RCW 35.79.030 provides for, but does not require, the City to collect compensation for the value of
public right-of-way that is vacated. Further, VMC 11.05.120 authorizes the City to collect
compensation for vacated public right-of-way. The vacation area has been part of the public right-of-
way for at least 25 years. Based on these circumstances, and pursuant to VMC 11.05.120(C), staff
recommends charging 100% of the total property value as a condition for vacating the property.

In October 2023, the applicant agreed to dedicate right-of-way on the south side of SE 1st Street to
accommodate the corridor improvement project that would begin construction the following year. In
exchange for this property dedication, the City issued Transportation Impact Fee (TIF) credits to the
applicant based on a unit price of $13 per square foot. The City has agreed to apply this established
unit price to calculate the value of the requested vacation area. The total area of the proposed
vacation is 19,931 square feet. This results in a total estimated property value equal to $259,103.

Advantage(s)

    1. Allows the applicant to maximize use of the abutting property.
    2. Relieves the City of maintenance and liability obligations for the property.
    3. Eliminates excess and unused right-of-way.


Challenge(s)
None

Budget Impact
Payment of $259,103 to the City’s Transportation fund.

Prior Council Review
None

AN ORDINANCE relating to Comprehensive Plan and Zoning for the City of Vancouver and the
   Vancouver Municipal Code (VMC) Title 20 in matter related to statutory requirements to
   facilitate housing near transit; amending VMC 20.450.030 and 20.530.040; providing for
                        severability; and establishing an effective date.

                                       Staff Report: 195-26

To:       Mayor and City Council
From:     City Manager
Date:     October 5, 2026

Subject
Ordinance Amending Vancouver Municipal Code Title 20 pursuant to House Bill 1491

Key Points

    • House Bill (HB) 1491 includes a range of requirements and incentives intended to facilitate
      affordable housing near transit, including requirements to adopt minimum housing affordability
      thresholds and reduce transportation impact fees near transit.
    • HB 1491 establishes a state model ordinance which effectively imposes its requirements and
      incentives if cities do not adopt local implementing standards.
    • The Planning Commission voted 4-0 to adopt Title 20 changes regarding minimum affordability
      thresholds and transportation impact fees at a September 8, 2026, public hearing with no
      comments received.


Strategic Plan Alignment
Housing and Human Needs – meeting basic needs and partnering with organizations to support the
community

Present Situation
HB 1491 applies in Vancouver’s case to properties within a ¼ mile walking distance of all 51 existing
Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) stations. Within these areas, exemptions from the affordability requirements
are allowed for properties that had previously been zoned to allow Floor Area Ratios (FARs) that
meet or exceed the new HB 1491 FAR requirements of 2.5. Cities may also exempt an additional
25% of otherwise non-exempt properties within each station area if they allow for an additional 0.5
FAR average across station areas. As a result, staff proposes that downtown Vancouver properties
be exempted, along with 25% of properties near other BRT stations. Non-exempt properties subject
to HB 1491 must include one of the following, to be retained for 50 years and enforced by deed or
covenant:

    • At least 10% of the housing units must be affordable for households earning 60% of Area
      Median Income (AMI) for 50 years, or
    • At least 20% of the units affordable at 80% AMI for 50 years, or
    • At least 10% of the units affordable at 80% AMI for 50 years, provided at least 10% of the units
      have two or more bedrooms




                                                                                           Page 109 of 169
Development meeting one of the above may reduce transportation impact fees by 50%.

The proposed changes updating affordability requirements and providing for the impact fee reduction
represent the final components of City compliance with HB 1491. Documentation of compliance with
the other components of 1491 was included in 2026-2045 Our Vancouver Comprehensive Plan,
Appendix V, HB 1491 Compliance Memo.


Vancouver’s deadline for compliance was June 30, 2026, six months after the date of its required
Comprehensive Plan update adoption. Until the City adopts its own standards, it is subject to the
state pre-emptive model code which implements HB 1491 and was adopted by the Department of
Commerce this past June. No local development proposals or inquiries have been submitted in the
impacted properties since that time. No other Washington city has yet been subject to HB 1491,
which applies after a jurisdiction’s first periodic update following the bill’s adoption in 2025. Puget
Sound cities must comply in 2029, and the City of Spokane must comply in 2027.

The City of Vancouver has lobbied for changes to HB 1491 and will continue to do so as the
affordability requirements constitute inclusionary zoning mandates which are not offset by the
incentives, many of which are already included in the newly adopted zoning code. The affordability
requirement will likely discourage development in the areas near transit where it is desired.


Advantage(s)
Adoption of the minimum affordability thresholds and related transportation impact fee reductions is
required by state law.

Challenge(s)
Adopts local standards which, although already effectively in place through the state’s pre-emptive
model ordinance, will likely disincentivize housing in areas where it is desired.

Budget Impact
No significant direct impacts are anticipated.

Prior Council Review
The Council reviewed the full scope of HB 1491 requirements, including Multi-family Tax Exemption
incentives at an August 10, 2026, workshop.

public hearing on October 12, 2026.

               RESOLUTION NO. M- [Resolution Number]
       A RESOLUTION expressing the City Council’s opposition to the passage of

Initiative No. IL26-001, concerning parental rights relating to their children in public

schools, and Initiative No. IL26-638, concerning participation in athletics at K-12 schools,

on the November 3, 2026 General Election Ballot.

       WHEREAS, the Vancouver City Council has identified equity and community

safety as two of its universal policy priorities and in 2021 identified Equity and Inclusion

as one of the five core values for Vancouver as reflected in the 2023-2029 Vancouver

Strategic Plan and believes that all children should be respected, and

       WHEREAS, Initiative No. IL26-001 would re-enact certain rights of parents and

guardians of public school children set forth in Initiative 2081, passed in 2024 and

known as the Washington Declaration of Parental and Legal Guardian Rights, including

the rights to review curriculum materials and student records, receive certain

notifications about medical services being offered or provided or law enforcement

actions, and permitting parental opt-outs of academic activities addressing sexuality,

religion, and mental health, and

       WHEREAS, Initiative No. IL26-001 will force schools to hand over sensitive

information about child abuse victims to their parents but contains inadequate

safeguards in circumstances where those parents may also be abusers, and

       WHEREAS, this initiative does not allow disclosure to parents who are under

active criminal investigation for abuse but does not consider circumstances outside an




                                                                                           Page 152 of 169
active criminal investigation, including the complaint stage or when parents have been

charged with or convicted for raping, assaulting, or otherwise harming their own child

and mandates their access to sensitive information, including private social work and

school counseling records, putting kids at greater risk, and

       WHEREAS, by removing record protections, Initiative No. IL26-001 discourages

students facing domestic violence, sexual abuse or bullying, from seeking help from

trusted school counselors, who would be required to release students’ confidential

information, reducing or altogether eliminating the possibility of confidential support

within the public school environment, thereby leaving LGBTQIA2S+ youth either

unsupported or less likely to seek needed support, and

       WHEREAS, Initiative No. IL26–001 would also require schools to “out” LGBTQ+

kids, and

       WHEREAS, Initiative No. IL26-638 would require all female students, and only

female students, who wish to participate in interscholastic athletics to undertake, as

part of the required routine sports physical examination, additional physical, genetic, or

hormonal tests in order to be determined as not “biologically male,” as defined by the

Initiative’s language, and

       WHEREAS, boys are completely exempt from this requirement – only girls will be

subjected to the medical sex certification and genital exams – putting another barrier

between girls and sports and creating opportunities for potential sexual abuse, and

       WHEREAS, Initiative No. IL26-638’s requirement for female students to verify their

sex violates their privacy, denies recognition of LGBTQIA2S+ students and could increase

stigma and harassment against such students, and could force the “outing” of




                                                                                         Page 153 of 169
LGBTQIA2S+ students to unsupportive families so as to put them at risk of abuse or

homelessness, and

       WHEREAS, residents of the City of Vancouver have expressed concerns to the

Vancouver City Council regarding the effects of Initiative No. IL26-001 and Initiative No.

IL26-638 and called on the Vancouver City Council to oppose them.

       NOW, THEREFORE,

       BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY OF VANCOUVER:

       Section 1. The City Council hereby expresses its opposition to Initiative No. IL26-

001 and Initiative No. IL26-638 on the November 3, 2026 General Election Ballot.
