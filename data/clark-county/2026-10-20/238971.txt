        BACKGROUND
        Clark County Public Works, Solid Waste and Recycling Division entered into a professional services
        agreement with Parametrix, Inc. following competitive selection through RFP #898 to complete a
        multi‑phase study for siting a new solid waste transfer facility in northern Clark County. The study is
        designed to address long‑term capacity needs, traffic congestion, and system constraints identified in the
        Clark County Comprehensive Solid Waste Management Plan (CSWMP) and the Regional Solid Waste
        System Study (RSWSS). The contract was structured to proceed in phases, with each phase initiated only
        upon successful completion of the preceding work.

        Phase I has been completed and resulted in the development of siting criteria, fatal‑flaw screening
        methods, and a numeric ranking system, consistent with CSWMP Appendix M and expanded technical
        criteria from the RSWSS. Phase I identified and screened candidate parcels and finalized a list of seven
        potential sites that meet the required siting and fatal‑flaw criteria. Completion of Phase I satisfies the
        contractual condition for initiating Phase II.

        This amendment authorizes and funds Phase II of the siting study. Phase II will further narrow candidate
        sites and initiate the public engagement and environmental review processes required to advance site
        selection, including work involving the Solid Waste Advisory Commission (SWAC), the Regional Solid
        Waste System Steering Committee (RSWSSC), and the County’s internal siting review committee.

        Phase II Scope of Work:
           • Narrow the four candidate sites to a minimum of two viable locations
           • Develop, finalize, and implement public involvement and communication plans
           • Coordinate public and interested‑party meetings, briefings, and open houses
           • Conduct SEPA environmental and technical analyses
           • Prepare the Preliminary Draft, Draft, and Final Environmental Impact Statement (EIS)

            Introduction
            Based on the findings and recommendations of the Clark County (County) Comprehensive Solid Waste
            Management Plan (CSWMP) and the Regional Solid Waste System Study (RSWSS), the County wishes to
            complete a siting study for a new solid waste management facility to serve the northern region of the
            county. The siting study will identify and evaluate the suitability of sites for the new facility, narrow
            down the list of candidate sites to the two most promising sites, and perform environmental reviews
            under State Environmental Policy Act (SEPA) rules.

            Scope of Work
            Parametrix (the Consultant) will provide siting, architectural, engineering, environmental, and related
            services to the County to site the North County Recycling and Transfer Station (NCRTS).

            Siting a transfer facility is a multi-dimensional, multi-step process based on operational needs, site
            constraints, and the needs and concerns of the service-area communities. The siting process will
            consider these environmental issues:
                    Noise and traffic
                    Economics such as effects on property values and construction costs
                    Equity and social justice considerations

            The project will employ an approach that meets community, financial, and environmental priorities,
            including climate change impacts and sustainability. The facility will be programmed to meet the
            performance standard of immediate occupancy following a natural disaster (essential public facility).

            This scope of work defines the services needed for facility programming, siting, environmental
            assessment, real estate services, land-use permitting, community and interested-party engagement,
            sustainable development, equity and social justice, and property acquisition support for the NCRTS.

            The work defined below is expected to require at least 46 months to complete, as indicated in the
            schedule in Attachment A. The work is divided into four phases.

            Phase I (In Progress)
            Task 1.0 – Data Collection and Analysis
            This task includes work that will inform subsequent facility programming, site identification and
            screening, and environmental review tasks. This task will continue through Phases I and II except as
            noted otherwise.
