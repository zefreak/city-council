        BACKGROUND
        In March 2025, Solid Waste Operations and the Education and Outreach programs were moved from Public
        Health to Public Works. This reorganization shifted responsibilities across both departments and prompted a
        review of solid waste related code language to ensure Clark County Code reflects the current organizational
        structure and state regulatory requirements. As a result, updates are needed to CCC 2.37.030 through
        2.37.040, CCC 24.12.370 through 24.12.380 and CCC 24.12.130.

        Compost procurement
        Clark County adopted the Compost Procurement Ordinance (CPO) in March 2023, as required by RCW
        43.19A.120 and RCW 43.19A.150, to increase the diversion of organic materials from landfills and support the
        use of locally produced compost. Compost procurement requirements in CCC 2.37.030 through 2.37.040
        currently identify Public Health as the department responsible for reporting; the ordinance update assigns this
        responsibility to the Solid Waste and Recycling Division and adjusts reporting deadlines to match current
        Department of Ecology requirements. The reporting and education provisions in CCC 24.12.370 through
        24.12.380 are proposed for rescission because these requirements are already addressed in state law and the
        County’s Comprehensive Solid Waste Management Plan, making them redundant in local code. A countywide
        policy and procedure for compost procurement and reporting is under review and will be finalized once
        updated code language is adopted.

        Recission of the $1.43 tip fee
        The reorganization also affected funding requirements for the solid waste responsibilities that remained
        within Public Health. Clark County Code 24.12.130 established a temporary $1.43 tipping fee in 2017 during a
        period of reduced state funding to support solid and hazardous waste programs. When operations, education,
        and outreach shifted to Public Works in 2025, Public Health retained only solid waste enforcement, prompting
        a reevaluation of program funding. Public Works and Public Health jointly determined that the temporary fee
        is no longer warranted, and rescission of CCC 24.12.130 is now proposed. Both departments are currently
        collaborating on an interdepartmental agreement to ensure continuity of services and programs for the
        regional solid waste system.

            •    The transition of Solid Waste and Recycling to Public Works requires clear coordination between

                 These coordinated administrative processes ensure consistent service delivery and compliance with
                 state funding requirements.
