2027 Budget Submission at a Glance
2027 Budget Environment
Animal Control staffing has not kept pace with the population and pet explosion in recent
years. The need for additional officers and equipment increases with every passing year.
Ensuring that the department receives the needed funding and support in the 2027 Budget
will provide an immediate boost to public perception and staff wellbeing.
Primary Budget Focus for 2027
Approving recommended proposal to change licensing fees will ensure Animal Control can
keep up with the public demand for timely service without having a negative impact on the
general fund.
Key Considerations for Council:
• Ensuring Public Safety
• Equipment needs
• Identifying areas of potential revenue
• Addressing staffing/workload capacity/compassion fatigue/burnout/possible turnover
• Expanding service capacity/catching up with population growth of people and pets
• Approve funding of current Project Position (primary revenue generating)
• National Animal Control Associate (NACA) recommended staffing guidelines

2027 Priority Items for Council Consideration
• PRIORITY 1 — Project Position to FTE COD-05-27AD
  Request: $50,000 FTE | General Fund/Ongoing
• Why it matters: This is the one position that is a revenue generating position.
• Council consideration: The ability to conduct facility inspections and process rabies certificates and
  licensing renewals is not feasible without position this going forward.
• PRIORITY 2 — Budget Intervention 2.5% Reduction Revenue Increase Proposal
  COD-91-27AD
  Impact: $153,458 revenue increase in lieu of cutting expense budget
• Why it matters: Not approving this could mean reducing staffing levels and impact services provided
  to the public.
• Council consideration: By approving an increase in pet licensing, the department would be able to
  cover the expense reduction needed and provide additional revenue to the general fund.
• PRIORITY 3 — Abatement Fund/ COD-02-27AD
  Request: $25,000 General Fund
• Why it matters: Animal Control has been seeing large scale impounds on a yearly basis, the cost of
  providing care/boarding falls on the county during the holding period.
• Council consideration: The ability to provide medical treatment, boarding, food and resources to
  animals taken under a cruelty investigation add up quickly and funds are needed to cover these
  unpredictable costs.
