Priority 1: JUV-03-27AD – Family Services Care Coordinator (PC II)
Request:
• $126,502
• One (1) FTE
• Mental Health Sales Tax Fund 1033, Ongoing
Why it matters:
• Delivering early intervention, restorative inquiry, and community-based support at a
  family's first contact with the juvenile justice system.
•   Offering alternatives to formal charges and detention while filling a critical gap in
    domestic-violence intervention by providing immediate short-term assistance and
    connections to community services.
•   Supports diversion from further system involvement, prevents improper use of secure
    confinement and addresses racial/ethnic disparity at the point of arrest;
Council consideration:
If not funded, the service will not be provided due to limited capacity in existing units.

Priority 2: JUV-02-27AD - Budget Increase for Post-Doctoral Stipend
Request:
• $28,370
• Mental Health Sales Tax Fund 1033, Ongoing
Why it matters:
• To sustain a competitive stipend for the Psychology Post-doctoral position through
  2028, Juvenile Court is requesting an increase to the Non-Procedural Payroll Costs
  budget line, from $99,030 to $133,100.
•   Juvenile Court can contribute $5,700 in underspent funding within the 1033 fund,
    resulting in a request for $28,370 in additional funds to meet the request.
•   This fellowship program increases community capacity for mental health providers
    with experience and focus on adolescent behavioral health
Council consideration:
Without an adjustment to the budget, Juvenile Court will be unable to remain competitive
in recruitment for this essential position.

Priority 3: JUV-01-27AD - Juvenile Detention Alternatives Implementation
(JDAI) Award 2025-2027
Request:
• $14,000
• General Fund, One-time
Why it matters:
This request is for the last six (6) months (Jan 1, 2027 – June 30, 2027) of the Juvenile
Detention Alternatives Initiative (JDAI) grant awarded for July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2027.
The JDAI grant supports Detention Alternatives program needs, training, family
engagement and positive youth development programming.
Council consideration:
If approval is not received, Juvenile Court will end involvement with JDAI losing funding to
support and evaluate detention alternatives, state and national technical assistance for
juvenile justice reform efforts.

Priority 4: JUV-90-27AD – Juvenile Court General Fund Reduction
The department’s requested 2.5% reduction is $291,283. Juvenile Court is proposing a 1.6% reduction
of $186,239, leaving a gap of $105,044 that cannot be met without significant service and staffing impacts.
Proposed reduction represents 26% of the department’s controllable budget. Juvenile Court’s 2027
general fund budget is $11.6 million (4.88% of the county’s general fund).

Impacts by Ledger:
•  Supplies: $17,537 would reduce youth incentives, emergency funds for youth & families and
   program materials across all programs
•   Services: $147,704 in proposed cuts –
     •   Decrease in expense authorization for two grant funded programs totaling $111,761 would
         have minimal impact on the department
     •   Remaining $35,943 reduction would reduce access to diagnostic evaluations, treatment services
         and positive youth development activities
•   Travel/Training: $10,998 would impact mandated training and reduce capacity to maintain best
    practices and meet evolving needs of youth and families
•   Non-Procedural Payroll Costs: $10,000 reduction would have minimal impact; it would eliminate
    our on-call detention officer program, which is no longer viable due to increased training
    requirements and workforce changes
