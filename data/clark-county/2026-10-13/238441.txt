BACKGROUND

Background:
Clark County is required to review and, if needed, revise its comprehensive plan, in accordance with the goals
and requirements of RCW 36.70A (the Growth Management Act, or GMA) by December 31, 2025; and on
August 13, 2026, the Growth Management Hearings Board adopted an amended compliance schedule for the
county’s completion of the plan with a compliance deadline of October 29, 2026.

The county's comprehensive plan is required to include maps and descriptive text covering the objectives,
principles and standards addressing the essential elements of the plan required by RCW 36.70A.070 along
with any additional elements and/or subarea plans prepared pursuant to RCW 36.70A.080. GMA directs
counties to adopt urban growth areas (areas within which urban growth is encouraged and outside of which
only non-urban growth can occur) (“UGAs”) and to address these areas in the countywide planning policies


Review by applicable departments/offices:
The Community Planning Department has coordinated comprehensive plan periodic update plan review
process with numerous affected internal and external departments, offices, and agencies. Details of the plan
adoption process and coordination are contained in the ordinance and associated exhibits, as well as the index
of record. This includes but is not limited to coordination with state agencies such as the Department of
Commerce and Department of Ecology, as well as all of the affected local jurisdictions and the Tribes.

Highlights of this coordination include:
   • State Environmental Policy Act (SEPA) issuance of Final Environmental Impact Statement (FEIS).
   • Department of Commerce 60-day notice of intent to adopt with comments received.
   • Regular meetings with the city and agency partners for plan coordination.

Prior council discussions/direction/approval:
Ordinance 2026-10-01 and the associated exhibits summarize the council’s numerous council times, work
sessions, and public hearings to consider and approve aspects of the plan as it moved through the adoption
process. The council held the final public hearing for consideration of the Planning Commission’s
recommendations on plan on October 1, 2, and 5, 2026. The council’s deliberations and decisions occurred at
that hearing. This ordinance and supporting materials reflect the council’s decisions at the hearing.

COMMUNITY OUTREACH
As summarized in the Public Participation section of the ordinance:

       a. A Technical Advisory Committee comprised of planners from the cities, who met monthly to
          discuss planning issues of a technical nature;
       b. Four work sessions and two public hearings related to the Housing Options Study and Action Plan;
       c. Three rounds of public meetings (May 2024 (scoping); December 2024 (alternatives); and January
          2026 (preferred alternative)) on the environmental impact statement;
       d. 33 work sessions and 6 public hearings before the Planning Commission throughout the update
          process;
       e. 36 work sessions and 17 public hearings before the Council throughout the update process to
          discuss/adopt population and jobs targets, planning assumptions, the vacant and buildable lands
          model, principles and values, EIS alternatives, and various other plan-related items. This included a
          joint hearing with the Planning Commission to discuss the preferred alternative.
       f. Extensive use of the Clark County Community Planning website,
          https://www.clark.wa.gov/community-planning/2025-update,
          that included information on the GMA; an outline of the comprehensive plan update process;
          notices of meetings and hearings; maps of alternatives considered in the SEPA process; meeting
          summaries and issue discussions; the DSEIS; and the FSEIS. In addition, a dedicated email address
          was included to comment on the comp plan as well as an electronic comment form;
       g. Periodic email announcements sent to comprehensive plan e-mail lists containing over 2,250
          addresses;
       h. Periodic press releases on the status of the update process;
       i. Outreach to neighborhood associations and community groups;
       j. Several ‘Clark County Close-up’ segments on the update process on CVTV;
       k. Televised public hearings held by the Planning Commission and the County Council on plan
          adoption.
