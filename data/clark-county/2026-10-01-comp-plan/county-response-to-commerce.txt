Dear Joshua,

As Clark County advances toward the final draft of its 2024/25 Comprehensive Growth management plan,
we appreciate the Department of Commerce’s ongoing efforts to work with Clark County to ensure maximum
legal defensibility for our 2024/2025 Comprehensive Growth Management Plan before entering the final
drafting and legal adoption phases.

While the issuance of Final Environmental Impact Statement on July 24, 2026, provided significant clarity on
our preferred growth alternatives, with your assistance our legal and planning teams are working on
identified specific gaps that require reconciliation to withstand potential Growth Management Hearings
Board Appeals.

To ensure a legally resilient final output, the County is actively working with the cities within our jurisdiction
who have requested expansion and we are conducting targeted audits of the draft plans and documentation
supporting expansion and weighing these against the applicable statutory and administrative code authority.
Clark County is committed to addressing these technical and legal gaps transparently. (See items
addressed below)

In response to paragraph two and three addressing urban growth area expansions and de-designation of
resource lands, we acknowledge further de designations of agricultural lands based solely or primarily on
proximity to urban growth that otherwise meet the definition of agricultural lands historically has not been
found to be defensible by the Growth Management Hearings Board. Towards this end Clark County intends
to demonstrate the evidentiary nexus for urban growth area expansion where it has been requested and we
will ensure that every boundary adjustment explicitly references applicable portions of the 2026 FEIS and
the updated buildable lands report.

Furthermore, to mitigate the impact of land conversions and maintain overall regional balance we will review
policy 1.1.24 to ensure consistency with state statute. We intend to work towards a TDR policy that is
separate from urban growth expansion efforts so that there is an appropriate buffer in preserving resource
lands while accommodating urban growth. In line with the Final Environmental Impact Statement, the County
will explicitly evaluate the cumulative loss of agricultural and forest economies in Clark County resulting from
the proposed UGA expansions rather than evaluating each parcel in isolation.
