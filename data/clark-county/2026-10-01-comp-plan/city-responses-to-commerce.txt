Urban Growth Area Expansions
Commerce notes that WAC 365-196-310 requires UGAs to contain sufficient area and densities to
accommodate the projected 20-year growth and generally not include more land than necessary to
accommodate that growth, including a reasonable land market supply factor. Camas has been
allocated a 2045 population of 37,080, an increase of almost 10,000 residents, or over one-third of the
city’s 2024 population. To accommodate this growth, Camas is required to provide capacity for 4,226
housing units and 11,615 jobs. Additionally, RCW 36.70A.070 requires the city to provide sufficient
housing capacity for all economic segments of the community.

The city’s preferred alternative relies on a combination of rezoning within the existing city limits and
UGA and targeted UGA expansions to accommodate growth. Camas’s plan includes substantial
increases in housing capacity within the existing UGA through higher-density residential zoning, new
mixed-use zoning, expanded allowances for middle housing and accessory dwelling units, and new
redevelopment opportunities in Downtown Camas through the city’s draft downtown subarea plan.

Camas’s middle housing code provides for at least two residential units per lot and up to four units if at
least one unit is affordable. Camas also allows two accessory dwelling units on each residential lot
citywide. We have also removed impact fees and system development charges for accessory dwelling
units and relaxed utility requirements to encourage infill development and lower development costs.

Camas has proposed to increase the amount of multifamily zoning within the city, but has also already
increased development capacity in existing multifamily zones. Developments in multifamily zones can
achieve a 100% density bonus if developed exclusively with apartments. We have also removed
barriers to housing development in our downtown area by relaxing mixed-use requirements and have
seen some success through the multifamily tax exemption program. The city has also increased
employment capacity through new mixed-use and mixed employment zoning that provides for a
broader range of employment uses in zones that were formerly industrial-only, increasing the
potential number of jobs per acre.

Much of the remaining land within the Camas UGA is increasingly parcelized, constrained by critical
areas, access, and infrastructure, or held by landowners who have shown little interest in developing.
Much of the recent development activity has occurred at densities or intensities below those allowed
by zoning. The city has taken meaningful steps over the past decade and as part of its proposed
periodic update to increase housing supply and affordability, but scarcity of available land and the
high cost of available land have limited the extent to which planned capacity has translated into
housing production and affordability.

While Commerce has suggested that the proposed UGA expansions are unnecessary because the VBLM
shows excess capacity, these expansions more accurately provide a necessary land market supply
factor to ensure that the county has adequate land capacity to accommodate forecast housing needs
through 2045. Such a factor is contemplated by WAC 365-196-310. When looking at the County as a
whole, the “excess capacity” accounts for just under 10% of the allocated housing units. The City
believes this approximately 10% margin is a reasonable land market supply factor to ensure that the
County’s projected housing need can realistically be met by 2045.

Grove Field
The 83.79-acre proposed Grove Field expansion area is unique in that it is owned by a public entity, the
Port of Camas-Washougal, and includes an existing operating airport. While currently located outside
of the UGA, the airport exhibits characteristics much more consistent with an urban public facility and
employment use than with the rural or resource lands ordinarily located outside UGAs. The site is
already substantially committed to airport infrastructure and economic activity, including a paved
runway and taxiways, public and private hangars, a fueling facility, a flight school, an aircraft
mechanic, and other airport-related operations.

The proposed land use and zoning designation for the property is Mixed Employment, a jobs-focused
zone that allows a broad range of employment uses, including light industrial, advanced
manufacturing, research and development, office, technology, and compatible artisan or commercial
uses. Inclusion of Grove Field in the Camas UGA would provide the Port of Camas-Washougal with
urban zoning and infrastructure options more appropriate for its long-term operation and
development.

Nevin

UGA. It is located approximately two miles from downtown Camas, one-half mile from Camas High
School, and near existing and planned urban residential development in Camas and Washougal.
Located near the intersection of SE 283rd Avenue and SE Nourse Road, the property would be
designated and zoned for multifamily residential development, and the vacant buildable lands model
(VBLM) demonstrates potential capacity for over 1,200 multifamily residential units on the site. The

Agricultural Resource Land De-designation
Commerce commented that the draft plan did not adequately demonstrate that certain agricultural
lands proposed to be included in UGAs satisfy the de-designation criteria in WAC 365-190-040.
The Nevin property is predominantly designated agricultural and is proposed to be de-designated
through the County’s plan update. We believe the record already contains both a comprehensive
countywide analysis and detailed site-specific information applying the designation criteria to the
property that support its de-designation.

Clark County completed an agricultural lands study specifically as part of the periodic update to
evaluate whether existing agricultural resource land designations remain consistent with state law.
The study was prepared as a technical foundation for potential designation changes and informed the
County’s preferred alternative and specific de-designation proposals. This study, along with a site-
specific analysis prepared for the Nevin property, provides evidence that the requirements of WAC
365-190-040(10)(c) are met.

The Johnson Economics Agricultural Resource Land Analysis evaluated 18 parcels comprising 161.2
acres using the three factors in WAC 365-190-050(3):
    1. Whether the land is characterized by urban growth;
    2. Whether the land is used or capable of being used for agricultural production; and
    3. Whether the land has long-term commercial significance for agriculture.

WAC 365-196-310 recognizes that a UGA may include territory outside a city that is already
characterized by urban growth or is adjacent to territory already characterized by urban growth. The
Nevin property directly adjoins the existing Camas UGA and is in close proximity to existing or
emerging urban development. It is close to established Camas and Washougal urban facilities and
neighborhoods and to areas experiencing continued urban residential development. The fact that the
property is already parcelized into five-acre lots and situated within an increasingly urban setting
further supports consideration of the property for inclusion within the UGA.

The property is not presently functioning as a commercial agricultural operation and has mixed
agricultural capability. The principal feasible agricultural uses identified for the site are hay, pasture,
grasses, and limited grain production. According to the property owners, no crops have been grown on
the property for at least 30 years. Periodic haying has occurred for weed abatement and fire-hazard
mitigation, but no hay has been sold. Of approximately 161 acres, roughly 90 acres are identified as
presently usable for agriculture. Assuming these 90 acres were used for commercial hay production,
the report estimates gross revenue of approximately $54,000 per year before equipment, fertilizer,
labor, and other costs. The Johnson report identifies a substantial disparity between agricultural
economic potential and underlying land costs. Excluding parcels containing homes, the report
identifies an assessed market value of approximately $5.6 million and concludes that acquisition or
use of the land for the limited agricultural operations reasonably available would not be economically
feasible. These findings support the conclusion that the property lacks long-term commercial
significance for agriculture.

Comment (2): Urban Growth Boundary Expansions


Since 2021, the City of Ridgefield has employed multiple zoning strategies aimed at increasing capacity for
housing at different income levels:
    -    Allow accessory dwelling units, townhomes, duplexes, triplexes, and cottage housing by right in all
         low-density and medium-density residential zones.
    -    Allow upper story multifamily units in neighborhood commercial, downtown, and waterfront zones.
    -    Offer density bonuses for single family dwelling units under 1,600 SF, ADUs, cottages, duplexes and
         triplexes, and townhomes in new developments in low-density residential zones.
    -    On existing lots, allow duplexes to count as a half unit and triplexes to count as a third unit for
         density calculations.
    -    Allow two ADUs on residential lots.

These provisions have not yielded middle housing supply at that scale necessary to meet our requirement to
serve different income bands. Since 2021:
    -    263 townhomes have been constructed in medium-density residential zones; none have been
         constructed since 2023. Only two townhome lots have been permitted in exchange for density
         bonuses in low-density residential zones.
    -    Three duplex buildings (six units) and zero triplexes have been constructed. Only six duplex lots
         (twelve units) have been permitted in exchange for density bonuses in low-density residential zones.

Comment (3): De-Designation of Resource Lands


First, while it was not referenced in the Commerce comment letter, it is important to recognize the work
that was done to conduct a countywide agricultural lands study required by WAC 365-190-050. Beyond the
relevance to the FEIS and site-specific decisions, the City has been consistently supportive of the inquiry to
truly identify and protect farming as a natural resource industry contemplated in Goal 8 of the GMA. The
2025 study contains information that – when read in full and all criteria applied to site-specific de-
designation requests – can help appropriately guide these decisions in Clark County.


To address Commerce’s comments regarding the de-designation analysis, it is important to reverse the
order of their inquiries. In subsection (b), Commerce opines that “de-designations of agricultural lands
based solely or primarily on proximity to urban growth that otherwise meet the definition of agricultural
lands, historically has not been found to be defensible by the Growth Management Hearings Board.”


WAC 365-190-050 states that designation as agricultural resource land relies on three factors including that
the “land is not already characterized by urban growth” (WAC 365-190-050(3)(a)). To determine whether it
is characterized by urban growth, the WAC specifies that Counties and Cities “use the criteria contained in
WAC 365-196-310”. WAC 365-196-310(1)(c) states that “urban growth area[s] may include territory that is
located outside a city if such territory already is characterized by urban growth or is adjacent to territory
already characterized by urban growth.” The Growth Management Act further clarifies in RCW
36.70A.030(49) that “”[c]haracterized by urban growth" refers to land having urban growth located on it, or
to land located in relationship to an area with urban growth on it as to be appropriate for urban growth.”
Finally, the Court of Appeals in Clark County WA LA GM LLC v. Western WA Growth Management Hearings
Review Board (2011), ruled that under the “first prong of the Lewis County test, the statutory definition of

“urban growth” requires an assessment of the overall context of the land’s relationship to the surrounding
land—not just an evaluation of the land itself. See former RCW 36.70A.030(18); Lewis County, 157 Wash.2d at
502, 139 P.3d 1096.”


Thus, the County must consider the relationship between properties being analyzed for de-designation and
the area surrounding it. This analysis is especially important to accurately classify site-specific requests
adjacent to existing City UGAs in the FEIS Preferred Alternative. Specifically, Ridgefield’s expansion requests
meet the test of being characterized by urban growth as follows:
    -    The “Maul” property is completely surrounded by the Ridgefield UGA. The Maul Property parcels
         were added to the Ridgefield UGA during the 2007 Clark County Comprehensive Plan Update. That
         decision was appealed and ultimately invalidated, but not before the City had lawfully annexed
         surrounding included parcels into the Ridgefield City limits in 2008. The Maul property was
         excluded from that annexation map as a mapping error, creating an island surrounded by
         incorporated urban growth.
    -    The “North Industrial Urban Reserve Properties” are characterized by urban growth by themselves,
         having been classified by the County with an “Urban Reserve” Comprehensive Plan Overlay since
         1994. Under Clark County Code, this Comprehensive Plan Overlay is applied to lands “identified as
         being possible future additions to urban growth areas”. The overlay is intended to “protect areas
         from premature land division and development that would preclude efficient transition to urban
         development”. (CC Code Chapter 40.40.250.100) The Industrial Urban Reserve Overlay has remained
         without interruption on this property area since the overlay was first applied. In addition to the
         property area itself being characterized by urban growth, the area is bordered on the south, west,
         and east by the Ridgefield UGA and on the north by proximity to the Cowlitz Tribal Nation Initial
         Reservation.
