Dear Chair Marshall:

Thank you for the opportunity to review and comment on the Clark County’s (county’s) draft
comprehensive plan and development regulations. We appreciate the ongoing coordination
with our agency as you work to achieve the community’s vision consistent with the goals and
requirements of the Growth Management Act (GMA). Growth Management Services received
the proposed amendment on July 24, 2026, and processed them with material identification
numbers 2026-S-14100 and 2026-S-14101.

Your submission represents a great deal of work towards the 2025 periodic update of your
comprehensive plan and implementing development regulations, which were due December
31, 2025.

We have reviewed your submittal using Commerce’s Periodic Update Checklist and Expanded
Housing Checklist. We encourage you and your community to consider the following as you
finalize your drafts prior to adoption:

Comprehensive Plan

1. Land Capacity Analysis
   In our review, we did not find the county’s land capacity analysis identified insufficient land
   capacity before urban growth area changes. Before UGA expansions, jurisdictions must
   evaluate existing land capacity and that the jurisdiction has evaluated land use
   designations, zoning and development regulations to increase capacity before expansions
   (WAC 365-196-310(1)(f) and 310(5)(d), Draft Urban Growth Area Guidance, p. 61; Guidance
   for Updating your Housing Element, p. 39-43 – specifically Step 6).

   We recommend the county clearly identify that they have insufficient capacity for housing
   needs, document how they have evaluated zoning changes to accommodate deficits in
   housing capacity, and then document sufficient capacity with any UGA changes after these
   previous steps. See more discussion on UGA expansions in Item #2.

   Additionally, based on recent Growth Management Hearings Board (GMHB) cases findings,
   it is recommended that jurisdictions disaggregate housing capacity by income level so that
   it is clear where there are deficits for specific housing needs. At a minimum, Commerce
   recommends disaggregating any housing capacity that does not include subsidies
   (everything above 50% AMI).

2. Urban Growth Area Expansions
   a. In our review, we did not find sufficient analysis supporting the proposed expansion of
      various urban growth areas outside of Vancouver to meet future population and
      growth targets. WAC 365.196.310(1)(e) outlines that, “The combined urban growth
      area may not exceed the areas necessary to accommodate the growth management
      planning projections, plus a reasonable land market supply factor, or market factor.”
      From our understanding of the county’s Vacant Buildable Lands Model (2023), it
      appears the current urban growth area boundaries are sufficient for the projected
      population increases expected within Clark County (HAPT compared to Alternative 1).
   b. Additionally, we did not find any discussion or analysis documenting whether other
      zoning strategies have been utilized before concluding expansion of the various urban
      growth areas were necessary. The County’s Land capacity analysis is the appropriate
      place to document and justify the need to expand the urban growth areas proposed in
      the draft comprehensive plan. As mentioned above, we did not find the city’s land
      capacity analysis documenting such need.
   c. Finally, in our review we did not find documentation indicating how the County expects
      urban levels of service will be provided to the expanded areas over the 20-year
      planning horizon.

3. De-designation of Resource Lands
   a. In our review of the draft comprehensive plan and agricultural lands study, we did not
      find any discussion clearly demonstrating that the lands proposed for de-designation
      no longer meet the designation criteria. WAC 365.190.040(10)(c) outlines that

            new information related to designation criteria.
        b. Further, de-designations of agricultural lands based solely or primarily on proximity to
           urban growth that otherwise meet the definition of agricultural lands, historically has
           not been found to be defensible by the Growth Management Hearings Board1.

        As land capacity, UGA expansion, and de-designation of resource lands are inherently
        intertwined, we recommend re-evaluating each of these together. For consistency with

           i. Removing the areas proposed to be de-designated from the various urban growth
               area expansion proposals after the documentation that the land capacity analysis
               demonstrates adequate capacity, or
          ii. Including additional justification and analysis, consistent with RCW, that
               demonstrates the need for expansion of the various urban growth areas and for the

4. Transfer of Development Rights (TDR) Program
   In our review, we found Policy 1.1.24 potentially inconsistent with state statue. While the
   stated goal of resource land preservation is aligned with RCW 43.362.005, TDR programs
   are not intended to be used as a mechanism to expand UGA Boundaries. When designated
   resource lands are already within a UGA, TDR programs provide a valuable mechanism to
   ensure those lands do not face development pressure (WAC 365.196.310(5)(e)(v)). We
   recommend separating the establishment of the TDR program from components of UGA
   expansion. Please see Commerce’s Transfer of Development Rights website for more
   information.

   and code review with recommended actions, we could not find documentation of specific

Subject:          Traffic Impact Fee Formula (CCC 40.620.010)


As part of the code amendments to be presented to Council within the 2025 Comprehensive Growth
Management Plan Update, all references to the Traffic Impact Fee Technical Document are proposed for removal,
thereby rendering the document obsolete. In conjunction with this proposed rescission, and to ensure the
pass-by factor is applied accurately and consistently, CCPW respectfully proposes that the Traffic Impact
Fee Formula in section 40.620.010 be amended as set forth below:

                        TIF = (size of development by unit of measure/unit of measure) x
      (daily trips per unit of measure) x (1 - pass-by factor) x (BEF) x (0.85) x (fee per daily trip by district)
