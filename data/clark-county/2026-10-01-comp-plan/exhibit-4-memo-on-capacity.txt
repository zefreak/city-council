Background

Prior to the selection of the Preferred Alternative the Planning Commission was presented with tables
comparing the alternatives by housing and employment capacity. Alternative 1 showed a deﬁcit of 3,856
housing units and 5187 jobs, Alternative 2 had a surplus of 38,701 housing units and 3,352 jobs and
Alternative 3 had a surplus of 45,924 housing units and 5,929 jobs.

The Planning Commission recommendation resembled Alternative 2 with several exceptions. The PC
moved to retain the Meadowglade area within the BG UGA. The PC also included the expansion areas in La
Center and Ridgeﬁeld. The Council considered the PC recommendation and removed the Meadowglade
area from the Battle Ground UGA, added the Nevin property in Camas, and included the expansion areas in
La Center and Ridgeﬁeld. The selected Preferred Alternative was the basis for the FEIS published July 24,
2026.

Staff provided a memo to the Council dated April 27 prior to the Council selection of the Preferred
Alternative on April 28. The memo highlights excess capacity in the DEIS alternatives and the need to make
a correction to stay within the control totals. The only adjustment made was to the Vancouver UGA as
described below.

The analysis in the FEIS showed there is excess capacity in the Preferred Alternative countywide and in
most of the jurisdictions.

Vancouver UGA Allocation Reﬁnement

Below is an overview of Vancouver Urban Growth Area (VUGA) housing allocation reﬁnement in the
preferred land use alternative selected by the County Council on April 28, 2026.

Reﬁnements were needed because:

      1. Local jurisdictions cannot plan for more housing capacity than is needed in comprehensive plans
         based on RCW 36.70A.110(2).
      2. The Vacant Buildable Land Model (VBLM) showed excess capacity within the City of Vancouver and
         Unincorporated VUGA in the Planning Commission recommended Preferred Alternative relative to
         the allocation in RES. 2024-05-05.

the R1 zones, accounting for about 62% of the reduction, followed by the reduction in upzoning in the 179th
St/Fairgrounds area, accounting for approximately 30% of the reduction, further followed by the HWY 99
amendments, accounting for about 8% of the reduction.

These amendments were considered by County Council and included in the Preferred Alternative selected.

Capacity Comparison of Preferred Alternative to Allocation

Table 3 below reﬂects the updated changes in the Vancouver UGA and indicates that with the revised
housing allocation there still remains excess capacity of 10,498 housing units countywide. About 64% of
the excess capacity are in jurisdictions that are not proposing boundary expansions. The jurisdictions of
Camas, La Center and Ridgeﬁeld are all exceeding their allocation by 10% to 50% of their targeted
allocation and proposing UGA boundary expansions. Table 4 includes the comparison of the employment
allocation to the employment capacity. The comparison shows that net surplus of 1,874 in the Vancouver
UGA which is not proposing a boundary expansion.

The jurisdictions of Camas, La Center and Ridgeﬁeld are all exceeding their allocation and are proposing a
boundary expansion. In our view there is a need for a discussion that all jurisdictions have undertaken
some reasonable measures before concluding that expansion is necessary.
