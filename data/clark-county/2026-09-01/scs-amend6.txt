        BACKGROUND
        Leichner Landfill, located at 9411 NE 94th Avenue in Vancouver, Washington, is a closed landfill owned and
        maintained by Clark County under a post‑closure maintenance and monitoring plan established by Consent
        Decree between the County and the Washington State Department of Ecology. Under RCW 70A.305, the
        County operates the site under a post‑closure permit to ensure ongoing regulatory compliance and protect
        public health and safety.

        Clark County Public Health issued RFP 854, “Leichner Landfill Environmental Engineering Services for Landfill
        Maintenance and Monitoring,” on March 29, 2023, completed a full evaluation of the proposals received, and
        awarded the contract to Stearns, Conrad and Schmidt Consulting Engineers, Inc. (dba SCS Engineers) for
        environmental/engineering consulting and landfill operations, maintenance, and monitoring services.

        Due to discrepancies identified during contract review, an increase to the total contract amount is being
        requested to account for cumulative expenditures incurred during the contract period of December 1, 2023,
        through November 30, 2028. Approval of the updated total contract amount will authorize the County
        Manager to execute Amendment 6 to the SCS Engineers Maintenance and Operations Agreement, ensuring
        the agreement more accurately reflects the receipt‑to‑date balance and approved budget. The annual budget
        for each calendar year is included in the County budget and approved by Council.

        Funding for this agreement is provided by the Special Purpose Fund 6310, Solid Waste Closure Fund. The
        Revised Environmental Compliance Budget (RECB), a detailed annual cost estimate, is established by the
        Leichner Landfill Oversight Committee (LLOC), which includes representatives from both Clark County and
        the City of Vancouver.

              procured data. Compliance LFG monitoring data will be evaluated by comparing methane
              concentrations to Washington State MFS for Solid Waste Handling (Washington Administrative
              Code [WAC] 173-304).

              Operations, maintenance, and monitoring (OM&M) of the LFG flare system will be performed
              to ensure the LFG flare operates within permit requirements and to prevent subsurface LFG
              migration.

              Overview of Remote Monitoring and Control (RMC) System and SCS’s Database System (SCS
              DataServices)

              The Leichner Landfill is facilitated with a remote monitoring and control (RMC), data logging,
              and alarm notification system that are integrated with SCS’s database management system (SCS
              DataServices). The RMC system installed and operating at the Leichner Landfill is an online,
              web-based application specifically designed and configured for site. The system allows users to
              remotely monitor and manage several components of the Leichner Landfill operational systems,
              including the blower-flare stations (BFS), the North and South Pond pumping systems, the
              Module 2 stormwater collection vault, and an on-site weather station and security camera. As
              noted in SCS’s RMC Service Agreement (discussed in more detailed below), CCPH owns all
              RMC equipment installed at the Leichner Landfill.

              The RMC system incorporates a Cmore HMI control panel (Cmore) and Programmable Logic
              Control (PLC) that are networked and programmed for secure encrypted File Transfer Protocol
              (FTP) and Virtual Private Network (VPN) connections. The Cmore is setup to provided remote
              monitoring and control of networked operational systems and allows all recorded data to be
              uploaded to the RMC application. Specific users have login rights to the RMC and can receive
              email and text alerts of system conditions and alarms. The PLC is also configured to facilitate
              remote troubleshooting of the logic and set points. SCS DataServices is an online web-based
              database application that allows users to upload, store, review, and retrieve monitoring
              information and operational system (e.g., flare) performance data. Data collected as part of the
              routine following activities are uploaded into SCS database application for the Leichner Landfill:
              (1) semi-monthly GCCS and BFS performance monitoring, as described in detail this Task
              Assignment form and (2) quarterly perimeter (compliance) LFG monitoring, described in the
              Task 4 Assignment form.

              Routine Landfill OM&M

              Landfill Cover Inspection and Maintenance
              The landfill cover system should be inspected monthly to identify and document any potential
              problems. A site inspection should be conducted after significant rainfall or a severe storm event.
              Identifying problems early will reduce the cost of future repairs. An inspection checklist will
              cover each of these items:

                 •   Settlement: Identify sinkholes and new settlement depressions that could indicate a
                     possible landfill fire. Monitor main road settlement and grade to allow large emergency
                     vehicles and large equipment proper access to the landfill in case of an emergency or a
                     fire.
                 •   Landfill Cap: Inspect for changes in vegetation and surface soil conditions. Any vegetation
                     that can produce a deep, dense root structure should be removed. Evidence of ground
                     burrows should be monitored and rodent control measures taken, if needed. Temporary
                     ditches, berms and other erosion control measures will be recommended as needed to
                     prevent damage of cover areas until weather conditions permit final grading and
                     reconstruction of damaged areas.
                 •   Erosion and Surface Drainage: Erosion usually occurs in areas of storm water runoff. Our
                     inspections will include checks for erosion gullies, scars and possible issues with the
                     under drain system, as well as overland runoff issues (e.g., flat spots, standing water,
                     swampy soils, and dead vegetation). Any identified minor damaged areas will be repaired
                     in a timely manner. If attempts to repair minor, small gullies and scars are unsuccessful,
                     larger equipment will need to be utilized (non-routine repair).
              Control of Nuisance and Health Factors
              The following controls will be considered to mitigate potential nuisance or health issues:

                 •   Noise: Decibel levels of on-site equipment will be controlled by proper maintenance or
                     use of mufflers. Vibrations and air surging from the LFG BFS will be monitored and
                     minor modifications made, if needed.
                 •   Trespassing/Unauthorized Access: This will be controlled by maintenance of the
                     perimeter fence and lockable gates.
                 •   Fire/First Aid: All vehicles and the BFS will be equipped with proper fire extinguishers
                     and first aid supplies. All personnel will be trained in the proper use of various types of
                     fire extinguishers and their locations. Site personnel will have mandatory first aid training
                     every two years (The American Red Cross).

              Routine GCCS OM&M

              This section describes the routine monitoring, maintenance, and inspection activities performed
              on the Leichner Landfill GCCS and BFS. As previous noted, the monitoring data collected as
              part of these activities are routinely uploaded in the Leichner Landfill DataServices database.
              Additionally, BFS operational parameters, including blower inlet vacuum, methane and oxygen
              concentration, flow rate, flare temperature, air compressor pressure, and flare and blower
              operating hours, are continuous monitoring and recorded by the RMC/Cmore and the data can be
              access remotely. Meteorological conditions (i.e., wind velocity, barometric pressure, ambient
              temperature) are continuous monitored by the on-site weather station and the data is recorded
              and can be accessed using the site’s RMC system.

              Semi-monthly GCCS Inspection
              SCS will inspect and maintain the GCCS to provide continuous proper operation semi-monthly
              (twice per month) or on an as needed basis. The system contains piping, equipment, and various
              hoses and clamps that are all subject to the elements. Landfill settling and weathering both affect
              the efficiency of the GCCS. All wellheads will be inspected for shifting due to cover movement,
              leaky well bore seals, and air leaks from weathered flex lines. The GCCS will be inspected for
              proper pipe alignment, and pipe support settlement that would allow for liquid accumulation
              resulting in decreased system vacuum, and for open or broken labcock valves.

              The BFS total flow will be adjusted as required to help control LFG migration (methane gas
              levels below five percent by volume at the property line), and to help optimize system operation
              run time and performance.

              monitoring network for the closed Leichner Landfill to accommodate the planned development

                      the bottom up as the drill string is removed from the hole. The material used for
                      backfilling will be hydrated bentonite chips and/or bentonite grout. The surface will be
                      covered with soil.
                 •    A report will be submitted to the Washington Department of Ecology (Ecology) and
                      CCPH documenting the work. The report will describe the field methods and procedures
                      used to either decommission the monitoring well, a site map showing monitoring
                      locations, original consultant’s boring logs, and copies of the driller’s decommissioning
                      reports submitted to Ecology.

                      techniques. Dedicated bladder pumps are planned to be installed in the groundwater
                      monitoring wells and used to accommodate low-flow purge sampling.
                  •   Monitoring well purge water and decontamination water generated during sampling does
                      not require regulated disposal, and will therefore be discharged to the ground surface in
                      the vicinity of the sampled wells.
                  •   ALS Environmental (ALS) based in Kelso, Washington, will be used for analytical
                      testing of groundwater samples.

                Task No. 25: Design, Install and Connect Three LFG Extraction Wells to Extend GCCS

              This Task Assignment is provided in accordance with the signed Professional Services
              Agreement between Clark County Public Health (CCPH) and SCS Engineers (SCS) for the

              Scope of Work
              The anticipated scope of work for the design and construction of 3 vertical gas wells and
              associated gas conveyance pipe and well head control assemblies include the following
              engineering and construction services:

              Engineering Services:
              SCS will prepare drawings and specifications for the construction of 3 additional vertical gas
              wells along the north perimeter of the landfill. The design will include up to 16 drawings with
              the specifications also presented on the drawings. The landfill gas (LFG) extraction wells will
              have to be designed as “remote” wells due to the topography in the area. This means that each
              well head control assembly will be uphill and at a distance away from the well to allow for
              condensate to drain properly. Under these conditions, the condensate in the branch pipe (that
              connects the remote well head control assembly to the well) will drain back into the well. To
              minimize the amount of condensate generation, the gas conveyance pipes will be buried in the
              soil of the existing final cover system to provide insulation. The drawings and specifications will
              be of sufficient detail for developing a detailed list of material for procurement and for
              construction. The drawings and specifications are not meant for bidding purposes.
              SCS will also provide construction oversight for the project. This will include full-time
              construction quality assurance (CQA) inspection during well drilling to log the drill cuttings and
              confirm and document the well materials and construction. We find this to be a very important
              activity to achieve long-term functioning wells. From past experience, SCS has found there are
              many ways a vertical gas well can fail, and a lot of these failures occur due to lack of CQA and
              direct inspection during the process. SCS has budgeted 40 hours for the onsite CQA of 3 vertical
              gas extraction wells and 20 hours of onsite CQA for the gas conveyance pipe and well head
              control assemblies along with another 10 hours for submittal reviews and 24 hours for
              construction completion documents.
              Construction Services:
              SCS field services will provide construction services for the installation of the 3 vertical gas
              wells along the north perimeter of the landfill. The work will include procurement of materials,
              products, equipment and subcontracting with a drilling company. The construction includes
              mobilization, demobilization, insurance, safety plan, surveying, and locating existing utilities.
              The well construction includes well pad preparation, drilling 3 wells to an approximate depth of
              50 feet each, along with handling, loading, hauling and disposing of the refuse from the wells.
              The wells will be constructed by installing pipe, centralizers, rock, two bentonite seals, and final
