STATEMENTOF JUSTIFICATION

Proposed dark County Title 40 Equine, Livestock & Agricultural Use Amendment

PURPOSE

The purpose of this amendment is to establish consistent treatment of horses and other
equines as livestock and to clarify that ordinary equine husbandry constitutes an
agricultural activity under dark County Code Title 40.

THE ISSUE

Title 40 recognizes agriculture as a resource activity while also separately regulating
equestrian facilities.

This can create uncertainty over when keeping and working with horses ceases to be
livestock husbandry and becomes a separate commercial land use.

The distinction should be based upon the actual use and impacts of the property-not
simply the number of horses present or whether compensation is received.

HORSES ARE LIVESTOCK

Horses should receive the same fundamental agricultural recognition as cattle, sheep,
goats, camelids, and other livestock.

Keeping, feeding, grazing, breeding, foaling, exercising, conditioning, handling, and
training horses are activities directly associated with the care and husbandry of
livestock.

These activities should therefore remain agricultural unless the predominant character
of the property becomes a separate commercial, event, entertainment, or public-facing
use.


AGRICULTURECAN GENERATE INCOME

The receipt of compensation should not automatically transform an agricultural activity
into a commercial land use.

Farmers sell crops.

Cattle producers sell livestock.

Livestock breeders sell animals.

Agricultural operators receive compensation for activities associated with their
operations.

Equine agriculture should be treated consistently.

Receiving compensation for boarding, breeding, training, conditioning, or caring for
horses should not, by itself, cause the underlying agricultural use to become a
commercial equestrian facility.

REGULATE IMPACTS- NOT HORSE NUMBERS

The number of horses maintained on a property should not independently determine its
land-use classification.

Instead, dark County should regulate identifiable impacts such as:

. Traffic and parking

. Large public events

. Environmental impacts and drainage

. Manure management

. Nuisance conditions

. Public safety

. Animal welfare

Ten responsibly maintained horses should not automatically create a commercial land
use merely because there are ten horses.

COMMERCIAL EQUESTRIAN FACILITIES WOULD STILL BE REGULATED

This amendment would not eliminate the commercial equestrian facility classification.

Large riding schools, public event facilities, spectator venues, major competitions, and
other operations producing significant customer traffic, parking, or public activity could
continue to be regulated according to their actual land-use impacts.

The amendment would simply establish a clearer boundary between:

AGRICULTURAL EQUINE HUSBANDRY

and

COMMERCIAL PUBLIC-FACING EQUESTRIAN USES.

REQUESTED ACTION

dark County is respectfully requested to amend Title 40 to:

' Expressly recognize equines as livestock.

. Expressly recognize equine husbandry as agriculture.

. Prohibit horse numbers alone from determining whether a property is a commercial
equestrian facility.

. Prevent compensation for ordinary equine husbandry from automatically converting an
agricultural use into a commercial use.

. Base commercial equestrian classifications primarily upon actual land-use impacts.

. Maintain county authority over nuisance, environmental, traffic, public-safety and
animal-welfare concerns.

CONCLUSION
A horse is livestock.

The husbandry of livestock is agriculture.

Clark County Title 40 should clearly and consistently reflect both principles.
PROPOSEDCLARK COUNTY CODE TITLE 40 AMENDMENT

Equine, Livestock & Agricultural Uses

SECTION 1 - EQUINE DEFINITION

Add the following definition to the applicable definitions section of dark County Code
Title 40:

Equine.

"Equine" means a horse, pony, donkey, mule, burro, miniature horse, miniature donkey,
or other member or hybrid of the family Equidae.

SECTION 2 - LIVESTOCKDEFINITION

Amend the applicable definition of "livestock" to expressly provide:

Livestock.

"Livestock" includes cattle, equines, camelids, goats, sheep, swine, rabbits, poultry, and
other animals traditionally raised, kept, or maintained as part of agricultural husbandry.

For purposes of this title, equines shall be considered livestock unless a provision
expressly provides otherwise.

SECTION 3 - EQUINE HUSBANDRY

Add the following definition:

Equine Husbandry.

"Equine husbandry" means the keeping, feeding, watering, sheltering, grazing,
breeding, foaling, exercising, conditioning, handling, training, transporting, and routine
care and management of equines.

Equine husbandry constitutes an agricultural activity for purposes of this title.

SECTION 4 -AGRICULTURAL EQUINE USE

Add the following provision to the applicable agricultural standards:

Agricultural Equine Uses.

A. The keeping, raising, breeding, foaling, grazing, exercising, conditioning, handling,
training, and routine care of equines constitutes livestock husbandry and an agricultural
use.


B. The number of equines maintained upon a parcel shall not, standing alone, cause an
agricultural equine use to be classified as a commercial equestrian facility or other
commercial land use.

C. Receipt of compensation associated with boarding, breeding, training, conditioning,
handling, or care of equines shall not, standing alone, cause the underlying use to lose
its agricultural classification when the predominant use of the property remains equine
or livestock husbandry.
D. Agricultural equine uses remain subject to generally applicable requirements
concerning animal welfare, manure management, drainage, critical areas,
environmental protection, nuisance conditions, building and fire codes, and public
safety.

SECTION 5 - COMMERCIAL EQUESTRIAN FACILITY

Amend the definition or applicable standards for "equestrian facility" to distinguish
agricultural equine husbandry from commercial public-facing activities.

Commercial Equestrian Facility.

A commercial equestrian facility is a use in which substantial public-facing or customer
activity creates land-use impacts beyond those ordinarily associated with agricultural
equine husbandry.

Relevant factors may include:

1. Regular and substantial customer or spectator traffic;

2. Public events or competitions;

3. Significant dedicated customer or spectator parking;

4. Event facilities or spectator facilities;

5. Regular public gatherings; and

6, Other comparable land-use impacts beyond those ordinarily associated with livestock
husbandry.

The following shall not, individually or collectively, automatically establish a commercial
equestrian facility:

1. The number of equines maintained on the property;

2. Breeding equines;

3. Boarding equines;

4. Training or conditioning equines;

5. Exercising equines;

6. Sale of equines;

7. Receipt of compensation for equine husbandry; or

8. Construction or use of barns, pastures, paddocks, shelters, riding areas, or other
structures ordinarily associated with keeping and managing equines.

SECTION 6 -ACTUAL IMPACT STANDARD

The County may regulate agricultural equine uses when necessary to address
demonstrated land-use impacts, including nuisance conditions, environmental impacts,
traffic, parking, drainage, manure management, public safety, or animal welfare.

The existence of an agricultural equine use or the number of equines maintained on a
property shall not, without additional land-use impacts, constitute sufficient grounds to
classify the property as a commercial equestrian facility.

SECTION 7 - CONSISTENCYWITH OTHER COUNTY CODE

Nothing in this section limits the County's authority to enforce applicable animal cruelty,
neglect, dangerous animal, public health, building, fire, environmental, or nuisance
regulations.

However, regulation under another title of the dark County Code shall not, solely by
virtue of that regulation, alter the agricultural land-use classification of equine husbandry
established under Title 40.

LEGISLATIVEINTENT

The dark County Council finds that:

1. Equines are livestock.

2. Equine husbandry is a form of animal husbandry and agriculture.

3. Agricultural activities may generate revenue without losing their agricultural character

4. The number of livestock maintained on a property is not, by itself, an accurate
measurement of commercial land-use intensity.

5. Land-use regulations should primarily address actual impacts associated with a use.

6. Legitimate commercial equestrian facilities may create impacts warranting additional
land-use review.

7. Clear differentiation between agricultural equine husbandry and commercial public-
facing equestrian facilities will improve consistency, predictability, and fairness in
administration of Title 40.

The purpose of this amendment is therefore to protect legitimate agricultural equine
activities while preserving dark County's authority to regulate demonstrated land-use
impacts.

Prohibition of Non-Therapeutic DecLawing of Cats

The purpose of this proposal is to protect the health and welfare of cats in dark County by prohibiting
non-therapeutic declawing while preserving the ability of Licensed veterinarians to perform procedures
that are medically necessary to treat disease, infection, injury, or another condition affecting a cat's
health.

This proposal recognizes that decLawingis not simply the trimming or removal of a cat's claws. It involves
surgical alteration or removal of structures associated with the cat's digits and permanently prevents
normal functioning of the affected claws.

dark County should establish a clear animal-welfare standard that such a procedure may not be
performed solely for human convenience, cosmetic purposes, protection of furniture or property, or
management of normal feline scratching behavior.

REQUESTEDACTION

The dark County Council is respectfully requested to:

1. Direct County Counsel to determine whether current Washington Law permits dark County to prohibit
non-therapeutic feline declawing;

2. If legally permissible, prepare an ordinance amending dark County Code Title 8 to prohibit non-
therapeutic declawing;

3. Model the ordinance, where appropriate, on the existing City of Tacoma prohibition on cat declawing;

4. Preserve an exception for procedures determined by a licensed veterinarian to be medically necessary
for a therapeutic purpose; and

5. Establish an appropriate civil enforcement mechanism for violations.

PROPOSEDCODE LANGUAGE
DecLawingofCatsProhibited

A. Prohibition


Except as specifically permitted by this section, no person shall perform, cause to be performed, arrange
for, or knowingly authorize a dectawing procedure upon a cat within dark County.

B. Therapeutic Exception

A declawing procedure may be performed when:

1. The procedure is performed by a veterinarian licensed to practice veterinary medicine in the State of
Washington; and

2. In the veterinarian's professional medical judgment, the procedure is medically necessary for a
therapeutic purpose affectingthe health of the individual cat.

C. Definition-Declawing

For purposes of this section, "declawing" means an onychectomy, dactylectomy, phalangectomy,
tendonectomy, partial digital amputation, or other surgical procedure that removes, amputates, severs,
alters, or disables a portion of a cat's claw, digit, bone, tendon, or associated structure for the purpose or
effect of permanently preventing the normal functioningof the claw.

Declawingdoes not include-

1. Routine nail trimming;

2. Removal of nonviable claw tissue;


3. Treatment of an injured claw;

4. Temporary application of nonpermanent nail caps; or

5. A medically necessary procedure performed for a therapeutic purpose.

D. Definition -Therapeutic Purpose

"Therapeutic purpose" means treatment determined by a licensed veterinarian to be medically
necessaryto address an existing or recurring disease, infection, injury, tumor, abnormal condition, or
other medical condition affectinga cat's claw, nail bed, digit, paw, or associated structure when
treatment is necessaryto protect the cat's health or relieve a medically significant condition.

A therapeutic purpose does not include:

1 Cosmetic or aesthetic purposes;

2. Protection of furniture or other property;
3. Owner or caretaker convenience;


4. Prevention of normal scratching behavior;

5. Makinga cat easier to handle;

6. A landlord or housing requirement; or

7. A person's preference for a cat without functional claws.

E. Veterinary Medical Judgment

Nothingin this section shall prohibit a licensed veterinarian from performing a medically necessary
procedure when, in the veterinarian's professionaljudgment, the procedure is necessaryto treat an
individual cat.


Nothing in this section shall require a veterinarian to delay emergency treatment necessary to prevent
sufferingor protect the health of a cat.

F. Records


A veterinarian performing a declawing procedure under the therapeutic exception should document in
the animal's medical record:


1. The medical condition necessitatingthe procedure;

2. The therapeutic reason for the procedure;

3. The date the procedure was performed; and

4. The veterinarian performing the procedure.

The County should coordinate any record requirements with applicable Washington veterinary
recordkeeping laws and regulations to avoid unnecessary duplication.

G. Enforcement


A violation of this section shall constitute a civil violation subject to the enforcement provisions of Title 8.

Each cat upon which a prohibited procedure is performed shall constitute a separate violation.

The County Council should establish a civil penalty sufficient to discourageviolations white preserving
appropriate due-process and appeal procedures.

H. No Requirement to Declaw
No county-regulated animal facility, adoption organization, animat-related business, landlord, property
owner, or other person may require the non-therapeutic declawingof a cat as a condition of adoption,
sale, transfer, housing, boarding, or provision of services where such declawingwould otherwise be
prohibited bythis section.

LEGISLATIVE FINDINGS

The dark County Council should consider adoptingfindings that:

1. Cats use their claws for normal behaviors including scratching, stretching, climbing, balance, and
defense.


2. Declawing is distinguishable from routine nail trimming because declawing permanently alters
anatomical structures associated with the cat's digits.

3. Permanent surgical alteration of a healthy animal should not be performed solely for convenience or
cosmetic reasons when there is no therapeutic medical need.

4. Veterinarians must retain the ability to treat disease, injury, infection, tumors, abnormal tissue, or
other legitimate medical conditions affecting a cat's digits or paws.

5. Reasonable alternatives to non-therapeutic declawinginclude appropriate scratchingsurfaces,
behavioral modification, routine nail trimming, environmental management, and temporary nail caps
when appropriate.

6. A prohibition containing a therapeutic veterinary exception balances animal welfare with legitimate
veterinary medical needs.

7. dark County has an interest in promoting humane treatment of animals and preventing unnecessary
surgical procedures performed solely for convenience.

TACOMA PRECEDENT

The CityofTacoma has already adopted a municipal prohibition on cat declawing.

Tacoma Municipal Code 17. 02. 155 generally prohibits partial or complete declawingof cats while
creating an exception when the procedure is performed by a licensed veterinarian for a therapeutic
purpose.


dark County should considerTacoma's ordinance as an existing Washington local-government model
when evaluating a similar amendment to Title 8.

RELATIONSHIPTOWASHINGTONSTATE LEGISLATION

Washington legislators have considered statewide legislation, HB 1904, addressing non-therapeutic cat
declawing.
Because state legislation could affect the authority of Localgovernments to independently regulate this
subject, dark County should obtain review from County Counsel before finat adoption.

Accordingly, this proposal requests that the Council direct County Counsel to:

Determine whether dark County currently possesses authority under Washington law to prohibit non-
therapeutic feline declawingand, if so, prepare appropriate Title 8 ordinance language for Council
consideration.


If Washingtonsubsequently adopts a statewide prohibition that preempts local regulation, dark County
should conform its code to applicable state law.

POLICY OBJECTIVE

The purpose of this proposal is not to interfere with legitimate veterinary medicine.

The purpose is to establish a simple animal-welfare principle:

A healthy cat should not undergo permanent surgical alteration of its toes or claws merely for human
convenience.


When a procedure isgenuinely medically necessaryfor the individual cat, the decision should remain
between the veterinarian and the person responsible forthe cat.

When there is no therapeutic medical need, dark County should protect the animal from an
unnecessary permanent surgical procedure.

I respectfully request that the proposed amendment be directed to the appropriate board and/or County
Council for public review, recommendation, and consideration.
