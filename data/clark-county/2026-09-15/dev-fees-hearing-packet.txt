        BACKGROUND
        Historically, the Land Use Review and the Wetland and Habitat Review programs have each received General
        Fund support, with a trend upward in recent years. Reasons include sporadic updates to fees to reflect
        current costs of service, and intentional subsidization by Council for both fee-supported work and non-fee
        supported work, such as staff providing customer service to applicants.

        To decrease reliance on General Fund support, Community Development staff completed a cost-of-service
        study for both programs in 2024. Prior to that, there were no significant increases in either program’s fees
        since 2009. Smaller efforts to update fees in 2011 and 2017 included both increases and decreases in fees
        and did not have substantive impacts to program revenues.

        The fees, as proposed in the attached ordinance, reflect 100% cost recovery (staff wages and benefits,
        department and countywide overhead) and a 2.5% technology fee (only for sections 6.110A.010 and
        6.110.040) intended to fund the computer hardware, software and support costs associated with processing
        development applications. For example, the current boundary line adjustment base fee is $663. To recover
        100% of 2026 costs of service, the fee is calculated to be $687. The application of the 2.5% technology fee
        on top of the $686 results in the fee being proposed in the attached ordinance of $704.

        Change request COM-03-27AD has been submitted to reflect anticipated increases in revenue and is to be
        considered by the County Manager and Council for adoption into the 2027 annual budget.

                                                                   2
Background
Revenue sources
•   The Department of Community Development at Clark County is mostly
    funded by fees


•   Best practices, state law (RCW 82.02.020), and Clark County code
    (CCC 6.100.020), dictate that the fees our department charges should
    cover the cost of providing the service. This includes not only the
    salaries and benefits of staff providing the services, but also indirect
    costs like office rent and copier maintenance, and department overhead
    allocated to the programs


•   Those costs which cannot be tied to processing the applications, or are
    not subsidized by Council, must be covered by other funding sources.
    For the Department of Community Development, the alternate funding
    source is General Fund dollars



                                                                               3
Background (continued)
Main drivers of General Fund support
•   Sporadic updates to fees in past years
     •   Prior to the 2024 update, which set fees to cover the cost of
         providing the services, the Land Use Review and Wetland and
         Habitat Review programs had not seen meaningful updates to fees
         in nearly 15 years
     •   Cost of providing services increases each year, but fees did not
         cover those costs, resulting in the need for General Fund support


•   Intentional subsidization of program costs by previous councils
     •   General Fund support for fee work, also known as “public share”
     •   Allocation of General Fund dollars for non-fee supported activities
                •   Customer service to applicants – ‘planner of the day’,
                    extra assistance to one-time applicants
                •   Clark Conservation District - works with land owners to
                    maintain exempt status and avoid violations

Notes of Interest
•   The hourly compensation for the County’s two hearings examiners was
    increased on May 1, 2026 from $200/hour to $275/hour, a 38% increase.
    This was done to bring the rates up to market for this area. As a result,
    some fees experienced larger percentage increases year over year than
    others
•   Proposed fee updates were presented to the Development Engineering
    Advisory Board (DEAB) on September 3, 2026. Staffing challenges in
    the Department’s Development Engineering program were discussed.
    One board member noted the Department’s use of engineering staff from
    other Clark County departments and outside staffing agencies to help
    with workload, and asked that Community Development consider the
    cost of those services in future fee updates
     •   At the conclusion of the presentation, DEAB signaled its support for
         the Department’s proposed 2027 fee updates through a vote
