PROPOSED ACTION
Clark County Fire District 3 requests that Clark County review and approve its capital facilities
plan for the purpose of assessing development impact fees. The district requests that Clark
County formally adopt the plan by reference in the 20-year Clark County Comprehensive
Growth Management Plan and authorize the district to implement impact fees under the plan.

BACKGROUND
Chapter 36.70A of the Revised Code of Washington (RCW) authorizes public entities such as
fire districts to develop capital facilities plans and impact fee programs for new development to
offset the impacts of growth on capital facilities. These plans and programs must be reviewed
and approved as part of the county and city comprehensive plans where the district is located.
The minimum requirements of a fire district’s capital facilities plan are defined in RCW
36.70A.070(3) and Clark County Code (CCC) 40.620.050(A). A fire district requesting impact
fees shall submit to the county, and update at least every five (5) years, a capital facilities plan
adopted by the fire district board and consisting of the following elements:
   •    Fire protection services and inventory,
   •    Capital projects list,
   •    A forecast of future needs,
   •    A financing plan, and,
   •    Periodic review and update methods.
In February 2024, the Clark Conty Council adopted county code amendments that authorized
fire districts to request review of their capital facilities plans and approval of associated impact
fees. (See ORD 2024-02-03.) The fire districts did not formally submit their plans for review at
that time. Fire District 3 has now adopted a capital facilities plan with estimated impact fees
and has requested in writing that the county review the district’s plan for inclusion in the
county’s comprehensive plan. A copy of the district’s request letter and their capital facilities
plan with impact fee calculations are attached as Exhibits A and B, respectively.

Introduction
This document is the Capital Facilities Plan (CFP) of Clark County Fire District 3. It contains all the
elements required by Washington law for capital facilities plans that comply with Washington’s
Growth Management Act. This document is created with the ability to be adopted by reference into
the 20-year Comprehensive Growth Management plans of the City of Battle Ground and Clark
County, Washington.
Purpose
Capital facilities are the facilities needed to support growth. For a fire district, these facilities
include fire stations, training facilities, and the major apparatus needed to provide service, such as
fire engines and aid vehicles.
The purpose of the Fire District 3 Capital Facilities Plan is to use sound fiscal policies to ensure
adequate public fire facilities are planned and provided for as necessitated by growth over a 20-
year period.
Components of the CFP
In accordance with RCW 36.70A.070, Fire District 3’s capital facilities plan contains the following
elements:
A. Fire Protection Services and Inventory: This section provides a narrative description of the
fire district operations and the current inventory of stations and apparatus.
B. Capital Projects: This section lists the capital improvements that will eliminate existing
deficiencies, make available adequate facilities for future growth, and repair or replace obsolete or
worn-out facilities.
C. Forecast of Future Needs: This section identifies the level of service standards and analyzes the
need for capital improvements to achieve levels of service.
D. Financing Plan: This section lists the funding sources that will pay for needed capital
improvements. It also addresses the requirement that the land use element and capital facilities
plan element are coordinated and consistent.
E. Periodic Review and Update: This section identifies methods to be used to assure that the
accuracy and functionality of the capital facilities plan is periodically evaluated, updated, and
maintained.

A. Fire Protection Services and Inventory
Fire Protection Services
Fire District #3 serves an approximate 92 square mile area including the communities of
Hockinson, Brush Prairie, Venersborg, Heisson/Battle Ground Lake, Rawson Rd/Hockinson
Highlands and the City of Battle Ground. The fire district consists of many land uses to include
urban residential, single family residential, multi-family residential, commercial and industrial
development. The population within this area is approximately 46,000 people.

The district provides various services to the citizens within its service area including, but not
limited to:

    •   Responding to all fire related emergencies.

    •   Providing Advanced Life Support (ALS) and Basic Life Support (BLS) services.

    •   Responding to hazardous material emergencies with the ability to mitigate most
        emergencies and a contract with a regional hazardous materials team to handle
        extraordinary emergencies.

    •   Providing technical rescue capabilities through a countywide team in which the district
        participates.

    •   An active public education program.

    •   A model wildland fire prevention program.

    •   Enforcing the fire code, conducting fire investigations and inspections.

    •   Responding to many non-emergency type calls.

    •   Offering home safety surveys, address identification, smoke detector checks, etc.

Fire District 3 is a combination organization comprised of an elected board of three Fire
Commissioners, one Fire Chief, two Division Chiefs, one Administrative Officer, one Administrative
Assistant, one Fire Inspector, three Battalion Chiefs, 47-line personnel, and 10 Intern/Volunteer
Firefighters. District operations are comprised of three 24-hour shifts (A, B, and C shift) led by a
Battalion Chief who oversees assigned suppression/EMS personnel, apparatus, and equipment, that
operates from four staffed stations.

Levels of Service Measures
Clark County Fire District 3’s Capital Facilities Plan (CFP) analyzes the need for fire stations and
apparatus, including capital improvements needed to achieve level of service standards (e.g., the
response time objectives). Fire District 3’s level of service performance standards for response
times do not directly measure the number of fire stations and apparatus, therefore the analysis of
needs in this CFP uses several indicators of need to establish the relationship between the
performance objectives and the quantity of fire stations and apparatus. The final determination of
need is not the result of a formula but is the result of the interpretation of the multiple indicators of
need by fire service professionals and the Fire District 3 Commissioners.

New Growth

Two aspects of growth contribute to the total capital facility demand; an estimate of a growth
component that represents the quantity of new demand in both new population and employment
base over the planning period, and a growth share of the project capital needs, which is usually
expressed as a percentage of total need. Total capital need increases with the increase in the
volume of fire related responses resulting from an increase in the number of new residential
dwellings and new non-residential development within the district.

Establishment of future capital demand must be based upon an estimate of the total new demand
from new residential and non-residential growth that will occur during the planning period. This



                                                                                                          7
estimate in large part determines what the final amount of capital costs per unit of measure may be
covered by an impact fee. Growth covered by an impact fee is broken into two basic parts:
residential (including single- and multi-family) and non-residential (including commercial,
industrial, business park and institutional).

To ensure an accurate district-wide representation is achieved, this CFP uses an analysis of both
incorporated areas and unincorporated areas. It is the goal of the district that this CFP coincide with
the comprehensive growth management plans implemented by both the City of Battle Ground and
Clark County.

Growth’s Share of Projected Capital Needs
Proportionate Share
First, the "proportionate share" test requires that impact fees can only be charged for the portion of
the cost of public facilities that is "reasonably related" to new development. In other words, impact
fees cannot be charged to pay for the cost of reducing or eliminating deficiencies in existing


                                                                                                         12
facilities. However, impact fees may be imposed to pay for system improvement costs previously
incurred by the district to the extent that new growth and development will be served by those
improvements. (RCW 82.02.060(8)).
The analysis contained in Section C demonstrates that the new facilities financed in part by an
impact fee supported by this plan will not remedy any existing deficiencies, but rather will serve to
meet the demands created by new development.
Second, there are several important implications of the proportionate share requirement that are
not specifically addressed in the law, but which follow from the law:
   •   The costs of new facilities that will be used by new development and existing users must be
       apportioned between the two groups in determining the amount of the fee.

   •   Impact fees that recover the costs of existing unused capacity should be based on the actual
       cost, rather than the replacement cost of the facility. Carrying costs may be added to reflect
       the actual or imputed interest expense.
All the facilities described as future needs in this plan will be used by both existing and new
development. The analysis contained within this facilities plan accounts for this distribution of
benefit through an examination of the existing development within the district in contrast to the
expected new development that will benefit from the facilities paid for in part by an impact fee
supported by this plan.
The third aspect of the proportionate share requirement is its relationship to the requirement to
provide adjustments and credits to impact fees, where appropriate. These requirements ensure
that the amount of the impact fee does not exceed the proportionate share.
   •   The "adjustments" requirement reduces the impact fee to account for past and future
       payments of other revenues (if such payments are earmarked for or pro ratable to
       particular system improvements).

   •   The "credit" requirement reduces impact fees by the value of dedicated land, improvements
       or construction provided by the developer (if such facilities are in the adopted CFP and are
       required as a condition of development approval). The law does not prohibit a local
       government from establishing reasonable constraints on determining credits. For example,
       the location of dedicated land and the quality and design of a donated public facility can be
       required to conform to adopted local standards for such facilities.
With such adjustments and credits, new development which is well situated to provide system
improvements will not overpay the impact fees applicable to it. However, the district currently
does not have any past or future revenues that are earmarked for, dedicated to or pro ratable to
particular fire facility system improvements other than the revenue instruments identified in this
section of this plan. Further, any impact fee ordinance adopted by Fire District 3 to implement this
facilities plan pursuant to RCW 82.02.060 will include the provision for "credits" requirement
thereby reducing impact fees by the value of dedicated land, improvements or construction
provided by the developer.
Reasonably Related to Need.
There are many ways to fulfill the requirement that impact fees shall only be imposed for system
improvements that are reasonably related to new development, including use by the occupants,
owners or users of the new development (direct benefit), use by persons or organizations who


                                                                                                        13
provide goods or services to, or receive the same from the new development (indirect benefit), and
geographical proximity (presumed benefit). These measures of relatedness are implemented by
the following techniques:
   •   Impact fees for fire protection are charged to new developments which needs (i.e., benefits
       from) new fire protection facilities. Fire protection is provided to residences, businesses
       and other non-residential land uses. Impact fees for fire protection facilities are charged to
       all new development because the stream of benefits redounds to the occupants and owners
       of all residential and non-residential property.

   •   The relative needs of different types of growth are considered in establishing fee amounts.

   •   Fee-payers can pay a smaller fee if they can demonstrate that their use will have less impact
       than is presumed in the calculation of the impact fee schedule for their classification of
       property. Such reduced needs must be permanent and enforceable (i.e., through land use
       restrictions).
Another method of connecting a development to the need for public facilities is to establish impact
fee service areas (zones). All impact fees paid by new development in the service area would be
required to be spent on new public facilities in the same service area.
The geographical relationship between a fee-paying development and the impact on a public capital
facility will differ according to the type of public facility. Roads, for example, may have discernable
geographical service areas, based on average trip length. There is only a weak geographical linkage
to fire protection facilities because of the use of back-up response, i.e., facilities not funded by
impact fees imposed on new development in the district, by fire apparatus for a) multi-alarm fires,
and b) when apparatus from the nearest station is engaged in a response. Therefore, the use of a
single service area for fire protection facility needs in the district is reasonable.
Reasonably Related to Expenditures
Two provisions of the law tend to reinforce the requirement that expenditures be "reasonably
related" to new development which is subject to the impact fee. First, the requirement that fee
revenue must be earmarked for specific uses related to public facilities ensures that expenditures
are for identifiable projects, the benefit of which can be demonstrated. Second, impact fee revenue
must be expended within 10 years or otherwise encumbered, thus requiring timeliness of the
benefit to the fee-payer.
The combination of the timeliness ensured by an appropriate expenditure plan as well as the
analysis demonstrating proportional benefit contained within the plan assure expenditures of any
impact fee adopted pursuant to RCW 82.02.050 to implement this plan will be for system
improvements that reasonably benefit new development.
Per RCW 82.02.070, Impact fees shall be expended or encumbered for a permissible use within ten
years of receipt, unless there exists an extraordinary and compelling reason for fees to be held
longer than ten years. Such extraordinary or compelling reasons shall be identified in written
findings by the governing body.

E. Periodic Review and Update

To assure the accuracy and functionality of the capital plan and impact fee program, periodic
updates of the policies and capital costs contained in the plan should be completed. There are two
methods by which this may occur.

Annual Adjustments

The costs of capital needed to adequately fight and suppress fires in the district rise every year,
frequently in excess of the rate of inflation. The capital facilities identified in this plan will regularly
increase in cost. The district wishes to regularly keep pace with these cost increases to avoid the
need for extreme adjustments in the capital costs and the impact fee rate at the periodic plan
update interval. Based upon this, the district as a policy chooses to adjust the capital cost figures
identified in this plan on an annual basis based upon the appropriate economic indicators as well as
review and, if necessary, update the capital costs at regular plan review intervals.

In order to ensure costs are consistent with inflation, it is recommended that adopted impact fees
are adjusted annually proportionate to the Consumer Price Index (CPI).

Introduction
This document is the Capital Facilities Plan (CFP) of Clark County Fire District 6. It contains all the elements
required by Washington law for capital facilities plans that comply with Washington’s Growth Management
Act. This document is created with the ability to be adopted by reference into the 20-year Comprehensive
Growth Management plan of Clark County, Washington.

Purpose
Capital facilities are the facilities needed to support growth. For a fire district, these facilities include fire
stations, training facilities, and the apparatus needed to provide service, such as fire apparatus and aid
vehicles.

The purpose of the Clark County Fire District 6 Capital Facilities Plan is to use sound fiscal policies to ensure
adequate public fire facilities are planned and provided for as necessitated by growth over a 20-year
period.

Components of the CFP
In accordance with RCW 36.70A.070, the Districts Capital Facilities Plan contains the following elements:

        A. Fire Protection Services and Inventory: This section provides a narrative description of the
           District operations and the current inventory of stations and apparatus.

        B. Capital Projects: This section lists the capital improvements that will eliminate existing
           deficiencies, make available adequate facilities for future growth, and repair and replace
           obsolete or worn-out facilities.

        C. Forecast of Future Needs: This section identifies the level of service standards and analyzes the
           need for capital improvements to achieve levels of service.

        D. Financing Plan: This section lists the funding sources that will pay for needed capital
           improvements. It also addresses the requirement that they land use element, and capital
           facilities plan element are coordinated and consistent.

        E. Periodic Review and Update: This section identifies methods to be used to assure that the
           accuracy and functionality of the capital facilities plan is periodically evaluated, updated, and
           maintained.

        Current Funding
        Currently the District funds all operations primarily through property tax revenues. The 2023 levy
        rates are as follows.

        General Levy of $1.15 per $1,000 of assessed value and an Emergency Services Levy of $0.43 per
        $1,000 of assessed valuation.

        Other sources of income include protection contracts with other governmental agencies,
        investment revenue, sale of surplus items, leasehold tax revenues, payment in lieu of taxes,

        The District can increase the per thousand rate operating levy to $1.50. The District may fund
        through excess levies. These measures take a vote of the taxpayers. The District may also use both
        voter and non-voter approved bond funding.

        Growth’s Share of Projected Capital Needs Proportionate Share
        First, the "proportionate share" test requires that impact fees can only be charged for the portion of
        the cost of public facilities that is "reasonably related" to new development. In other words, impact



13
     fees cannot be charged to pay for the cost of reducing or eliminating deficiencies in existing
     facilities. However, impact fees may be imposed to pay for system improvement costs previously
     incurred by the district to the extent that new growth and development will be served by those
     improvements. (RCW 82.02.060(8)).

     The analysis contained in Section C demonstrates that the new facilities financed in part by an
     impact fee supported by this plan will not remedy any existing deficiencies, but rather will serve to
     meet the demands created by new development.

     Second, there are several important implications of the proportionate share requirement that are
     not specifically addressed in the law, but which follow from the law:
        • The costs of new facilities that will be used by new development and existing users must be
            apportioned between the two groups in determining the amount of the fee.

        •   Impact fees that recover the costs of existing unused capacity should be based on the actual
            cost, rather than the replacement cost of the facility. Carrying costs may be added to reflect
            the actual or imputed interest expense.

     All the facilities described as future needs in this plan will be used by both existing and new
     development. The analysis contained within this facilities plan accounts for this distribution of
     benefit through an examination of the existing development within the District in contrast to the
     expected new development that will benefit from the facilities paid for in part by an impact fee
     supported by this plan.

     The third aspect of the proportionate share requirement is its relationship to the requirement to
     provide adjustments and credits to impact fees, where appropriate. These requirements ensure that
     the amount of the impact fee does not exceed the proportionate share.

        •   The "adjustments" requirement reduces the impact fee to account for past and future
            payments of other revenues (if such payments are earmarked for or pro-ratable to particular
            system improvements).

        •   The "credit" requirement reduces impact fees by the value of dedicated land, improvements
            or construction provided by the developer (if such facilities are in the adopted CFP and are
            required as a condition of development approval). The law does not prohibit a local
            government from establishing reasonable constraints on determining credits. For example,
            the location of dedicated land and the quality and design of a donated public facility can be
            required to conform to adopted local standards for such facilities.

     With such adjustments and credits, new development which is well situated to provide system
     improvements will not overpay the impact fees applicable to it. However, the District currently
     does not have any past or future revenues that are earmarked for, dedicated to or pro-ratable to
     particular fire facility system improvements other than the revenue instruments identified in this



14
     section of this plan. Further, any impact fee ordinance adopted by the Fire District 6 Board of
     Commissioners to implement this capital plan pursuant to RCW 82.02.060 will include the provision
     for "credits" requirement thereby reducing impact fees by the value of dedicated land,
     improvements or construction provided by the developer.

     Reasonably Related to Need
     There are many ways to fulfill the requirement that impact fees shall only be imposed for system
     improvements that are reasonably related to new development, including use by the occupants,
     owners or users of the new development (direct benefit), use by persons or organizations who
     provide goods or services to, or receive the same from the new development (indirect benefit), and
     geographical proximity (presumed benefit). These measures of relatedness are implemented by the
     following techniques:

        •   Impact fees for fire protection are charged to new developments which needs (i.e., benefits
            from) new fire protection facilities. Fire protection is provided to residences, businesses and
            other non-residential land uses. Impact fees for fire protection facilities are charged to all
            new development because the stream of benefits redounds to the occupants and owners of
            all residential and non-residential property.

        •   The relative needs of different types of growth are considered in establishing fee amounts.

        •   Fee-payers can pay a smaller fee if they can demonstrate that their use will have less impact
            than is presumed in the calculation of the impact fee schedule for their classification of
            property. Such reduced needs must be permanent and enforceable (i.e., through land use
            restrictions).

     Another method of connecting a development to the need for public facilities is to establish impact
     fee service areas (zones). All impact fees paid by new development in the service area would be
     required to be spent on new public facilities in the same service area.

     The geographical relationship between a fee-paying development and the impact on a public capital
     facility will differ according to the type of public facility. Roads, for example, may have discernable
     geographical service areas, based on average trip length. There is only a weak geographical linkage
     to fire protection facilities because of the use of back-up response, i.e., facilities not funded by
     impact fees imposed on new development in the District, by fire apparatus for a) multi-alarm fires,
     and b) when apparatus from the nearest station is engaged in a response. Therefore, the use of a
     single service area for fire protection facility needs in the District is reasonable.

     Reasonably Related to Expenditures
     Two provisions of the law tend to reinforce the requirement that expenditures be "reasonably
     related" to new development which is subject to the impact fee. First, the requirement that fee
     revenue must be earmarked for specific uses related to public facilities ensures that expenditures
     are for identifiable projects, the benefit of which can be demonstrated. Second, impact fee revenue



15
        must be expended within 10 years or otherwise encumbered, thus requiring timeliness of the
        benefit to the fee-payer.

        The combination of the timeliness ensured by an appropriate expenditure plan as well as the
        analysis demonstrating proportional benefit contained within the plan assure expenditures of any
        impact fee adopted pursuant to RCW 82.02.050 to implement this plan will be for system
        improvements that reasonably benefit new development.

        Per RCW 82.02.070, Impact fees shall be expended or encumbered for a permissible use within ten
        years of receipt, unless there exists an extraordinary and compelling reason for fees to be held
        longer than ten years. Such extraordinary or compelling reasons shall be identified in written
        findings by the governing body.

FIRE CAPITAL FACILITIES PLAN
Introduction
This document is the Capital Facilities Plan (CFP) of Clark County Fire District 10. It contains all the
elements required by Washington law for capital facilities plans that comply with Washington’s
Growth Management Act. This document is created with the ability to be adopted by reference into
the 20 year Comprehensive Growth Management plans of the Clark County, Washington.
Purpose
Capital facilities are the facilities needed to support growth. For a fire district, these facilities
include fire stations, training facilities, and the major apparatus needed to provide service, such as
fire engines and aid vehicles.
The purpose of the Fire District Capital Facilities Plan is to use sound fiscal policies to ensure
adequate public fire facilities are planned and provided for as necessitated by growth over a 20
year period.
Components of the CFP
In accordance with RCW 36.70A.070, Fire District 10’s capital facilities plan contains the following
elements:
A. Fire Protection Services and Inventory: This section provides a narrative description of the
fire district operations and the current inventory of stations and apparatus.
B. Capital Projects: This section lists the capital improvements that will eliminate existing
deficiencies, make available adequate facilities for future growth, and repair or replace obsolete or
worn out facilities.
C. Forecast of Future Needs: This section identifies the level of service standards and analyzes the
need for capital improvements to achieve levels of service.
D. Financing Plan: This section lists the funding sources that will pay for needed capital
improvements. It also addresses the requirement that the land use element and capital facilities
plan element are coordinated and consistent.
E. Periodic Review and Update: This section identifies methods to be used to assure that the
accuracy and functionality of the capital facilities plan is periodically evaluated, updated, and
maintained.

component that represents the quantity of new demand in both new population and employment

Growth’s Share of Projected Capital Needs
Proportionate Share
First, the "proportionate share" test requires that impact fees can only be charged for the portion of
the cost of public facilities that is "reasonably related" to new development. In other words, impact
fees cannot be charged to pay for the cost of reducing or eliminating deficiencies in existing
facilities. However, impact fees may be imposed to pay for system improvement costs previously
incurred by the district to the extent that new growth and development will be served by those
improvements. (RCW 82.02.060(8)).
The analysis contained in Section C demonstrates that the new facilities financed in part by an
impact fee supported by this plan will not remedy any existing deficiencies, but rather will serve to
meet the demands created by new development.
Second, there are several important implications of the proportionate share requirement that are
not specifically addressed in the law, but which follow from the law:



                                                                               Adopted September 5th, 2024   12
   •   Costs of new facilities that will be used by new development and existing users must be
       apportioned between the two groups in determining the amount of the fee.

   •   Impact fees that recover the costs of existing unused capacity should be based on the actual
       cost, rather than the replacement cost of the facility. Carrying costs may be added to reflect
       the actual or imputed interest expense.
All of the facilities described as future needs in this plan will be used by both existing and new
development. The analysis contained within this facilities plan accounts for this distribution of
benefit through an examination of the existing development within the district in contrast to the
expected new development that will benefit from the facilities paid for in part by an impact fee
supported by this plan.
The third aspect of the proportionate share requirement is its relationship to the requirement to
provide adjustments and credits to impact fees, where appropriate. These requirements ensure
that the amount of the impact fee does not exceed the proportionate share.
   •   The "adjustments" requirement reduces the impact fee to account for past and future
       payments of other revenues (if such payments are earmarked for or pro ratable to
       particular system improvements).

   •   The "credit" requirement reduces impact fees by the value of dedicated land, improvements
       or construction provided by the developer (if such facilities are in the adopted CFP and are
       required as a condition of development approval). The law does not prohibit a local
       government from establishing reasonable constraints on determining credits. For example,
       the location of dedicated land and the quality and design of a donated public facility can be
       required to conform to adopted local standards for such facilities.
With such adjustments and credits, new development which is well situated to provide system
improvements will not overpay the impact fees applicable to it. However, the district currently
does not have any past or future revenues that are earmarked for, dedicated to or pro ratable to
particular fire facility system improvements other than the revenue instruments identified in this
section of this plan. Further, any impact fee ordinance adopted by Fire District 10 to implement
this facilities plan pursuant to RCW 82.02.060 will include the provision for "credits" requirement
thereby reducing impact fees by the value of dedicated land, improvements or construction
provided by the developer.
Reasonably Related to Need.
There are many ways to fulfill the requirement that impact fees shall only be imposed for system
improvements that are reasonably related to new development, including use by the occupants,
owners or users of the new development (direct benefit), use by persons or organizations who
provide goods or services to, or receive the same from the new development (indirect benefit), and
geographical proximity (presumed benefit). These measures of relatedness are implemented by
the following techniques:
   •   Impact fees for fire protection are charged to new developments which needs (i.e., benefits
       from) new fire protection facilities. Fire protection is provided to residences, businesses
       and other non-residential land uses. Impact fees for fire protection facilities are charged to
       all new development because the stream of benefits redounds to the occupants and owners
       of all residential and non-residential property.



                                                                               Adopted September 5th, 2024   13
   •   The relative needs of different types of growth are considered in establishing fee amounts.

   •   Fee-payers can pay a smaller fee if they can demonstrate that their use will have less impact
       than is presumed in the calculation of the impact fee schedule for their classification of
       property. Such reduced needs must be permanent and enforceable (i.e., through land use
       restrictions).
Another method of connecting a development to the need for public facilities is to establish impact
fee service areas (zones). All impact fees paid by new development in the service area would be
required to be spent on new public facilities in the same service area.
The geographical relationship between a fee-paying development and the impact on a public capital
facility will differ according to the type of public facility. Roads, for example, may have discernable
geographical service areas, based on average trip length. There is only a weak geographical linkage
to fire protection facilities because of the use of back-up response, i.e., facilities not funded by
impact fees imposed on new development in the district, by fire apparatus for a) multi-alarm fires,
and b) when apparatus from the nearest station is engaged in a response. Therefore, the use of a
single service area for fire protection facility needs in the district is reasonable. When impact fees
are calculated, growth data is differentiated between incorporated and unincorporated areas to
ensure that both urban and rural geography and land use is represented fairly.
Reasonably Related to Expenditures
Two provisions of the law tend to reinforce the requirement that expenditures be "reasonably
related" to new development which is subject to the impact fee. First, the requirement that fee
revenue must be earmarked for specific uses related to public facilities ensures that expenditures
are for identifiable projects, the benefit of which can be demonstrated. Second, impact fee revenue
must be expended within 10 years or otherwise encumbered, thus requiring timeliness of the
benefit to the fee-payer.
The combination of the timeliness ensured by an appropriate expenditure plan as well as the
analysis demonstrating proportional benefit contained within the plan assure expenditures of any
impact fee adopted pursuant to RCW 82.02.050 to implement this plan will be for system
improvements that reasonably benefit new development.
Per RCW 82.02.070, Impact fees shall be expended or encumbered for a permissible use within ten
years of receipt, unless there exists an extraordinary and compelling reason for fees to be held
longer than ten years. Such extraordinary or compelling reasons shall be identified in written
findings by the governing body.

E. Periodic Review and Update
To assure the accuracy and functionality of the capital plan and impact fee program, periodic
updates of the policies and capital costs contained in the plan should be completed. There are two
methods by which this may occur.


Annual Adjustments

The costs of capital needed to adequately fight and suppress fires in the district rise very year,
frequently in excess of the rate of inflation. The capital facilities identified in this plan will regularly
increase in cost. The district wishes to regularly keep pace with these cost increases to avoid the
need for extreme adjustments in the capital costs and the impact fee rate at the periodic plan
update interval. Based upon this, the district as a policy chooses to adjust the capital cost figures
identified in this plan on an annual basis based upon the appropriate economic indicators as well as
review and, if necessary, update the capital costs at regular plan review intervals.

Periodic Update

In addition to the above annual adjustments, the Fire Capital Facilities Plan should be updated
periodically in conjunction with other comprehensive plans and other capital elements. This will
assure not only accuracy in the capital components and associated costs identified in the plan, but
also a mechanism to reassess the land use element if probable funding falls short of meeting

existing needs and to ensure that the land use element, capital facilities plan element, and financing
plan within the capital facilities plan element are coordinated and consistent.

BACKGROUND
Chapter 36.70A of the Revised Code of Washington (RCW) authorizes public entities such as
fire districts to develop capital facilities plans and impact fee programs for new development to
offset the impacts of growth on capital facilities. These plans and programs must be reviewed

Introduction
This document is the Capital Facilities Plan (CFP) of Clark-Cowlitz Fire Rescue (CCFR). It contains
all the elements required by Washington law for capital facilities plans that comply with
Washington’s Growth Management Act. This document is created with the ability to be adopted by
reference into the 20-year Comprehensive Growth Management plans of the City of Ridgefield, City
of La Center, City of Woodland and Clark County, Washington.
Purpose
Capital facilities are the facilities needed to support growth. For a fire district, these facilities
include fire stations, training facilities, and the major apparatus needed to provide service, such as
fire engines, ladder trucks, and aid vehicles.
The purpose of the Clark-Cowlitz Fire Rescue Capital Facilities Plan is to use sound fiscal policies to
ensure adequate public fire facilities are planned and provided for as necessitated by growth over a
20-year period.
Components of the CFP
In accordance with RCW 36.70A.070, CCFR’s capital facilities plan contains the following elements:
A. Fire Protection Services and Inventory: this section provides a narrative description of the fire
district operations and the current inventory of stations and apparatus.
B. Capital Projects: this section lists the capital improvements that will eliminate existing
deficiencies, make available adequate facilities for future growth, and repair or replace obsolete or
worn-out facilities.
C. Forecast of Future Needs: this section identifies the level of service standards and analyzes the
need for capital improvements to achieve levels of service.
D. Financing Plan: this section lists the funding sources that will pay for needed capital
improvements. It also addresses the requirement that the land use element and capital facilities
plan element are coordinated and consistent.
E. Periodic Review and Update: this section identifies methods to be used to assure that the
accuracy and functionality of the capital facilities plan is periodically evaluated, updated, and
maintained.

Clark-Cowlitz Fire Rescue serves an approximate 125 square mile area including the cities of La
Center, Ridgefield, Woodland, the Cowlitz Indian Reservation, and the unincorporated communities

New Growth

Two aspects of growth contribute to the total capital facility demand; an estimate of a growth
component that represents the quantity of new demand in both new population and employment
base over the planning period, and a growth share of the project capital needs, which is usually
expressed as a percentage of total need. Total capital need increases with the increase in the
volume of fire related responses resulting from an increase in the number of new residential
dwellings and new non-residential development within the district.

Establishment of future capital demand must be based upon an estimate of the total new demand
from new residential and non-residential growth that will occur during the planning period. This
estimate in large part determines what the final amount of capital costs per unit of measure may be
covered by an impact fee. Growth covered by an impact fee is broken into two basic parts:
residential (including single- and multi-family) and non-residential (including commercial,
industrial, business park and institutional).

Call Volume, Type and Distribution

Another variable affecting the attribution of capital costs to new development is the actual benefit
derived by new development based upon call volumes and emergency response types. The benefit
of fire capital facilities, because of their broad emergency response purpose, is a more complex
measurement than other capital facilities, such as streets, which generally serve a single purpose
and benefit all new development equally.

Further, there is no definition or specific direction given in state statute as to what constitutes
“reasonable benefit” to new development of investment in fire facilities. Therefore, it is necessary to
provide a definition of reasonable benefit in CCFR based upon historic call volume data, existing
and future land uses, projected new development over the balance of the planning period, and the
projected increase in different call types resulting for new development over the planning period.

The District has the ability to increase the per thousand rate operating levy to $1.50. The District

also use bond funding. Part of this funding is allowed without a vote of the taxpayers and part
requires a vote of the taxpayers.
Growth’s Share of Projected Capital Needs Proportionate Share
First, the "proportionate share" test requires that impact fees can only be charged for the portion of
the cost of public facilities that is "reasonably related" to new development. In other words, impact
fees cannot be charged to pay for the cost of reducing or eliminating deficiencies in existing
facilities. However, impact fees may be imposed to pay for system improvement costs previously
incurred by the district to the extent that new growth and development will be served by those
improvements. (RCW 82.02.060(8)).
The analysis in Section C shows that the new facilities financed in part by an impact fee supported
by this plan will not remedy existing deficiencies but rather will serve to meet the demands created
by new development.
Second, there are several important implications of the proportionate share requirement that are
not specifically addressed in the law, but which follow from the law:
   •   The costs of new facilities that will be used by new development and existing users must be
       apportioned between the two groups in determining the amount of the fee.

   •   Impact fees that recover the costs of existing unused capacity should be based on the actual
       cost, rather than the replacement cost of the facility. Carrying costs may be added to reflect
       the actual or imputed interest expense.
All the facilities described as future needs in this plan will be used by both existing and new
development. The analysis contained within this facilities plan accounts for this distribution of
benefit through an examination of the existing development within the district in contrast to the
expected new development that will benefit from the facilities paid for in part by an impact fee
supported by this plan.




                                                                                                         13
The third aspect of the proportionate share requirement is its relationship to the requirement to
provide adjustments and credits to impact fees, where appropriate. These requirements ensure
that the amount of the impact fee does not exceed the proportionate share.
   •   The "adjustments" requirement reduces the impact fee to account for past and future
       payments of other revenues (if such payments are earmarked for or pro ratable to
       particular system improvements).

   •   The "credit" requirement reduces impact fees by the value of dedicated land, improvements
       or construction provided by the developer (if such facilities are in the adopted CFP and are
       required as a condition of development approval). The law does not prohibit a local
       government from establishing reasonable constraints on determining credits. For example,
       the location of dedicated land and the quality and design of a donated public facility can be
       required to conform to adopted local standards for such facilities.
With such adjustments and credits, new development which is well situated to provide system
improvements will not overpay the impact fees applicable to it. However, the district currently does
not have any past or future revenues that are earmarked for, dedicated to or pro ratable to
particular fire facility system improvements other than the revenue instruments identified in this
section of this plan. Further, any impact fee ordinance adopted by CCFR to implement this plan
pursuant to RCW 82.02.060 will include the provision for "credits" requirement thereby reducing
impact fees by the value of dedicated land, improvements or construction provided by the
developer.


Reasonably Related to Need.
There are many ways to fulfill the requirement that impact fees shall only be imposed for system
improvements that are reasonably related to new development, including use by the occupants,
owners or users of the new development (direct benefit), use by persons or organizations who
provide goods or services to, or receive the same from the new development (indirect benefit), and
geographical proximity (presumed benefit). These measures of relatedness are implemented by
the following techniques:
   •   Impact fees for fire protection are charged to new developments which need (i.e., benefits
       from) new fire protection facilities. Fire protection is provided to residences, businesses,
       and other non-residential land uses. Impact fees for fire protection facilities are charged to
       all new development because the stream of benefits contributes greatly to the occupants
       and owners of all residential and non-residential property.

   •   The relative needs of different types of growth are considered in establishing fee amounts.

   •   Fee-payers can pay a smaller fee if they can demonstrate that their use will have less impact
       than is presumed in the calculation of the impact fee schedule for their classification of
       property. Such reduced needs must be permanent and enforceable (i.e., through land use
       restrictions).
Another method of connecting a development to the need for public facilities is to establish impact
fee service areas (zones). All impact fees paid by new development in the service area would be
required to be spent on new public facilities in the same service area.




                                                                                                        14
The geographical relationship between a fee-paying development and the impact on a public capital
facility will differ according to the type of public facility. Roads, for example, may have discernable
geographical service areas, based on average trip length. There is only a weak geographical linkage
to fire protection facilities because of the use of back-up response, i.e., facilities not funded by
impact fees imposed on new development in the district, by fire apparatus for a) multi-alarm fires,
and b) when apparatus from the nearest station is engaged in a response. Therefore, the use of a
single service area for fire protection facilities in the district is reasonable.
Reasonably Related to Expenditures
Two provisions of the law tend to reinforce the requirement that expenditures be "reasonably
related" to new development which is subject to the impact fee. First, the requirement that fee
revenue must be earmarked for specific uses related to public facilities ensures that expenditures
are for identifiable projects, the benefit of which can be demonstrated. Second, impact fee revenue
must be expended within 10 years or otherwise encumbered, thus requiring timeliness of the
benefit to the fee-payer.
The combination of the timeliness ensured by an appropriate expenditure plan as well as the
analysis demonstrating proportional benefit contained within the plan assure expenditures of any
impact fee adopted pursuant to RCW 82.02.050 to implement this plan will be for system
improvements that reasonably benefit new development.
Per RCW 82.02.070, Impact fees shall be expended or encumbered for a permissible use within ten
years of receipt, unless there exists an extraordinary and compelling reason for fees to be held
longer than ten years. Such extraordinary or compelling reasons shall be identified in written
findings by the governing body.

E. Periodic Review and Update
To assure the accuracy and functionality of the capital plan and impact fee program, periodic
updates of the policies and capital costs contained in the plan should be completed. There are two
methods by which this may occur.

Annual Adjustments

The costs of capital needed to adequately fight and suppress fires in the district rise every year,
frequently in excess of the rate of inflation. The capital facilities identified in this plan will regularly
increase in cost. The district wishes to regularly keep pace with these cost increases to avoid the
need for extreme adjustments in the capital costs and the impact fee rate at the periodic plan
update interval. Based upon this, the district as a policy chooses to adjust the capital cost figures
identified in this plan on an annual basis based upon the appropriate economic indicators as well as
review and, if necessary, update the capital costs at regular plan review intervals.

In order to ensure costs are consistent with inflation, it is recommended that adopted impact fees
are adjusted annually proportionate to the Consumer Price Index (CPI).


Periodic Update

In addition to the above annual adjustments, the Fire Capital Facilities Plan should be updated
periodically in conjunction with other comprehensive plans and other capital elements. This will
assure not only accuracy in the capital components and associated costs identified in the plan, but
also a mechanism to reassess the land use element if probable funding falls short of meeting
existing needs and to ensure that the land use element, capital facilities plan element, and financing
plan within the capital facilities plan element are coordinated and consistent.

This should occur at least once every five years, and in conjunction with overall comprehensive
plan reviews. Specifically, the following components should be reviewed:

    •   Estimate of the list of capital items identified in the plan and their associated capital costs.
        An update of these costs would reflect the actual price increases from the manufacturer.
        These costs would be adjusted to reflect growth’s share of the need for these items as
        described in section C.

    •   Update the projected number of new dwelling units and new business square footage.

    •   Update the percentage split of fire responses attributable to residential versus
        nonresidential land uses based on actual responses during the time since adoption or last
        update of the plan.
