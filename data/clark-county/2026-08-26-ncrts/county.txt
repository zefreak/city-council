Clark County population and MSW projections
• Clark County is projected to see a large population growth over the next
  several decades, significantly increasing municipal solid waste (MSW)
  generation in the region.




                                                                             3
Our existing transfer system is aging
• West Van and Central transfer stations are more than 30 years old, and
  Washougal, the newest facility, is already 17 years old.
• Aging infrastructure requires ongoing maintenance, upgrades, and
  capital investment.
• Planning for a new North County facility now helps ensure capacity is
  available as the existing system ages and demand grows.

Why do we need a transfer station?
• Residents and haulers in north county currently travel long distances
  to access southern stations. A new facility would cut travel time and
  provide better access for north county residents.
     City/Town          Central TS        West Van TS            Washougal TS

• The siting of a north county transfer station is part of a long-term,
  60+ year strategic plan aligned with Clark County’s Comprehensive
  Solid Waste Management Plan. It ensures solid waste infrastructure
  keeps pace with population and waste generation over decades.

Siting a transfer station
• The siting process does not determine
  who will own or operate the facility.

• Multiple ownership and operating models
  remain available, including:
   o Private ownership and operation (current
     model)
   o Public ownership with contracted
     operation
   o Public ownership with public operation
• Future ownership and operational
  structures would be evaluated separately
  from this siting study.

Recommendations
• Recommend the County Council approve an amendment authorizing
  staff to proceed with Phase 2 of the North County Recycling and Transfer
  Station siting process
   What does Phase 2 involve?
   • Reduce the four preferred sites to two and conduct a more detailed evaluation of
     the remaining sites
   • Conduct an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) for the two remaining sites
   • Expand public outreach and engagement throughout the Phase 2 process

• Recommend the County Council conduct a public hearing on the siting of
  a North County Recycling and Transfer Station
