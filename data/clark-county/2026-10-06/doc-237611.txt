        BACKGROUND
        The criteria for the County Road Administration Board (CRAB) to certify that a County is considered to be in
        reasonable compliance with provisions of law relating to County road administration and with the standards of
        good practice are set forth in Title 136 of the Washington Administrative Code (WAC). Specifically, WAC
        136.50.050 stipulates that a County must establish written policies that cover matters relating to County road
        department administration, maintenance, and construction. Although Clark County Public Works has
        maintained a Survey Monument Preservation policy, the WAC specifically identifies survey monument
        preservation as a policy the County’s legislative authority must adopt by resolution or ordinance. Adopting the
        attached resolution satisfies WAC 136.50.050(5).

        The written policy was developed in coordination between the County Surveyor and County Engineer, is based
        on model policy language provided by CRAB, and aligns with the County’s Memorandum of Understanding
        with the Washington Department of Natural Resources with respect to the preservation of survey
        monuments.

         I. PURPOSE
             A. The purpose of this policy is to ensure that the Department understands its duty to
                protect and preserve survey monuments within the county road right of way when
                performing any type of work, but in particular construction projects and pavement
                preservation projects, where there is a risk that monuments could be disturbed or
                covered.
         II. REFERENCES
             A. The Department of Natural Resources (DNR) has rule making responsibility regarding
                survey monument protection within Washington State.
                  1. RCW 58.24: State Agency for Survey and Maps - Fees
                  2. RCW 58.09: Survey Recording
                  3. WAC 332-120: Survey Monuments – Removal or Destruction
             B. The Board of Registration for Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors has
                regulatory authority over individual professional engineers and land surveyors
                regarding monument protection but lacks authority over public agencies.
                  1. RCW 58.09: Surveys - Recording
                  2. WAC 332-120: Survey Monuments – Removal or Destruction

         III. POLICY STATEMENT
             A. It is the policy of the Clark County Public Works Department (CCPW) to ensure that all
                survey monuments of record within maintenance, construction, and pavement
                preservation treatment project areas are located and perpetuated in compliance with
                Washington State Administrative Code (WAC) 332-120.
         IV. DEPARTMENT RESPONSIBILITIES
             A. Train Public Works staff (Engineering, Survey, Inspection, and Maintenance and
                Operations) on the importance of the law regarding survey monuments and this policy.
             B. Direct private surveyors, engineers, and others to report issues encountered with
                survey monuments within the county road right of way (i.e. buried monument in paved
                surface, damaged case and cover, etc.) by contacting the County Surveyor.
             C. Protect survey monuments during all maintenance activities. The assigned
                maintenance Crew Chief will inspect each worksite for monuments before any asphalt
                disruption or application of surface treatments. If monuments are present within the
                work area, a survey request must be submitted to the Surveyor's Office. Work may
                proceed only after receiving confirmation from the Surveyor's Office that the area has
                been evaluated as required.

             D. Address legacy issues from past Public Works practices once identified, including
                raising monuments, repairing/replacing monument cases and covers, assessing for
                disturbance, and notifying the surveyor of record if possible.
             E. Locate all survey monuments of record during the preliminary design phase of any
                County road project. This includes a physical site inspection. Show found monuments
                on construction plans whenever practical.
             F. Require that contractors notify CCPW whenever monuments not shown on the plans
                are discovered during the course of construction. A survey request will be submitted
                to the Surveyor’s Office at least five days before disturbing any monuments not
                previously located and shown on the plans.
             G. Replace, raise, or uncover all survey monuments of record which become disturbed or
                covered during the course of County road projects. Suitable reference monuments
                may be established instead as allowed for in RCW 58.24.040(8).
             H. Document any change in character of the monuments in the public record according to
                the Survey Recording Act RCW 58.09.
             I. Abide by the terms of the Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between CCPW and
                DNR concerning monuments within the limits of applicable pavement preservation
                treatment projects.
                  1. CCPW will send a notification letter with necessary attachments to DNR each year
                     identifying road names and beginning/ending intersections or mileposts of the
                     planned projects.
                  2. The County Surveyor will sign and seal a letter with necessary attachments
                     certifying that the affected monuments were uncovered as soon as possible after
                     completion.
             J. Follow DNR’s permitting procedures for disturbing, destroying, resetting, and
                completion reporting as outlined in WAC 332-120.
             K. All permitted work within Clark County right-of-way and/or County properties shall be
                required to preserve any/all existing survey monumentation. The preservation of
                existing monumentation shall be a condition of all construction related permits and
                shall be enforced by Public Works Inspection Services.
