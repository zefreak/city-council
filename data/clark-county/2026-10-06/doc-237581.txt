        BACKGROUND

        Background:
        Clark, Cowlitz, Cowlitz Indian Tribe, Skamania, and Wahkiakum are all local or tribal health jurisdictions that
        make up Emergency Preparedness Region 4 in Washington State. Historically these jurisdictions mutually
        support and provide assistance during public health emergencies and disasters. This mutual aid agreement
        formalizes the resources and assets that are available in the region in the event jurisdictions wish to request
        assistance or support.

        Mutual aid partnerships are common amongst partners in emergency management and response agencies to
        promote regional coordination and consistency. By working together under established agreements and
        common response frameworks, local health jurisdictions can leverage assets and resources during
        emergencies when local resources are whelmed, and assistance is needed to save lives. This collaborative
        approach helps ensure that community members across SW Washington and Clark County receive timely and
        equitable public health services regardless of where an emergency occurs.

        Several assets and resources are listed amongst the counties represented in this agreement; staff, Medical
        Reserve Corps. volunteers, alternate facility trailer, morgue trailer, emergency response supplies that are used
        for mass dispensing activities, and behavioral health support. Leveraging these resources across SW
        Washington increases our overall ability to respond effectively during emergencies, it enhances community
        resilience by ensuring that local public health agencies can both receive assistance when local resources are
        strained and provide support to neighboring jurisdictions when able.

        Review by applicable departments/offices:
        The ability to deploy equipment to Clark and EPR Region 4 jurisdictions was discussed with Clark County
        Public Works. Additionally, an interdepartmental between departments exists to support the staging,
        maintenance, and deployment of equipment.

            1. This Public Health Inter-Jurisdictional Mutual Aid Agreement ("Agreement") between County
               or Tribal jurisdictions (Agencies) within Emergency Preparedness and Response Region 4 in the
               State of Washington that operates a public health department or division within its county
               government.

                These Agencies confront numerous threats to public health, including but not limited to, Public
                Health incidents, emergencies, and/or disasters that could overwhelm their response capabilities.
                None of the Agencies to this Agreement possess all the necessary resources to cope with every
                public health incident, emergency or disaster by itself. More efficient and effective responses can
                be achieved by the application and leveraging of collective resources. Each Agency agrees to
                voluntarily aid and assist each other with public health resources and services in the event the
                other Agency requests help. The Agency to this Agreement have determined it is in their
                collective best interest to incorporate mutual aid response into their existing emergency response
                plans. Training and exercising mutual aid protocols, in advance of a public health incident,
                emergency or disaster will enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of each Responding Agency.
                The Agency will cooperate to receive state and/or federal resources provided such cooperation
                does not compromise a Agency’s public health response. The Agency’s desire to engage in
                frequent consultation and to allow free exchange of information, plans, and resource records
                related to assistance activities.
