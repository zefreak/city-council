   4. TIMING CONSIDERATIONS:
      Why now?

      Clark County is currently developing the next Parks & Natural Areas Comprehensive Plan to
      guide investments and priorities for the next 10–20 years. Trails are one of the county’s
      most-used recreational assets, yet the current plan lacks a dedicated chapter that integrates
      regional connectivity, community needs, equity considerations, maintenance planning, and
      emerging recreational trends. Including the Trails Chapter during this planning window
      ensures alignment with countywide growth, the Comprehensive Growth Management Plan,
      multimodal transportation efforts, and partnership opportunities already underway.

   5. DESIRED RESULTS:
      What is being asked of the council?

      Staff is requesting Council’s insight, feedback, input and informal approval on the Draft
      Trails Chapter Outline and the initial Draft Regional Trails Map in the updated Parks &
      Natural Areas Comprehensive Plan. This approval allows staff and consultants to develop
      the chapter’s content, conduct community engagement, draft network recommendations,
      and integrate long-term capital and maintenance strategies.

   6. IMPACTS (COMMUNITY/FINANCIAL/STAFF/OTHER):
      Why should the council hear about this?

      Trails support recreation, public health, transportation options, access to nature, and
      equitable park experiences. A dedicated chapter will improve connectivity between
      neighborhoods, parks, and regional trails. Trails generate strong public interest and
      engagement; a dedicated chapter will improve transparency and long-term stewardship.

      Trails require coordinated capital investment and predictable long-term maintenance
      funding. A Trails Chapter will clarify capital priorities, identify partnership funding, and

   7. POLICY IMPLICATIONS:
      Are there implications for county or department policy; if so, what?

      A Trails Chapter may guide future policy development related to trail classifications, design
      standards, maintenance practices, equity-driven investment, interagency partnerships, land
      acquisition strategy, zoning considerations, and connectivity with multimodal transportation
      plans. It could provide the basis for policy consistency across departments and regional
      collaborators.

   8. ISSUES TO BE CONSIDERED:
      What should the council be aware of? Is there some disagreement or controversy?
      What are the options?

      Council should be aware of existing gaps in trail connectivity, rising community demand, and
      the lack of a formal trail prioritization framework. Potential differing views may arise around
      funding allocations, rural vs. urban investment balance, or land acquisition needs.

   9. RECOMMENDED ACTION:
      What will be asked of the council at the conclusion of the work session?

      Staff recommend that Council provide insight, feedback, input and informal approval of a
      dedicated Trails Chapter in the Parks & Natural Areas Comprehensive Plan and authorize
      staff to develop its scope, community engagement process, draft content, and
      recommended capital priorities.
