Executive summary
When the County presented the Comprehensive Plan’s transportation funding in July,
the six-year program was shown in balance and the funding gap sat in the last 14 years
of the plan. Less than three months later, Public Works reported that the 2027–2032
TIP is projected to draw down Road Fund reserves even with a 1% levy increase. That
program starts only one year later than the one shown as balanced.

The near-term funding the plan relies on is now in doubt, and the long-term gap, still in
the range of $500 million, remains unresolved. The plan places density along the edges
of the urban growth areas, including along NE 72nd Avenue and SR 503, without a
funded program for the north-south capacity those areas need, a need Public Works
identified in 2014.

The public cannot see how road projects are prioritized. The one set of projects whose
reason for being on the TIP is known, the NE 179th Street projects required to lift the
urban holding, is barely underway nearly seven years later, with the remaining work
scheduled through 2033.

The Council should not adopt the Comprehensive Plan on October 13 while the land
use, transportation, and capital facilities elements remain out of balance.

1. The six-year program was presented as funded but no longer is
The July 15, 2026 Council work session presentation (pp. 11–16) showed revenue and
expenditures for 2026–2031 both at $246.8 million. The same presentation showed
$1,605.8 million in 20-year needs against $1,021.3 million in revenue, a gap of about
$585 million that fell almost entirely after 2031. Later revisions narrowed the gap, but
the gap remains in the range of $500 million.

On October 7, 2026, Public Works told the Council that operating costs are outpacing
recurring revenues, so Road Fund money available for capital shrinks each year. Public
Works reported that even with a 1% levy increase, the TIP is projected to draw on Road
Fund reserves by the end of the six-year period. The presentation listed the
consequences as jeopardized grant leverage, reduced ability to meet capital
commitments, and future discussions of level-of-service reductions.

The October 14, 2026 TIP presentation shows a $260.8 million program. Of its $74.9
million in grants, $38.2 million is projected rather than secured. Among the risks the
presentation lists are a shrinking Road Fund for capital and volatile traffic impact fee
revenue.

The plan’s funding analysis rests on a six-year program that Public Works now says
cannot be sustained without reserves. That raises concern about delivering what is
currently on the TIP, before the larger post-2031 gap is considered. RCW
36.70A.070(6)(a)(iv)(C) requires the plan to address how additional funding will be
raised, or how land use assumptions will be reassessed, to ensure level of service
standards will be met. I have been unable to find a revenue plan in the Comprehensive
Plan that closes the gap.

2. Density is being placed at the edges of the urban growth areas without the
north-south capacity to serve that density
At the February 12, 2014 Council work session, Steve Schulte of Public Works
described modeling of the adopted land use that showed NE 72nd Avenue failing
northbound from NE 119th to NE 219th Street. He said “72nd clearly is a need.” He also
said the County was not adding north-south capacity on SR 503, NE 50th, NE 29th, or
east of SR 503, and that 72nd was the only place new north-south capacity would be
built over 20 years.

The 2027–2032 TIP includes only NE 72nd Avenue from NE 119th to NE 139th Street,
scheduled for 2031–2032. The 2014 modeling showed the failure extending to NE 219th
Street.

NE 72nd Avenue and SR 503 carry traffic between Battle Ground and the Vancouver
urban growth area, and the plan adds density along both routes. NE 179th Street is
another area of significant growth in the plan. SR 503 is a state highway, and its
improvements are WSDOT’s responsibility. That does not relieve the County of planning
for the effect of the density the County places along a state route. I have been unable to
find a funded capacity project for SR 503 in this area.

Ridgefield’s growth adds to the load on County north-south roads. Without a direct
connection to I-5 on the south side of the city, Ridgefield traffic uses County roads,
including NE Delfel Road, to reach the NE 179th Street interchange.
3. The cumulative effect of concurrent development is captured only in part
RTC monitors traffic on the regional system, but counts show today’s conditions. They
do not include trips from development that is approved or in process but not yet built.
Those trips enter the analysis through developer-commissioned traffic studies. Each
study is scoped to a single development and includes other development only to the
extent that development is loaded into that study. With so much development in process
at the same time, the cumulative effect on a corridor can be understated.

NE 72nd Avenue shows the risk. The County produced an early version of PLS
Engineering’s failing-corridors map in response to public records request P056419-
051226 (p. 000180). That version marks segments of NE 72nd Avenue as failing, along
with segments on SR 503. The version submitted to the record of the 174th Street
Subdivision (PLD-2026-00002, Ex. 100) does not show the NE 72nd Avenue segments,
and the developer-commissioned traffic studies I have reviewed do not include NE 72nd
Avenue in their study areas. A corridor failure that does not appear in the studies used
to approve development cannot shape where density goes or which projects are
funded.

4. How road projects are prioritized is unknown
With constrained funds and density planned at the edges of the urban growth areas, the
order in which road projects are built determines whether infrastructure keeps pace with
growth. The October 14 TIP presentation lists the evaluation criteria, with concurrency
worth 10 points. The presentation does not show any project’s score, its rank, or why a
project is scheduled, deferred, or left off the TIP.

The NE 179th Street projects are the only TIP projects whose reason for being there is
known: they were required to lift the urban holding. Nearly seven years after the Council
voted to lift the holding, two left-turn lanes have been added at NE 50th Avenue and the
NE 29th Avenue intersection is under construction through 2027. The October 14 TIP
schedule places the remaining NE 179th Street work in 2028 through 2033, with NE
29th to NE 50th Avenue in 2032–2033, beyond the six-year TIP. If projects tied directly
to lifting the urban holding are moving this slowly, the public needs to see how other
projects are ranked, including the north-south capacity needed for the density this plan
adds.

Conclusion
The six-year program the plan relies on was presented as funded in July and is now
projected to need reserves. The 20-year gap, still in the range of $500 million, remains
unresolved. North-south capacity needs identified in 2014 remain unfunded, while the
plan adds density at the edges of the urban growth areas that depend on those roads.
The cumulative effect of concurrent development is captured only in part, and the public
cannot see how projects are prioritized.

The land use element is not matched by the transportation and capital facilities
elements. The plan adds density without showing that the roads serving that density can
be funded or built, and without reassessing land use as RCW 36.70A.070(6)(a)(iv)(C)
requires when funding falls short. The Council should not adopt the Comprehensive
Plan on October 13 while the elements remain out of balance.

A Dangerous Situation on SE Crown Road - Low Cost Interim Solution with Powerful Impact

Since the middle of May we have been in touch with Clark County Public Works personnel trying to
address an urgent safety issue on SE Crown Road: Pedestrians and cyclists in Washougal living within
easy walking and cycling distance of the 312-acre Lacamas Regional Park and the adjacent Woodburn
Elementary School must navigate a dangerous section of SE Crown Rd which has no shoulder and blind
curves.

the Washougal side of SE Crown Road, many people continue to walk or bike along these stretches of the
road. Both individuals taking this risk and drivers are concerned. This is a known problem that is being
unaddressed at severe risk to our children and adults. Someone is going to get badly injured, or worse.

identified a safe location for an RRFB crosswalk where there is good visibility up and down Crown Road.
One end of the crosswalk would be at the existing sidewalk that connects with all the new
neighborhoods on Washougal’s west side. At the other end of the crosswalk there is plenty of room in
the park for a few hundred yards of new trail connecting with Woodburn School and the trail network in
the park.

URGENT Safety Hazard & Funding Request: RRFB Installation at Crown Road and just
south of W Fir Court (Woodburn Elementary School Zone), with trail or sidewalk connection


Susan, Steve, and David,

We’ve been communicating with you about this urgent safety issue since last spring and
have worked to envision a common-sense, low cost, workable plan. We understand
there is a $5.1 million dollar plan which may begin construction in 2030. We appreciate the
need for this long-term solution; however we are requesting Emergency Funds to
address the safety issue before a tragedy occurs.

An upfront expenditure of under $100,000 now for installation of an RRFB Crosswalk plus
1,200 feet of new trail could deliver much of the safety beneﬁt that the $5.1 million dollar
plan is intended to provide. We support protection of environmentally sensitive areas, but
common sense tells us that a narrow corridor between the Red Tape Trail and Crown Rd
can be developed thoughtfully with minimal impact. Determined creative problem-solving
can protect both the environment and people’s lives.

The Problem:
      Vulnerable Road Users at Risk: This specific stretch serves as a walking and biking
       corridor for students attending Woodburn Elementary School and residents
       accessing Lacamas Regional Park.

      Severe Sight Distance Gaps: The natural curvature of Crown Road near W Fir Court
       creates critical blind spots for oncoming vehicles. Pedestrians and cyclists,
       including elementary school students, are forced onto narrow gravel margins
       or active tra ic lanes with zero bu er. It is more dangerous now with the
       recent repaving.

      Speeding vs. Geometry: High vehicle speeds coupled with blind curves heavily
       multiply the probability of a severe or fatal pedestrian collision.

Requested Countermeasures & Infrastructure:

   1. RRFB Crosswalk Installation: Implementation of a Rectangular Rapid Flashing
      Beacon (RRFB) at the optimal visibility point between W Fir Court and McKever Road
      to give pedestrians and cyclists a highly visible, activated crossing point. (See
      LacamasPathwayProject.org for maps)

   2. Trail/Sidewalk Connection: Construction of a multi-use trail or sidewalk on the
      west side of Crown Rd to permanently isolate pedestrian and bicycle tra ic from
      vehicle lanes.
Applicable Funding Alignments:
      WSDOT: Pedestrian and Bicycle Safety: Enhance safety and mobility for people
       who choose to walk or bike.

      WSDOT: Safe Routes to School (SRTS) Program: This intersection lies directly
       inside the active student arrival/departure zone for Woodburn Elementary, making it
       a premier candidate for SRTS priority allocation.

      Federal: Transportation Alternatives (TA) Program: On- and O -road pedestrian and
       bicycle facilities, recreational trail program projects, and safe routes to school projects.

      Federal: Surface Transportation Block Grant (STBG): Bicycle and pedestrian projects.

      Federal: Land and Water Conservation Fund (LWCF): Develop public recreation trails.
      State: Washington Wildlife Recreation Program (WWRP): Develop non-motorized
       public recreation pedestrian or bicycle trails that provide connections to neighborhoods,
       communities, or regional trails.

      Clark County Pedestrian Crossing Prioritization Program (PCPP): The overlapping
       metrics of high vehicle speeds, sharp curvature, lack of shoulder infrastructure, and
       presence of elementary school children warrant an immediate escalation on the PCPP
       priority matrix.

My name is Alex Maier, and I am a resident living near the SE Crown Road corridor at

First, we want to express our sincere thanks to Clark County Public Works for the
recent paving and signage improvements along SE Crown Road. Upgrading the
driving surface and adding clear warnings has undoubtedly increased safety, visibility,
and vehicle handling for motorists driving through these challenging curves.

However, because these improvements focused entirely on vehicles, they left a
critical gap unaddressed: the safety of the kids, parents, and community members
walking and biking alongside the road.

SE Crown Road functions as a primary route for young families and children traveling
to Woodburn Elementary School and accessing Lacamas Regional Park. With no
shoulders or sidewalks, pedestrians are forced onto active traffic lanes. While the
newly paved road is smoother for cars, it unfortunately encourages higher vehicle
speeds, making the lack of pedestrian infrastructure even more perilous for children
walking to school. Relying solely on a major $5.1 million capital project slated for
2030 leaves our students unprotected for years to come.

We don't need to wait for a tragedy to happen. As demonstrated by the community-
backed Lacamas Pathway Project https://lacamaspathwayproject.org/, supported by
over 240 local petitioners, there is a common-sense, low-cost interim solution that can
complement the county's recent work:

   1. An Expedited Safety Crosswalk: Install an RRFB (Rectangular Rapid
      Flashing Beacon) crossing at a high-visibility point south of W. Fir Court.
   2. A Park-Side Trail Connector: Build a short, protected 1,200-foot trail on the
      park (west) side of Crown Road, utilizing public land to bypass expensive
      retaining walls and private property impacts.
With a modest investment of roughly $100,000, we can build on the county's recent
safety momentum for drivers and extend that same protection to the children and
families walking to school and the park.

Please amend the 2027-2032 capital plans to include interim emergency safety
funding for this student and park pathway. Let’s protect our kids now, before someone
gets seriously hurt or killed.

Thank you for your time, dedication, and leadership.

Dear Members of the Clark County Planning Commission,

I am writing to submit formal written testimony regarding items CPZ-2026-
00002 (2027–2032 Transportation Improvement Program) and CPZ-2026-
00004 (2027–2032 Parks and Nature Capital Improvement Plan). I urge the
Planning Commission to support the inclusion and prioritization of non-
motorized pathway projects—specifically the Lacamas Pathway
Project (lacamaspathwayproject.org)—within these 2027–2032 capital
improvement cycles.

As a resident of the Northside neighborhood in Washougal and an avid
cyclist, one of the greatest benefits of living here is our proximity to
Lacamas Regional Park and Woodburn Elementary School. I regularly rely
on quick bike rides into the park to exercise and enjoy nature without having
to drive my car. However, getting to and from both the park and Woodburn
Elementary safely on foot or by bike remains a significant daily challenge
due to infrastructure gaps.
To reach the park from my neighborhood, my route takes me through
Lookout Ridge, Coda Ridge, and Sweet Briar Estates, down West Fir Court,
and onto Crown Road. While navigating our local neighborhood streets is
simple and safe enough, connecting onto Crown Road poses a serious
safety hazard:

     Inadequate Shoulder Width: Crown Road has a very narrow
     shoulder, forcing people on foot or bikes into dangerous proximity with
     passing vehicles.

     Dangerous Sightlines and Crossing Points: To enter the park, I
     must ride up Crown Road from West Fir Court, stop at a small turn-off
     near Woodburn Elementary, and wait for a break in traffic from both
     directions before crossing.

     Return-Trip Risks: On the return trip, crossing back over Crown Road
     to re-enter West Fir Court requires constantly watching for oncoming
     cars from both directions while managing fast-moving traffic.

This lack of safe infrastructure affects more than just recreational cyclists
like myself—it directly impacts local children and families trying to safely
reach Woodburn Elementary School. Without dedicated pathways or safe
crossings along Crown Road, parents are left with no practical choice but to
drive, adding unnecessary traffic around the school and park.

The Lacamas Pathway Project provides a long-overdue solution. Building
safe, non-motorized infrastructure to connect our Washougal neighborhoods
to Lacamas Park and Woodburn Elementary will protect pedestrians,
runners, and cyclists, encourage healthy habits, reduce vehicular
congestion, and fulfill the core safety and connectivity goals of both the
Transportation Improvement Program and the Parks and Nature Capital
Improvement Plan.

I strongly urge the Planning Commission to recommend approval of the
2027–2032 TIP (CPZ-2026-00002) and Parks CIP (CPZ-2026-00004) with
explicit priority given to funding, planning, and constructing safe multi-modal
connections along Crown Road and adjacent corridors. Because these
safety hazards pose a daily risk to students, pedestrians, and cyclists,
I urge the Commission to prioritize this project for implementation as
promptly as possible early in the 2027–2032 funding cycle.

Thank you for your time, consideration, and leadership in planning a safer,
more connected Clark County.
