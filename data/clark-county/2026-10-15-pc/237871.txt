PROPOSED ACTION
The purpose of this staff report is to present changes to CCC Chapter 40.240.170, the county’s
Columbia River Gorge National Scenic Area Ordinance for Disaster Recovery. The proposed plan
amendments address replacement of structures destroyed by disaster. The proposed
amendments clarify several provisions that update the difference between the replacement
structure and the original structure that would still qualify for the simpler review, which allows
placement of a recreational vehicle and temporary structure on a disaster-affected parcel for
temporary use.



BACKGROUND
The Columbia River Gorge National Scenic Area (NSA) was created in 1986. The NSA includes
parts of three Washington counties (Clark, Skamania, and Klickitat) and three Oregon counties
(Multnomah, Hood River, and Wasco). The bi-state Columbia River Gorge Commission was
created to manage the non-federal land in the NSA, while the U.S. Forest Service manages
federal land in the NSA. Clark County has approximately 7800 acres located inside the
boundaries of the National Scenic Area.
The Gorge Commission adopted the first version of the Management Plan in October 1991.
The U.S. Secretary of Agriculture concurred with the Management Plan in February 1992.

Congress directed the Gorge Commission to review the Management Plan no sooner than 5
years, but at least every 10 years, to determine whether it should be revised. The Gorge
Commission adopted the first Revisions to the Management Plan in May 2004. The Secretary of
Agriculture concurred with the revisions in August 2004.
In 2016, the Gorge Commission and Forest Service began work on a second revision to the
Management Plan and adopted the revisions in October 2020. Upon adoption of the
Management Plan, the Gorge Commission submitted the plan to the Secretary for review. The
Forest Service provided the Management Plan for the Special Management Areas (SMAs),
which must be adopted by the Gorge Commission. The Secretary of Agriculture concurred with
the revisions on October 13, 2020.] By law, the Columbia River Gorge Commission and the
Secretary of the Department of Agriculture through the U.S. Forest Service have to determine
that the proposed new code changes are substantially compliant with the Management Plan for
the Scenic Area.
In the July 2025 Gorge Commission meeting, Gorge staff informed the Gorge Commission that
it would research whether the Management Plan adequately provides for Gorge communities
and landowners to recover and rebuild after a disaster. This was in response to the devastating
Rowena fire in June 2025, in a large area of National Scenic Area land in Wasco County. The
Gorge Commission decided to initiate a plan amendment. Unfortunately, a few days after their
July meeting, the Burdoin fire erupted and raced through a large area of National Scenic Area
land in Klickitat County. In both fires, dozens of landowners and residents lost their homes and
other structures, and more.

Staff also researched recovery and rebuilding in Oregon, Washington, and other states and
communities that have suffered recent disasters, and talked to Gorge landowners, Gorge
counties, state agencies, and others about what changes the National Scenic Area Management
Plan may need to help with recovery from these and other disasters. Principally, staff heard and
learned that streamlining permitting was critically important.

APPLICABLE CRITERIA, EVALUATION AND FINDINGS
The proposed provisions were linked to existing provisions in the Gorge Commission’s
Management Plan, either directly by reference, or indirectly by using the same terms and
standards. Thus, some proposed provisions allow differences to a replacement structure that
are comparable to new development or limited like new development. A bullet-point summary
of the proposed amendment changes and clarifications are listed below:

Process Clarifications and Changes for Replacement of Structures Destroyed by Disaster
CCC40.240.170(C)

    •   Clarifies that a replacement structure that is in the same location and that does not
        exceed the size, footprint, and height (with some variations as specified) of the original
        structure is reviewed using expedited review procedures.

Standards Clarifications and Changes for Replacement of Structures Destroyed by Disaster
CCC40.240.170. (C)

    •   Clarifies that a replacement structure that is in the same location and does not exceed
        the size, footprint, and height of the original structure is reviewed only for color,
        reflectivity, and landscaping standards, and no other land use designation or resource
        protection standards.

    •   Clarifies the standards that constitute the same location, size, footprint, and height of
        the original structure, and what variations are permitted.

    •   Specifies that a replacement structure that varies from the identical location and size of
        the original structure and is within 500 feet of a known cultural resource or within 100
        feet of an area that has a high probability of containing cultural resources requires
        cultural resource review prior to using the expedited review process.

    •   Removes the provision allowing an engineering opinion to specify a new location for a
        replacement building (the current Management Plan already requires a full review if the
        location changes, so this provision is repetitive).

Temporary Structures and Land Uses Useful for Recovery and Rebuilding CCC40.240.170 (D)

    •   Allows a landowner to place a recreational vehicle on the parcel where a dwelling was
        destroyed or damaged and is uninhabitable as temporary housing before they build a
        permanent replacement home.

    •   Allows a landowner to place a temporary structure for storage of salvaged items,
        construction materials, and land management equipment and supplies on the parcel
        where a non-residential structure was destroyed before they build a permanent
        replacement structure.

    •   Allows campgrounds to permit a landowner displaced by a disaster to occupy a
        recreational vehicle in the campground before they build a permanent replacement
        home.

    •   Removes the term “Damaged” from the phrase “Damaged or Destroyed by Disaster.”
        The term “Damaged” suggests that the replacement provisions apply to a damaged

        without review.

Staff reviewed information from the Gorge Commission’s Management Plan and determined
that the disaster recovery amendments would streamline the process if a home or structure
was destroyed due to natural causes.
